2. Reading a graph from left to right

What you will be able to do

  • Trace a graph left to right.
  • Write where it rises, falls, or is flat as an open interval of x-values.
  • Separate where a hill is from how high it is.

Always read left to right

We read a graph the way we read a sentence: from left to right. Put your finger on the left end of the graph and trace it toward the right. Three things can happen as you move.

  • Your finger goes up. The graph is rising. The lecture calls this increasing.
  • Your finger goes down. The graph is falling. The lecture calls this decreasing.
  • Your finger stays level. The graph is flat. The lecture calls this constant.

Describe "where" with x-values only

On Graph A the first piece rises. Where does it rise? The answer is a stretch of the x-axis: from x = -5 x equals negative 5 to x = -2 x equals negative 2 . We write that stretch as the interval (-5, -2) open parenthesis negative 5, negative 2 close parenthesis . The heights of the graph are not part of the answer at all.

This is the most common mix-up in the section, and it is easy to see why. The first piece ends at the point (-2, 4) open parenthesis negative 2, 4 close parenthesis , and a point is also written with parentheses. But an interval and a point are different things. The point (-2, 4) open parenthesis negative 2, 4 close parenthesis is one location: over 2 to the left, up 4. The interval (-5, -2) open parenthesis negative 5, negative 2 close parenthesis is a stretch of x-values: everything between -5 negative 5 and -2 negative 2 .

A picture that helps. Think of walking a trail and telling a friend where the uphill part is. You would say "from mile 2 to mile 5." You describe it by position along the trail, not by how high you climbed. The x-axis is the trail. The y-axis is only the altitude.

The lecture uses open intervals for these answers. That means parentheses on both ends, never brackets. At the exact turning point the graph is not going up or down, so the end values are left out.

  2. x runs from -2 to 1 x runs from negative 2 to 1 Read only the x-values of the two ends.
  3. (-2, 1) open parenthesis negative 2, 1 close parenthesis Write the interval with the smaller x first and with parentheses.

Answer: (-2, 1) open parenthesis negative 2, 1 close parenthesis

Hills and valleys

A hill is a point that is higher than the graph on both sides of it. A valley is a point that is lower than the graph on both sides. The lecture calls the top of a hill a relative maximum and the bottom of a valley a relative minimum. Every hill or valley is a point, so it has two numbers, and the lecture asks for them separately:

  • Where it happens. This is the x-value.
  • How high or low it is. This is the y-value.

On Graph B the hill is at the point (-2, 4) open parenthesis negative 2, 4 close parenthesis . It happens at x = -2 x equals negative 2 , and its height is 4. Notice that a graph goes up before a hill and down after it. Going down and then up makes a valley.

3. Conditions: which numbers fit

What you will be able to do

  • Test a number against a condition.
  • Decide which of two conditions owns the boundary number.
  • Match open and closed circles to the symbols.

Reading an inequality as a test

A statement like x < 3 x is less than 3 is a condition. It is a test that a number passes or fails. To test a number, put it in place of x and ask whether the statement is true.

  • x < 3 x is less than 3 means "x is less than 3." The number 2 passes. The number 3 fails, because 3 is not less than itself.
  • x ≤ 3 x is less than or equal to 3 means "x is less than or equal to 3." Now 3 passes.
  • x > 3 x is greater than 3 means "x is greater than 3." The number 3 fails.
  • x ≥ 3 x is greater than or equal to 3 means "x is greater than or equal to 3." The number 3 passes.

The small bar under the symbol is the whole difference. With the bar, the boundary number is included. Without the bar, it is not. You can type ≤ as <= and ≥ as >=.

Two conditions that split the number line

The lecture works with functions that use one rule for some inputs and another rule for the rest. The inputs are sorted by a pair of conditions, such as x < 4 x is less than 4 and x ≥ 4 x is greater than or equal to 4 . Together these two cover the whole number line with no overlap. Every number passes exactly one of them. The only number that takes care is the boundary, 4. Look for the bar: x ≥ 4 x is greater than or equal to 4 has it, so 4 belongs to the second condition.

A picture that helps. Think of ticket prices: "under 12 pays the child price, 12 and over pays the adult price." Everyone fits exactly one group. A person who is exactly 12 has to look at the wording to see which group includes 12. The conditions on a function work the same way, and the bar under the symbol is the wording.

Pick the rule, then use it

A function uses 2x + 1 2 x plus 1 when x < 4 x is less than 4 and uses x - 6 x minus 6 when x ≥ 4 x is greater than or equal to 4 . Find the output for the input 4.

  1. Is 4 < 4? Is 4 is less than 4 ? No. 4 is not less than itself. The first rule does not apply.
  2. Is 4 ≥ 4? Is 4 is greater than or equal to 4 ? Yes, because of the "or equal to." Use the second rule.
  3. (4) - 6 = -2 open parenthesis 4 close parenthesis minus 6 equals negative 2 Substitute 4 into x - 6 x minus 6 only.

Answer: -2 negative 2 . The first rule is never touched.

Open and closed circles

On a graph, a boundary that is included is drawn as a filled (closed) circle. A boundary that is not included is drawn as an open circle. So x ≤ 0 x is less than or equal to 0 ends in a closed circle at 0, and x > 0 x is greater than 0 starts with an open circle at 0.

The graph of a single number

Sometimes one of the rules is only a number, such as -2 negative 2 . That means the output is -2 negative 2 no matter what the input is. Every point has height -2 negative 2 , so the graph is a flat horizontal line through -2 negative 2 on the y-axis.

4. Squaring (x + h) open parenthesis x plus h close parenthesis and distributing

What you will be able to do

  • Distribute a number into (x + h) open parenthesis x plus h close parenthesis .
  • Expand (x + h)^2 open parenthesis x plus h close parenthesis squared with its middle term.
  • Replace x with x + h x plus h in a short formula.

What the lecture is about to ask

The difference quotient starts by replacing x with the group x + h x plus h . That creates expressions like (x + h)^2 open parenthesis x plus h close parenthesis squared and 3(x + h) 3 open parenthesis x plus h close parenthesis . The letter h is only another variable. It behaves exactly like any other letter. This section is about expanding those two kinds of expressions correctly.

Distributing a number into (x + h) open parenthesis x plus h close parenthesis

A number in front of parentheses multiplies every term inside. This is the distributive property.

3(x + h) = 3x + 3h 3 open parenthesis x plus h close parenthesis equals 3 x plus 3 h -5(x + h) = -5x - 5h negative 5 open parenthesis x plus h close parenthesis equals negative 5 x minus 5 h

In the second line the -5 negative 5 multiplies both terms, so both come out negative.

Squaring a group means multiplying it by itself

(x + 3)^2 open parenthesis x plus 3 close parenthesis squared means (x + 3)(x + 3) open parenthesis x plus 3 close parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 3 close parenthesis . To multiply two groups, every term in the first multiplies every term in the second. That is four small multiplications.

(x + 3)(x + 3)= x * x + x * 3 + 3 * x + 3 * 3 equals x times x plus x times 3 plus 3 times x plus 3 times 3 = x^2 + 3x + 3x + 9 equals x squared plus 3 x plus 3 x plus 9 = x^2 + 6x + 9 equals x squared plus 6 x plus 9

The two middle products are always the same, so they combine. That is where the middle term comes from. The tempting shortcut is to square each piece and write x^2 + 9 x squared plus 9 . That drops the middle term, and it is the most common error in this whole topic.

Why there is a middle term. Check with numbers. Let x = 2 x equals 2 . Then (2 + 3)^2 = 5^2 = 25 open parenthesis 2 plus 3 close parenthesis squared equals 5 squared equals 25 . The shortcut gives 2^2 + 3^2 = 4 + 9 = 13 2 squared plus 3 squared equals 4 plus 9 equals 13 , which is wrong. The full expansion gives 4 + 12 + 9 = 25 4 plus 12 plus 9 equals 25 , which is right. You can also picture a square with side x + 3 x plus 3 . Cut it into four parts: an x by x square, a 3 by 3 square, and two x by 3 rectangles. The two rectangles are the middle term.

5. Subtracting an expression and combining like terms

What you will be able to do

  • Change every sign when subtracting a group.
  • Tell xh, h^2 h squared , x, and h terms apart.
  • Recognize that every term without h should cancel.

A minus sign in front of parentheses

The second step of the difference quotient subtracts one whole expression from another. When a minus sign stands in front of parentheses, it applies to every term inside. Each term changes to its opposite. A plus becomes a minus and a minus becomes a plus.

-(x^2 + 3x) = -x^2 - 3x negative open parenthesis x squared plus 3 x close parenthesis equals negative x squared minus 3 x -(x^2 - 3x) = -x^2 + 3x negative open parenthesis x squared minus 3 x close parenthesis equals negative x squared plus 3 x

The lecture calls this "distributing the negative." You can think of the minus sign as a -1 negative 1 being multiplied through.

Why every sign changes. Suppose you have 10 dollars and you pay back a debt of (4 + 3) open parenthesis 4 plus 3 close parenthesis . You subtract the 4 and you also subtract the 3: 10 - 4 - 3 = 3 10 minus 4 minus 3 equals 3 . If you only subtracted the 4 and then added the 3 you would get 9, which is too much. Subtracting a group means subtracting every part of it. And subtracting a negative part, such as -(-3x) negative open parenthesis negative 3 x close parenthesis , gives the part back as +3x.

Like terms

Like terms have exactly the same letters with exactly the same powers. Only like terms can be combined. With two letters in play there are more kinds of terms to keep apart:

  • x^2 x squared terms
  • xh terms (x times h)
  • h^2 h squared terms
  • x terms
  • h terms
  • plain numbers

So 2xh and h^2 h squared are not like terms. Neither are 3x and 3h. When two like terms are opposites, such as x^2 x squared and -x^2 negative x squared , they add to zero and disappear. In a difference quotient, everything without an h always disappears at this step. If a term without an h is left over, that is a signal to recheck the signs.

6. Dividing every term by h

What you will be able to do

  • Split a fraction term by term.
  • Remove one factor of h from each term.
  • Know that h/h is 1 and h^2/h is h.

What a fraction bar does

The last step of the difference quotient divides by h. A fraction bar divides the entire top by the bottom. When the top has several terms added or subtracted, each term must be divided.

(6 + 9)/3 = 6/3 + 9/3 = 2 + 3 = 5 open parenthesis 6 plus 9 close parenthesis over 3 equals 6 over 3 plus 9 over 3 equals 2 plus 3 equals 5

Check: 6 + 9 = 15 6 plus 9 equals 15 and 15/3 = 5 15 over 3 equals 5 . Same answer. So splitting a fraction term by term is safe.

Dividing a term by h

Dividing by h removes one factor of h from the term. A letter divided by itself is 1, as long as it is not zero. The lecture writes "h ≠ 0 h is not equal to 0 " beside the difference quotient for exactly this reason.

6h/h = 6 6 h over h equals 6 2xh/h = 2x 2 xh over h equals 2 x h^2/h = (h * h)/h = h h squared over h equals open parenthesis h times h close parenthesis over h equals h h/h = 1 h over h equals 1

Two of these deserve a second look. h^2/h is h, not 1 and not h^2 h squared : there were two factors of h and one was removed. And h/h is 1, not 0. A term that is exactly h turns into 1 after dividing. It does not vanish.

Why a factor of h comes off. Dividing undoes multiplying. 6h means 6 times h. Dividing by h undoes the "times h" and leaves 6. Test with h = 2 h equals 2 : 6h is 12, and 12/2 = 6 12 over 2 equals 6 . For h^2/h, test with h = 5 h equals 5 : 25/5 = 5 25 over 5 equals 5 , which is h.

What cannot be cancelled

You may only remove h when it is a factor of every term on top. In (2x + h 2 x plus h )/h the first term has no h, so nothing cancels. The fraction stays as it is. This will not happen in a correct difference quotient, because the step before leaves an h in every term. If you reach this step and a term has no h, go back and check the subtraction.
