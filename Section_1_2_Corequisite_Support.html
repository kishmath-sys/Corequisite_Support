2. Distributing and combining like terms

What you will be able to do

  • Identify terms, coefficients, and like terms.
  • Combine like terms.
  • Distribute a positive or negative number.

Terms

An expression such as 5x - 3 + 2x 5 x minus 3 plus 2 x is made of terms. Terms are the pieces separated by plus and minus signs, and each term owns the sign in front of it. Here the terms are 5x, -3 negative 3 , and 2x. The number multiplying a variable is called its coefficient: in 5x the coefficient is 5. A plain x has a coefficient of 1, and -x negative x has a coefficient of -1 negative 1 .

Like terms

Like terms have exactly the same variable part. 5x and 2x are like terms. 5x and 3 are not. You can combine like terms by adding their coefficients, the same way 5 apples and 2 apples make 7 apples. You cannot combine 5 apples and 3 oranges into one pile, and you cannot combine 5x and 3 into one term.

5x + 2x = 7x 5 x plus 2 x equals 7 x 9x - 4x - x = 4x 9 x minus 4 x minus x equals 4 x 6x + 5 - 2x - 8 = 4x - 3 6 x plus 5 minus 2 x minus 8 equals 4 x minus 3

In the last line, the x-terms were combined (6x - 2x = 4x 6 x minus 2 x equals 4 x ) and the plain numbers were combined (5 - 8 = -3 5 minus 8 equals negative 3 ). The plain numbers are called constants.

The distributive property

Parentheses with a number in front, such as 3(x + 4) 3 open parenthesis x plus 4 close parenthesis , mean "3 times everything inside." To remove the parentheses, multiply the outside number by each term inside. This is called distributing.

Why this works. 3(x + 4) 3 open parenthesis x plus 4 close parenthesis means three copies of (x + 4) open parenthesis x plus 4 close parenthesis : (x + 4) + (x + 4) + (x + 4) open parenthesis x plus 4 close parenthesis plus open parenthesis x plus 4 close parenthesis plus open parenthesis x plus 4 close parenthesis . Count what you have: three x's and three 4's. That is 3x + 12 3 x plus 12 . You can also picture a rectangle that is 3 wide and x + 4 x plus 4 long. Its area is 3 times x plus 3 times 4.

Distribute a positive number

Remove the parentheses: 3(x + 4) 3 open parenthesis x plus 4 close parenthesis

  1. 3 * x + 3 * 4 3 times x plus 3 times 4 Multiply 3 by each term inside.
  2. 3x + 12Simplify each product.

Answer: 3x + 12 3 x plus 12

Distributing a negative

When the number in front is negative, its sign goes along to every term. A negative times a positive is negative, and a negative times a negative is positive. So every sign inside the parentheses flips.

A minus sign alone in front of parentheses, as in -(x - 6) negative open parenthesis x minus 6 close parenthesis , is a hidden -1 negative 1 . Distribute the -1 negative 1 and each sign flips: -x + 6 negative x plus 6 .

  2. -6x + 10 negative 6 x plus 10 Negative times positive is negative. Negative times negative is positive.

Answer: -6x + 10 negative 6 x plus 10

3. Keeping an equation balanced

What you will be able to do

  • Explain why the same operation must be done to both sides.
  • Collect the variable on one side.
  • Solve multi-step linear equations.

What an equation is

An equation says that two expressions have the same value. A solution is a number that makes the statement true when you put it in for x. For 2x + 1 = 9 2 x plus 1 equals 9 , the solution is 4, because 2(4) + 1 = 9 2 open parenthesis 4 close parenthesis plus 1 equals 9 .

Picture a scale with the two sides of the equation in the two pans. It is balanced. If you do the same thing to both pans, it stays balanced. That is the one rule of solving: whatever you do to one side, do to the other.

Undoing operations

The goal is to get x alone. To do that, undo whatever has been done to x, using the opposite operation. Addition and subtraction undo each other. Multiplication and division undo each other. Undo additions and subtractions first, then multiplications and divisions.

Practice 10. Solve: x + 9 = 4 x plus 9 equals 4

Hint 1

Show answer

-5 negative 5

When x is on both sides

If x appears on both sides, collect the x-terms on one side first. You do that the same way as anything else: add or subtract the same x-term on both sides. It does not matter which side you choose. Many people move the smaller x-term so the coefficient stays positive.

Variable on both sides

Solve 6x + 5 = 2x - 9 6 x plus 5 equals 2 x minus 9 .

  1. 4x + 5 = -9 4 x plus 5 equals negative 9 Subtract 2x from both sides. Now x is only on the left.
  2. 4x = -14 4 x equals negative 14 Subtract 5 from both sides.
  3. x = -14/4 = -7/2 x equals negative 14 over 4 equals negative 7 over 2 Divide both sides by 4, then reduce the fraction by dividing top and bottom by 2.

Answer: x = -7/2 x equals negative 7 over 2

Why this works. Subtracting 2x from both sides is legal for the same reason subtracting 5 is: 2x stands for some number, and removing the same amount from both pans keeps the scale level. On the right side, 2x - 2x 2 x minus 2 x is zero, so the x-term disappears from that side.

The usual order of steps

  1. Remove parentheses by distributing.
  2. Combine like terms on each side.
  3. Collect the x-terms on one side and the constants on the other.
  4. Divide by the coefficient of x.

Parentheses first

Solve 7x - 2(x + 3) = 9 7 x minus 2 open parenthesis x plus 3 close parenthesis equals 9 .

  1. 7x - 2x - 6 = 9 7 x minus 2 x minus 6 equals 9 Distribute the -2 negative 2 to both terms.
  2. 5x - 6 = 9 5 x minus 6 equals 9 Combine like terms: 7x - 2x = 5x 7 x minus 2 x equals 5 x .
  3. 5x = 15 5 x equals 15 Add 6 to both sides.
  4. x = 3 x equals 3 Divide both sides by 5.

4. Fractions and clearing denominators

What you will be able to do

  • Find the LCD of two or three denominators.
  • Add and subtract fractions.
  • Multiply a fraction by a whole number.
  • Explain why multiplying every term by the LCD removes the fractions.

Why fractions need a common denominator

The bottom number of a fraction, the denominator, tells you the size of the pieces. The top number, the numerator, counts how many pieces you have. You can only add pieces of the same size. Halves and thirds are different sizes, so 1/2 + 1/3 1 over 2 plus 1 over 3 cannot be added until both are rewritten in pieces of the same size.

Finding the least common denominator (LCD)

The LCD is the smallest number that every denominator divides into evenly. One reliable way to find it: list the multiples of the largest denominator until you reach one that the others divide into.

LCD of 2 and 3: multiples of 3 are 3, 6. 2 divides 6. LCD = 6 LCD of 2 and 3 : multiples of 3 are 3, 6 . 2 divides 6 . LCD equals 6 LCD of 4 and 10: multiples of 10 are 10, 20. 4 divides 20. LCD = 20 LCD of 4 and 10 : multiples of 10 are 10, 20 . 4 divides 20 . LCD equals 20

Adding and subtracting fractions

To rewrite a fraction with a new denominator, multiply the top and the bottom by the same number. That does not change its value, because you are multiplying by 1.

Add fractions

Add 2/3 + 1/4.

  1. LCD = 12 LCD equals 12 Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12. And 3 divides 12.
  2. 2/3 = 8/12 and 1/4 = 3/12 2 over 3 equals 8 over 12 and 1 over 4 equals 3 over 12 Multiply top and bottom of 2/3 by 4. Multiply top and bottom of 1/4 by 3.
  3. 8/12 + 3/12 = 11/12 8 over 12 plus 3 over 12 equals 11 over 12 Same size pieces now, so add the tops. The bottom stays 12.

Answer: 11/12

Multiplying a fraction by a whole number

To multiply a whole number by a fraction, multiply the whole number by the top, then divide by the bottom. It is often easier to divide first: in 12 times 5/6, divide 12 by 6 to get 2, then multiply 2 by 5 to get 10.

12 * (5/6) = (12/6) * 5 = 2 * 5 = 10 12 times open parenthesis 5 over 6 close parenthesis equals open parenthesis 12 over 6 close parenthesis times 5 equals 2 times 5 equals 10 6 * (x/3) = (6/3) * x = 2x 6 times open parenthesis x over 3 close parenthesis equals open parenthesis 6 over 3 close parenthesis times x equals 2 x

Clearing fractions from an equation

Here is the idea the lecture relies on. In an equation you are allowed to multiply both sides by any number except zero. If you multiply both sides by the LCD, every denominator divides into it evenly, and all the fractions disappear at once. The only rule: every term on both sides gets multiplied, including terms that are not fractions.

Clear the fractions

Solve x/3 + 1/4 = 2 1 over 4 equals 2 .

  1. LCD = 12 LCD equals 12 The denominators are 3 and 4.
  2. 12(x/3) + 12(1/4) = 12(2) 12 open parenthesis x over 3 close parenthesis plus 12 open parenthesis 1 over 4 close parenthesis equals 12 open parenthesis 2 close parenthesis Multiply every term by 12, including the 2.
  3. 4x + 3 = 24 4 x plus 3 equals 24 12/3 = 4 12 over 3 equals 4 , 12/4 = 3 12 over 4 equals 3 , and 12 times 2 = 24 2 equals 24 . No fractions are left.
  4. 4x = 21 4 x equals 21 Subtract 3 from both sides.
  5. x = 21/4 x equals 21 over 4 Divide by 4. The fraction cannot be reduced, so this is the answer.

Answer: x = 21/4 x equals 21 over 4

Finish this one: Clear the fractions

The first steps are done for you. You finish it.

  1. LCD = 10 LCD equals 10 The denominators are 5 and 2.
  2. 2x + 5 = 30 2 x plus 5 equals 30 Every term was multiplied by 10: 10(x/5) = 2x 10 open parenthesis x over 5 close parenthesis equals 2 x , 10(1/2) = 5 10 open parenthesis 1 over 2 close parenthesis equals 5 , 10(3) = 30 10 open parenthesis 3 close parenthesis equals 30 .

5. When the variable disappears

What you will be able to do

  • Tell a true statement from a false one.
  • Say what it means when the x-terms cancel.
  • Tell x = 0 x equals 0 apart from 0 = 0 0 equals 0 .

True statements and false statements

Some statements about numbers are simply true, like 6 = 6 6 equals 6 . Some are simply false, like 2 = 5 2 equals 5 . No value of x can change either one, because there is no x in them.

Usually solving ends with something like x = 3 x equals 3 . But sometimes, when you subtract the x-terms from both sides, the x-terms cancel completely and you are left with a statement that has no x in it. What that statement says tells you about the original equation.

What the last line tells you
Last line looks likeWhat it meansName
x = a x equals a numberexactly one solutionconditional equation
a true statement, like 12 = 12 12 equals 12 every real number is a solutionidentity
a false statement, like 2 = 5 2 equals 5 no number is a solutioninconsistent equation
Why this works. Every step of solving keeps the equation equivalent to the one before it. So if the last line is always true, the first line was always true, whatever x is. If the last line is never true, the first line was never true, and no x can fix it.

The x-terms cancel and leave a true statement

Simplify 3(x + 4) = 3x + 12 3 open parenthesis x plus 4 close parenthesis equals 3 x plus 12 .

  1. 3x + 12 = 3x + 12 3 x plus 12 equals 3 x plus 12 Distribute on the left. Both sides are now identical.
  2. 12 = 12 12 equals 12 Subtract 3x from both sides. The x-terms cancel.

12 = 12 12 equals 12 is true, so every real number is a solution.

The x-terms cancel and leave a false statement

Simplify 2(x + 1) = 2x + 5 2 open parenthesis x plus 1 close parenthesis equals 2 x plus 5 .

  1. 2x + 2 = 2x + 5 2 x plus 2 equals 2 x plus 5 Distribute on the left.
  2. 2 = 5 2 equals 5 Subtract 2x from both sides. The x-terms cancel.

2 = 5 2 equals 5 is false, so there is no solution.

Do not confuse these. Ending with x = 0 x equals 0 is a normal single solution: the number zero. Ending with 0 = 0 0 equals 0 is a true statement with no x, so every real number works. They look similar and mean very different things.

6. Checking answers and using Desmos

What you will be able to do

  • Check a solution by substituting into the original equation.
  • Type a fraction with a grouped numerator.
  • Read a solution from a point of intersection.

Checking by substitution

A solution is a number that makes the equation true. So you can always test an answer: replace every x in the original equation with your number, simplify each side separately, and see whether the two sides match. If they do, the answer is right. If they do not, something went wrong along the way.

Checking is not extra work for its own sake. It is the one tool that lets you know you are right before you submit.

Check a proposed solution

Is x = 7 x equals 7 a solution of 2(x - 1) + 3 = 3(x - 2) 2 open parenthesis x minus 1 close parenthesis plus 3 equals 3 open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis ?

  1. left side: 2(7 - 1) + 3 = 2(6) + 3 = 15 left side : 2 open parenthesis 7 minus 1 close parenthesis plus 3 equals 2 open parenthesis 6 close parenthesis plus 3 equals 15 Replace x with 7 and simplify. Parentheses first.
  2. right side: 3(7 - 2) = 3(5) = 15 right side : 3 open parenthesis 7 minus 2 close parenthesis equals 3 open parenthesis 5 close parenthesis equals 15 Do the same on the right.
  3. 15 = 15 15 equals 15 Both sides match.

Yes, x = 7 x equals 7 is a solution.

Practice 34. Is x = 2 x equals 2 a solution of 4x - 3 = x + 5 4 x minus 3 equals x plus 5 ?
Hint 1

Work out each side separately with x = 2 x equals 2 .

Show answer

No. Left side: 4(2) - 3 = 5 4 open parenthesis 2 close parenthesis minus 3 equals 5 . Right side: 2 + 5 = 7 2 plus 5 equals 7 . Since 5 does not equal 7, x = 2 x equals 2 is not a solution.

Typing fractions so the computer reads them correctly

On paper, a fraction bar groups everything above it and everything below it. A keyboard slash does not. If you type 2x + 5/4 2 x plus 5 over 4 , the computer divides only the 5 by 4. To keep a whole numerator together, wrap it in parentheses: (2x + 5 2 x plus 5 )/4.

Practice 35. Which is the correct way to type "2x + 5 2 x plus 5 , all over 4"?
Show answer

(2x + 5 2 x plus 5 )/4. The parentheses keep 2x + 5 2 x plus 5 together as the numerator.

What a Desmos check shows

Desmos can check a solution with a picture. Type the left side of the equation as one graph (y = left side) and the right side as another (y = right side). Each graph shows the value of that side for every x. Where the two graphs cross, the two sides have the same value, which is exactly what "solution" means. The x-coordinate of the crossing point is the solution. The y-coordinate is the value both sides share there.
