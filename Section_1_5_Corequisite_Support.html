2. Standard form and the plus-or-minus symbol

What you will be able to do

  • Recognize standard form.
  • Read a, b, and c with their signs.
  • Rearrange an equation so one side is 0.

What makes an equation quadratic

A quadratic equation is one where the highest power of x is 2. Every quadratic can be arranged into the same layout, called standard form:

ax^2 + bx + c = 0 ax squared plus bx plus c equals 0

Here a, b, and c stand for plain numbers. a is the number multiplying x^2 x squared , b is the number multiplying x, and c is the number with no x. The right side is exactly 0, and the terms are in order from the highest power down. Every method in Section 1.5 starts from this layout or from the numbers a, b, and c, so reading them correctly matters.

Reading a, b, and c

  • Each number takes the sign in front of it. In 3x^2 - 8x - 5 = 0 3 x squared minus 8 x minus 5 equals 0 , b is -8 negative 8 and c is -5 negative 5 .
  • If no number is written in front, the coefficient is 1. In x^2 + 4x - 7 = 0 x squared plus 4 x minus 7 equals 0 , a is 1.
  • If a term is missing, its coefficient is 0. In 2x^2 - 9 = 0 2 x squared minus 9 equals 0 , b is 0.

Getting to standard form

If the equation is not in standard form, make it so. Remove any parentheses by distributing. Then move every term to one side by adding or subtracting the same thing on both sides, so that the other side is 0. A term changes sign when it crosses the equals sign, because you subtracted (or added) it on both sides.

3. Factoring

What you will be able to do

  • Explain factoring as multiplication run backward.
  • Factor out a GCF.
  • Factor x^2 x squared + bx + c using the product and the sum.
  • Factor a difference of squares.
  • Factor ax^2 + bx + c with the ac method.

What factoring is

Multiplying takes pieces and combines them: 3 times 4 is 12. Factoring goes the other way. It starts with the result and finds the pieces that multiply to make it: 12 = 3 12 equals 3 times 4. The pieces are called factors.

In algebra, factoring rewrites a sum of terms as a product. For example, x^2 + 7x + 10 x squared plus 7 x plus 10 can be rewritten as (x + 2)(x + 5) open parenthesis x plus 2 close parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 5 close parenthesis . Nothing about its value changes. Only its form changes, from "terms added" to "groups multiplied."

The built-in check. You can always check a factoring yourself: multiply your factors back out. If you get the original expression, the factoring is right. You never have to wonder.

Tool 1: the greatest common factor (GCF)

Always look first for something that divides every term. The greatest common factor is the largest number, and the highest power of x, that divides all the terms. Write it in front, and inside the parentheses write what is left of each term after dividing.

Tool 4: trinomials that start with a number times x^2 x squared (the ac method)

When the first coefficient is not 1, as in 2x^2 + 7x + 3 2 x squared plus 7 x plus 3 , the simple "multiply to c, add to b" search has to be adjusted. One dependable method is called the ac method:

  1. Multiply a times c.
  2. Find two numbers that multiply to that product and add to b.
  3. Rewrite the middle term bx as two terms using those numbers.
  4. Factor by grouping: pull a GCF from the first two terms and from the last two terms. The same group should appear twice. Factor that group out.

4. The zero-factor property

What you will be able to do

  • Explain why a product of 0 forces a factor to be 0.
  • Turn a factored equation into two small equations.
  • Explain why the other side must be 0.

What is special about zero

Suppose two numbers multiply to 12. What are they? You cannot tell: 3 and 4, or 2 and 6, or 24 and one half. Knowing the product tells you almost nothing about the factors.

Now suppose two numbers multiply to 0. This time you know something for certain: at least one of them is 0. There is no other way to get a product of 0. That fact is called the zero-factor property (your notes also call it the zero-factor theorem or zero-product principle).

If A * B = 0, then A = 0 or B = 0. If A times B equals 0, then A equals 0 or B equals 0 .

How it is used

If an equation says (one group)(another group) = 0, then one of the groups must equal 0. So you set each group equal to 0 and solve each small equation. One hard equation turns into two easy ones.

Use the zero-factor property

Solve (3x - 4)(x + 6) = 0 open parenthesis 3 x minus 4 close parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 6 close parenthesis equals 0 .

  1. 3x - 4 = 0 or x + 6 = 0 3 x minus 4 equals 0 or x plus 6 equals 0 The product is 0, so one of the groups is 0.
  2. 3x = 4, so x = 4/3 3 x equals 4, so x equals 4 over 3 First equation: add 4, then divide by 3.
  3. x = -6 x equals negative 6 Second equation: subtract 6.

Answer: x = 4/3 x equals 4 over 3 or x = -6 x equals negative 6

Notice the signs. The group (x + 6) open parenthesis x plus 6 close parenthesis gives the solution -6 negative 6 , and a group like (x - 2) open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis would give +2. The solution is the number that makes the group zero, so its sign is the opposite of what you see in the group.

A factor that is just a number times x, such as the 3x in 3x(x - 5) = 0 3 x open parenthesis x minus 5 close parenthesis equals 0 , counts as a group too. Setting 3x = 0 3 x equals 0 gives x = 0 x equals 0 . It is easy to forget that solution.

The other side has to be zero

The property only works for a product equal to 0. If the equation says (x - 2)(x + 3) = 6 open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 3 close parenthesis equals 6 , you cannot set each group equal to 6, because many pairs of numbers multiply to 6. That is why standard form puts 0 on one side before anything else happens.

5. Radicals and the square root property

What you will be able to do

  • Explain why undoing a square gives two answers.
  • Simplify a square root.
  • Take the square root of a fraction.

Two square roots

Squaring 7 gives 49. Squaring -7 negative 7 also gives 49, because a negative times a negative is positive. So the question "what number squared is 49?" has two answers, 7 and -7 negative 7 .

The radical sign by itself means only the positive one: sqrt(49) = 7 the square root of 49 equals 7 . So when you undo a square in an equation, you have to put the second answer back yourself. That is the job of the ± symbol.

If x^2 = 49, then x = ±7. If x squared equals 49, then x equals plus or minus 7 .
Forgetting the ± is the most common slip in this section. Any time you take the square root of both sides of an equation, write ± on the number side.

Simplifying a square root

If the number is a perfect square (1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144), its square root is a whole number. If it is not, simplify: find the largest perfect square that divides it, and bring that square root out front. This uses the rule sqrt(a * b) = sqrt(a) * sqrt(b) the square root of a times b, end root, equals the square root of a times the square root of b .

  2. sqrt(9) * sqrt(7) the square root of 9 times the square root of 7 Split the radical.
  3. 3sqrt(7) 3 the square root of 7 The square root of 9 is 3. The 7 stays inside.

The square root of a fraction is the square root of the top over the square root of the bottom. If the bottom is a perfect square, the radical disappears from the bottom completely.

Piece 3: simplifying the result

After substituting, you have one fraction with two terms on top. Three clean-up steps follow, in this order:

  2. Look for a number that divides every term: both terms on top and the bottom. If there is one, divide all three by it.
  3. If the directions ask, split into two fractions.

A common factor that does not clear the bottom

8. Graphs, intercepts, and Desmos

What you will be able to do

  • Explain why x-intercepts are solutions.
  • Predict the number of real solutions from a graph.
  • Turn a Desmos decimal into an exact answer.

What an x-intercept is

The graph of an equation like y = x^2 - 5x - 14 y equals x squared minus 5 x minus 14 is a U-shaped curve called a parabola. An x-intercept is a point where the graph touches or crosses the x-axis. Every point on the x-axis has y = 0 y equals 0 . So at an x-intercept, the equation becomes 0 = x^2 - 5x - 14 0 equals x squared minus 5 x minus 14 .

That is why solving and graphing are connected: the x-coordinates of the x-intercepts are the solutions of the equation you get by setting y to 0. If the graph crosses at (-2, 0) open parenthesis negative 2, 0 close parenthesis and (7, 0), the solutions are x = -2 x equals negative 2 and x = 7 x equals 7 .

How many solutions to expect

  • The parabola crosses the x-axis twice: two real solutions.
  • It touches the x-axis at one point: one real solution.
  • It never reaches the x-axis: no real solutions. (The solutions involve the number i from Section 1.4.)

Reading intercepts in Desmos

Type the equation into Desmos and click a point where the curve meets the x-axis. Desmos labels it with coordinates. Desmos shows decimals, and MyMathLab usually wants exact answers, so:

  • If the decimal ends or repeats, such as -2.5 negative 2.5 or -2.3333 negative 2.3333 , it is a fraction. Type the decimal into a new Desmos line and use the fraction button to convert it.
  • If the decimal goes on with no pattern, the exact answer has a square root in it. Use Desmos to check your algebra, not to replace it.
