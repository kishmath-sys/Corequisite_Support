1. Start here

What you will be able to do

Section 4.2 introduces logarithms. A logarithm is a way of asking for an exponent. The lecture converts equations between two forms, logarithmic and exponential, and evaluates logarithms without a calculator. This page builds the skills that work depends on: knowing common powers and asking "what exponent gives this number," using negative and fraction exponents, naming the base, the exponent, and the result in an equation, recognizing the hidden bases in log and ln, and solving the small equation that is left at the end.

Before you begin. The word logarithm sounds harder than the idea is. If it makes you uneasy, that usually comes from math anxiety or time away from math, not from a lack of ability. Nothing here is graded and nobody sees your answers. Mistakes only show which idea to look at again. Take breaks whenever you want. Your progress saves on this device.
How to read and enter logarithms. In your notes the base of a logarithm is a small lowered number. On this page it is typed with an underscore and the input goes in parentheses: log_5(25) log base 5 of 25 means "log base 5 of 25." Desmos accepts the same typing. Use a caret for powers and a slash for fractions, like 5^2 5 squared and 1/2.

2. Knowing your powers

What you will be able to do

  • Recall or build common powers.
  • Answer a backward exponent question.
  • Use the powers 1 and 0.

The question behind every logarithm

You already know how to go forward: 2^5 2 to the power 5 means multiply five 2s, which gives 32. A logarithm asks the same fact backward: "2 to what power gives 32?" The answer is 5. To answer backward questions quickly, it helps to know the common powers.

Powers worth knowing
Exponent12345
Base 22481632
Base 3392781243
Base 55251256253125
Base 1010100100010000100000

To answer "3 to what power gives 81?" find 81 in the base 3 row and read the exponent above it: 4.

Two special cases

  • Any base to the power 1 is itself. So "6 to what power gives 6?" has the answer 1.
  • Any base to the power 0 is 1. So "6 to what power gives 1?" has the answer 0.

If you do not know the power

You are not expected to have every power memorized. Raise the base to 2, then 3, then 4 until you land on the number. The lecture does exactly this in Desmos with the caret key. The only thing to avoid is typing the logarithm itself into Desmos when a question says not to use a calculator.

Why the backward question matters. Multiplying and dividing undo each other, and so do squaring and taking a square root. Raising a base to a power needs an undo as well. If 2^5 = 32 2 to the power 5 equals 32 is the forward trip, then "which exponent got me to 32?" is the trip back. The logarithm is the name for that trip back. So it is not a new kind of number. It is an exponent you already know how to find.

A backward question

4 to what power gives 64?

  1. 4^1 = 4 4 to the power 1 equals 4 Start low.
  2. 4^2 = 16 4 squared equals 16 Not yet.
  3. 4^3 = 64 4 cubed equals 64 There it is.

Answer: 3

3. Exponents that are negative or fractions

What you will be able to do

  • Use a negative exponent to reach one over a power.
  • Write square roots and cube roots as fraction exponents.

Reaching a fraction

Sometimes the target is a fraction, such as "5 to what power gives 1/25?" No positive exponent makes a number smaller than the base. A negative exponent does. A negative exponent means "one over."

5^-1 = 1/5 5 to the power negative 1 equals 1 over 5 5^-2 = 1/5^2 = 1/25 5 to the power negative 2 equals 1 over 5 squared equals 1 over 25 10^-3 = 1/1000 10 to the power negative 3 equals 1 over 1000

So 5 to the power -2 negative 2 gives 1/25. Find the power that gives the bottom number, then make it negative.

Reaching a root

Sometimes the target is a root, such as "5 to what power gives sqrt(5) the square root of 5 ?" Roots are fraction exponents.

  • A square root is the 1/2 power: sqrt(5) = 5^(1/2) the square root of 5 equals 5 to the power 1 over 2, .
  • A cube root is the 1/3 power: cbrt(10) = 10^(1/3) 10 to the power 1 over 3, .
  • A fourth root is the 1/4 power, and so on. The root number goes on the bottom of the fraction.

It works the other way too. 9^(1/2) 9 to the power 1 over 2, means the square root of 9, which is 3. And 27^(1/3) 27 to the power 1 over 3, means the cube root of 27, which is 3, because 3 * 3 * 3 = 27 3 times 3 times 3 equals 27 .

Why roots are fraction exponents. When you multiply powers of the same base, the exponents add. So 5^(1/2) * 5^(1/2) = 5^(1/2 + 1/2) = 5^1 = 5 5 to the power 1 over 2, times 5 to the power 1 over 2, equals 5 to the power 1 over 2 plus 1 over 2, equals 5 to the power 1 equals 5 . A number that multiplies by itself to give 5 is exactly what the square root of 5 means. So 5^(1/2) 5 to the power 1 over 2, has to be sqrt(5) the square root of 5 . The negative exponent has a similar check: 5^2 * 5^-2 = 5^0 = 1 5 squared times 5 to the power negative 2 equals 5 to the power 0 equals 1 , so 5^-2 5 to the power negative 2 must be the number that undoes 25, which is 1/25.

A fraction target

2 to what power gives 1/32?

  1. 2^5 = 32 2 to the power 5 equals 32 First find the power that gives the bottom number.
  2. 2^-5 = 1/32 2 to the power negative 5 equals 1 over 32 Make the exponent negative to put it under 1.

Answer: -5 negative 5

4. Base, exponent, and result in both forms

What you will be able to do

  • Name the base, exponent, and result in either form.
  • Say that a logarithm equals an exponent.
  • Move the three numbers into the other form.

Three numbers, three jobs

Every exponential equation has three parts. In 2^5 = 32 2 to the power 5 equals 32 :

  • 2 is the base, the number being multiplied.
  • 5 is the exponent, how many times.
  • 32 is the result.

The same three numbers in logarithmic form

The fact 2^5 = 32 2 to the power 5 equals 32 can be written a second way:

log_2(32) = 5 log base 2 of 32 equals 5

Read it "log base 2 of 32 equals 5." It says: the exponent that takes 2 to 32 is 5. Look at where each number went.

Where each part sits
PartExponential form 2^5 = 32 2 to the power 5 equals 32 Logarithmic form log_2(32) = 5 log base 2 of 32 equals 5
Basethe big number on the bottom, 2the small lowered number after log, 2
Exponentthe raised number, 5alone on the other side of the equals sign, 5
Resultalone on the other side, 32inside the parentheses, 32

Two things never change. The base stays the base in both forms. And the logarithm equals the exponent. If you remember only one sentence from this page, make it that one.

The lecture uses letters

The notes write the definition as y = log_b(x) y equals log base b of x if and only if x = b^y. x equals b to the power y . The letter b is the base, y is the exponent, and x is the result. "If and only if" means the two equations say the same thing, so you may swap one for the other at any time. In the videos the instructor labels b, y, and x in the given equation and then writes them into the other form.

Why the two forms say the same thing. Read log_2(32) log base 2 of 32 as a question: "2 to what power gives 32?" The answer to that question is 5, so log_2(32) = 5. log base 2 of 32 equals 5 . Writing the answer back as a power gives 2^5 = 32 2 to the power 5 equals 32 . Both lines carry the same three numbers and the same fact. One line puts the exponent by itself. The other puts the result by itself.

Label the parts

In log_3(81) = 4 log base 3 of 81 equals 4 , name the base, the exponent, and the result. Then write the exponential form.

  1. base = 3 base equals 3 The lowered number after log.
  2. exponent = 4 exponent equals 4 The log equals the exponent.
  3. result = 81 result equals 81 The number inside the parentheses.
  4. 3^4 = 81 3 to the power 4 equals 81 Base to the exponent equals the result.

Answer: 3^4 = 81 3 to the power 4 equals 81

5. log and ln: the bases you do not see

What you will be able to do

  • Supply base 10 for log.
  • Supply base e for ln.
  • Evaluate simple common and natural logarithms.

Two logarithms get shortcuts

Two bases are used so often that they have short names, and the base is not written.

  • log with no base written is the common logarithm. Its base is 10. So log(1000) log of 1000 means log_10(1000) log base 10 of 1000 .
  • ln is the natural logarithm. Its base is e. So ln(5) the natural log of 5 means log_e(5) log base e of 5 . It is pronounced "natural log" or "L N."

What e is

The letter e stands for one specific number, about 2.718. Like pi, its decimal goes on forever without repeating, so it gets a letter. The notes call it a transcendental number. For this section you only need to know that e is a positive number and that it can be a base like any other.

First step: write the base back in

When a problem has log or ln, write the hidden base before doing anything else. Then it is an ordinary logarithm and the backward question works as before.

log(1000) = log_10(1000): 10 to what power gives 1000? 3 log of 1000 equals log base 10 of 1000 : 10 to what power gives 1000 ? 3 ln(e^2) = log_e(e^2): e to what power gives e^2? 2 the natural log of e squared, end log, equals log base e of e squared, end log, : e to what power gives e squared ? 2

The second line needs no arithmetic with 2.718. The question is "e to what power gives e^2 e squared ," and the exponent is sitting right there.

Why these two bases. Our number system is built on tens: 10, 100, 1000. So base 10 is the natural choice for everyday measuring, and it earned the plain name log. The number e shows up whenever something grows continuously, like the continuously compounded interest in Section 4.1, so base e earned its own name too. They are not different kinds of logarithm. They are the same idea with a base you are expected to remember.

6. The small equation at the end

What you will be able to do

  • Replace a power with its value.
  • Solve a one-step or two-step equation.

What is left after converting

The last classroom example converts a logarithmic equation to exponential form and then solves for x. After the conversion there are no logarithms left. What remains is a short equation with a power on one side.

Step 1: compute the power

Replace the power with its value.

x + 3 = 2^4 x plus 3 equals 2 to the power 4 x + 3 = 16 x plus 3 equals 16

Step 2: undo what is attached to x

To get x alone, do the opposite operation on both sides. If 3 is added, subtract 3. If 5 is subtracted, add 5. If x is multiplied by 2, divide by 2.

x + 3 = 16 x plus 3 equals 16 x = 13 x equals 13

Sometimes two things are attached. Undo the addition or subtraction first, then the multiplication.

2x + 1 = 3^2 2 x plus 1 equals 3 squared 2x + 1 = 9 2 x plus 1 equals 9 2x = 8 2 x equals 8 x = 4 x equals 4
Why the same step goes on both sides. An equation is a balance. Whatever you do to one side you must do to the other, or it tips. Subtracting 3 from both sides of x + 3 = 16 x plus 3 equals 16 keeps it balanced and leaves x by itself. You can always check: put 13 back in. 13 + 3 = 16 13 plus 3 equals 16 , and 2^4 = 16 2 to the power 4 equals 16 . Both sides match.

Power, then solve

Solve x - 5 = 3^2. x minus 5 equals 3 to the power 2 .

  1. x - 5 = 9 x minus 5 equals 9 Compute 3^2. 3 to the power 2 .
  2. x = 14 x equals 14 Add 5 to both sides.

Answer: x = 14 x equals 14
