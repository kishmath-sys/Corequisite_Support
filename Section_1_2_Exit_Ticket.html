3. Types of Equations

These checks match item 3 on pages 3 and 4 and Classroom Examples 1.2.3 and 1.2.4 on page 7.

Check 7 (Conditional Equation). A student solves an equation and ends with x = 4. What type of equation is it?
Show answer

Conditional, because it has one solution.

Where this is in your notes: item 3 (Types of Equations), Conditional Equation, on page 3 of the notes.

Check 8 (Inconsistent Equation). Solve 3(x + 4) = 3x + 103 times the quantity x plus 4 equals 3 x plus 10. What is true about this equation?
Show answer

It is inconsistent, so it has no solution.

Where this is in your notes: item 3, Inconsistent Equation, on page 4 of the notes and the video "Classroom Example 1.2.3."

Check 9 (Identity Equation). Solve 2(x − 3) + x = 3x − 62 times the quantity x minus 3, plus x, equals 3 x minus 6. How would you write the solution in MML?
Show answer

{x | x is a real number}

Where this is in your notes: item 3, Identity Equation, on page 4 of the notes and the video "Classroom Example 1.2.4."

Check 10 (Reading the Last Line). After simplifying, a student is left with 0 = 0. What does that tell them?
Show answer

The equation is an identity, so every real number is a solution.

Where this is in your notes: item 3, Identity Equation, on page 4 of the notes and the video "Classroom Example 1.2.4."

Check 11 (Writing No Solution). How does MML show that an equation has no solution?
