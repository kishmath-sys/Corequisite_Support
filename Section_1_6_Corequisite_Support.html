2. Exponent rules and fractional exponents

What you will be able to do

  • Add exponents when multiplying and multiply them for a power of a power.
  • Read a negative exponent as one over.
  • Read a fraction exponent as a root.
  • Undo an exponent by raising both sides to the flipped power.

What an exponent counts

An exponent counts how many times a number or letter is multiplied by itself. x^3 x cubed means x * x * x x times x times x . The number being multiplied is called the base, and the small raised number is the exponent or power. Every rule below comes from simply counting factors.

Multiplying powers of the same base: add the exponents

x^3 * x^2 = (x * x * x)(x * x) = x^5 x cubed times x squared equals open parenthesis x times x times x close parenthesis open parenthesis x times x close parenthesis equals x to the power 5

Three x's and two more x's make five x's in a row. So x^a * x^b = x^(a + b) x to the power a times x to the power b equals x to the power a plus b, .

A power raised to a power: multiply the exponents

(x^2)^3 = x^2 * x^2 * x^2 = x^6 open parenthesis x squared close parenthesis cubed equals x squared times x squared times x squared equals x to the power 6

Three groups of two x's is six x's. So (x^a)^b = x^(a * b) open parenthesis x to the power a close parenthesis to the power b equals x to the power a times b, . This rule is the one Section 1.6 uses most.

Negative exponents: flip it

Look at a pattern: 2^3 = 8 2 cubed equals 8 , 2^2 = 4 2 squared equals 4 , 2^1 = 2 2 to the power 1 equals 2 . Each step down divides by 2. Keep going: 2^0 = 1 2 to the power 0 equals 1 , then 2^(-1) = 1/2 2 to the power negative 1, equals 1 over 2 , then 2^(-2) 2 to the power negative 2, = 1/4. A negative exponent does not make a number negative. It means "one over" the positive power.

x^(-1) = 1/x x to the power negative 1, equals 1 over x 5^(-1) = 1/5 5 to the power negative 1, equals 1 over 5 3^(-2) = 1/3^2 = 1/9 3 to the power negative 2, equals 1 over 3 squared equals 1 over 9

3. Factoring: GCF, grouping, and difference of squares

What you will be able to do

  • Find a GCF that includes a power of x.
  • Factor a difference of squares.
  • Factor four terms by grouping.
  • Keep factoring until nothing factors again.

What factoring is, and how to check it

Factoring rewrites a sum of terms as a product of pieces. It is multiplication run backward. You can always check a factoring by multiplying the pieces back out.

Section 1.6 uses three factoring tools, often one after another in the same problem.

Tool 1: greatest common factor with powers

The GCF is the largest number that divides every coefficient, together with the lowest power of x that appears in every term. Why the lowest? Because x^2 x squared divides into x^4 x to the power 4 (leaving x^2 x squared ), but x^4 x to the power 4 does not divide into x^2. x to the power 2 .

Tool 3: factoring by grouping

When there are four terms and no GCF for all of them, try grouping. Split the four terms into two pairs. Pull a GCF out of each pair. If the same group in parentheses shows up both times, that group is a common factor and can be pulled out in front.

4. Squaring and checking solutions

What you will be able to do

  • Explain why squaring removes a square root.
  • Isolate a radical before squaring.
  • Square a group without losing the middle term.
  • Explain where extraneous solutions come from and check for them.

A square undoes a square root

The square root of a number is the value whose square is that number. So squaring a square root brings you back to what was inside: (sqrt(9) the square root of 9 )^2 = 3^2 = 9 3 squared equals 9 . That works for expressions too.

(sqrt(x))^2 = x open parenthesis the square root of x close parenthesis squared equals x (sqrt(x + 7))^2 = x + 7 open parenthesis the square root of x plus 7, end root, close parenthesis squared equals x plus 7

Get the radical alone first

Squaring removes a radical only when the radical is by itself on one side. If another term is sitting next to it, squaring that side creates a middle term that still has the radical in it. So the first move is always to isolate the radical by moving the other terms to the opposite side.

Isolate the radical

Isolate the radical in sqrt(x + 7) + 5 = x the square root of x plus 7, end root, plus 5 equals x .

  1. sqrt(x + 7) + 5 - 5 = x - 5 the square root of x plus 7, end root, plus 5 minus 5 equals x minus 5 Subtract 5 from both sides.
  2. sqrt(x + 7) = x - 5 the square root of x plus 7, end root, equals x minus 5 The radical is alone on the left.

Result: sqrt(x + 7) = x - 5 the square root of x plus 7, end root, equals x minus 5

When both sides are squared, the side without the radical is often a group such as x - 5 x minus 5 . Squaring a group means multiplying it by itself, and that always produces a middle term.

Why squaring can create a false solution

Squaring hides signs. The numbers 3 and -3 negative 3 are different, but their squares are both 9. So if you square both sides of an equation, a value that made the two sides opposites will now make them look equal.

Here is the smallest example. The equation x = 3 x equals 3 has one solution. Square both sides and you get x^2 = 9 x squared equals 9 , which has two: 3 and -3 negative 3 . The -3 negative 3 was not a solution of the original. It was created by the squaring. A false solution like that is called extraneous.

This is why, after squaring both sides, every answer must be checked in the original equation. The check is part of the solution, not an optional extra.

Check proposed solutions

  3. x = 4: left side sqrt(2(4) + 1) = sqrt(9) = 3 x equals 4 : left side the square root of 2 open parenthesis 4 close parenthesis plus 1, end root, equals the square root of 9 equals 3 Substitute into the left side.

5. Substitution: quadratic in form

What you will be able to do

  • Explain substitution as a temporary renaming.
  • Test whether an expression is quadratic in form.
  • Rewrite an equation with u.
  • Switch back from u to x.

Renaming a piece

Sometimes an expression looks complicated only because the same chunk appears more than once. Substitution means giving that chunk a temporary one-letter name, usually u, so the expression becomes simpler to look at. When the work is done, you swap the original chunk back in.

It is like calling a long address "home." The place is the same. The name is shorter.

What "quadratic in form" means

A regular quadratic has the shape (something)^2, then that same something, then a number: u^2 u squared , u, constant. An expression is quadratic in form when it has that same shape with a bigger chunk in place of u. The test:

  • There are three terms.
  • The first exponent is exactly double the middle exponent.
  • The last term is a plain number.

The middle chunk is what you call u. The first chunk is then u^2 u squared , because of the power rule:

Switching back

Solving the u-equation gives values of u. But the question asked for x. So the last stage is to replace u with the chunk it stood for and solve one more small equation for each value. Which small equation you get depends on what u was:

  • If u = x^2 u equals x squared and u = 9 u equals 9 : solve x^2 = 9 x squared equals 9 , so x = ±3 x equals plus or minus 3 . Each u-value can give two x-values.
  • If u = x^(1/2) u equals x to the power 1 over 2, and u = 5 u equals 5 : solve x^(1/2) = 5 x to the power 1 over 2, equals 5 by squaring, so x = 25 x equals 25 .
  • If u = x^(-1) u equals x to the power negative 1, and u = 4 u equals 4 : solve 1/x = 4 by flipping, so x = 1/4.
Stopping at u is the most common slip. The values of u are not the answer. Always go back to x. And when the switch-back involves squaring both sides, the answers need to be checked in the original equation.

6. Absolute value

What you will be able to do

  • Explain absolute value as distance from zero.
  • Say how many solutions |x| = a the absolute value of x, end absolute value, equals a has.
  • Split |group| the absolute value of group, end absolute value, = number into two equations.
  • Isolate the absolute value first.

Absolute value is a distance

The absolute value of a number is how far it is from zero on the number line. Distance does not have a direction, so it is never negative. The symbol is a pair of vertical bars.

|6| = 6 the absolute value of 6, end absolute value, equals 6 |-6| = 6 the absolute value of negative 6, end absolute value, equals 6

Both 6 and -6 negative 6 sit six steps from zero. The bars are also grouping symbols, so simplify whatever is inside before taking the absolute value: |2 - 9| = |-7| = 7 the absolute value of 2 minus 9, end absolute value, equals the absolute value of negative 7, end absolute value, equals 7 .

Get the bars alone first

The split only works when the absolute value is by itself on one side. If numbers are added outside the bars or multiplied in front, undo those first, exactly as you would to get x alone. Treat the whole |...| the absolute value of . . ., end absolute value, as a single block while you do.

7. Checking with Desmos

What you will be able to do

  • Read solutions from intersection points.
  • Spot an extraneous solution on a graph.

What the graph shows

Desmos can check any equation in this section the same way. Type the left side as one graph (y = left side) and the right side as another (y = right side). Each graph shows the value of that side for every x. Where the two graphs cross, the two sides are equal. The x-coordinate of each crossing point is a solution.

If one side of the equation is 0, you can graph just the other side and look for x-intercepts instead. Both views give the same answers.

What an extraneous solution looks like

Desmos graphs the original equation, before any squaring. So a false solution created by squaring simply does not appear: there is no crossing point at that x-value. If your algebra produced two answers and the graph shows only one crossing, the missing one is extraneous.

Typing tips

  • Type sqrt and Desmos draws the radical. Everything under the radical must be inside the parentheses.
  • Type abs( or use the | key for absolute value.
  • Type a fraction exponent in parentheses: x^(1/2) x to the power 1 over 2, .
