2. Inequality symbols and the number line

What you will be able to do

  • Read the four symbols and match them to phrases.
  • Rewrite an inequality with x first.
  • Compare negative numbers and fractions.
  • Choose an open or closed endpoint.

Four symbols

An equation says two things are equal. An inequality says one is smaller or larger than the other. There are four symbols.

Inequality symbols

The symbol always opens toward the larger number and points at the smaller one. 3 < 8 3 is less than 8 and 8 > 3 8 is greater than 3 say the same thing.

An inequality has many solutions

The equation x = 4 x equals 4 has one solution. The inequality x < 4 x is less than 4 has endlessly many: 3, 0, -10 negative 10 , 3.99, and every other number to the left of 4. That is why the answer is shown as a shaded stretch of the number line instead of a single number.

Reading it with x first

Sometimes the variable ends up on the right, as in 5 > x 5 is greater than x . That says "5 is greater than x," which is the same as "x is less than 5," or x < 5 x is less than 5 . When you swap the two sides, the symbol must turn around too, so it still points at the same thing. Writing x first makes the graph easier to picture.

Comparing numbers, including negatives

On the number line, the number farther to the left is the smaller one. That is obvious for positives, but it trips people with negatives: -7 negative 7 is less than -2 negative 2 , because -7 negative 7 is farther left, even though 7 is bigger than 2. Think of temperature: -7 negative 7 degrees is colder than -2 negative 2 degrees.

To compare fractions, turn them into decimals or find where they sit between whole numbers. -9/2 negative 9 over 2 is -4.5 negative 4.5 , which is to the left of -4 negative 4 .

Graphing on a number line

To graph x > 2 x is greater than 2 , mark the endpoint 2 and shade everything to its right. The mark at the endpoint shows whether the endpoint itself is a solution:

  • Open circle (or a parenthesis): the endpoint is not included. Used with < and >.
  • Closed circle (or a bracket): the endpoint is included. Used with ≤ and ≥.

3. Solving linear inequalities

What you will be able to do

  • Solve an inequality with the same moves as an equation.
  • Explain why multiplying or dividing by a negative reverses the symbol.
  • Tell when the flip does not apply.

Almost everything is the same as solving an equation

To solve an inequality, get x alone using the same moves you use for equations: distribute, combine like terms, add or subtract the same thing on both sides, multiply or divide both sides by the same number. The answer is an inequality such as x < 5 x is less than 5 instead of a single number.

Adding or subtracting never causes trouble. Start with a true statement, 2 < 5 2 is less than 5 . Add 3 to both sides: 5 < 8 5 is less than 8 . Still true. Subtract 10 from both sides: -8 < -5 negative 8 is less than negative 5 . Still true.

Solve with no surprises

Solve 3x - 4 < 11 3 x minus 4 is less than 11 .

  1. 3x < 15 3 x is less than 15 Add 4 to both sides.
  2. x < 5 x is less than 5 Divide both sides by 3. Dividing by a positive number changes nothing about the symbol.

Answer: x < 5 x is less than 5

The one new rule

Now multiply both sides of 2 < 5 2 is less than 5 by -1 negative 1 . You get -2 negative 2 and -5 negative 5 . But -2 negative 2 is greater than -5 negative 5 , because it is farther right. The statement -2 < -5 negative 2 is less than negative 5 would be false. To keep it true, the symbol has to turn around: -2 > -5 negative 2 is greater than negative 5 .

When you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, reverse the inequality symbol.
Why this works. Multiplying by a negative reflects every number to the other side of zero, like a mirror. Whatever was on the right is now on the left. The order of the two numbers swaps, so the symbol between them must swap too.
What does and does not trigger the flip. Flip only when the number you multiply or divide by is negative. Do not flip because the answer is negative, because there are negatives somewhere in the problem, or because you subtracted. In 3x ≥ -12 3 x is greater than or equal to negative 12 you divide by positive 3, so nothing flips: x ≥ -4 x is greater than or equal to negative 4 .

  2. x ≤ -3 x is less than or equal to negative 3 Divide both sides by -4 negative 4 . Dividing by a negative reverses the symbol.

4. Interval notation

What you will be able to do

  • Write an interval from left end to right end.
  • Choose a bracket or a parenthesis for each end.
  • Use -∞ and ∞ with parentheses.
  • Join two pieces with a union.

A compact way to write a stretch of the number line

A solution such as x ≥ 2 x is greater than or equal to 2 is a whole stretch of numbers. Interval notation describes that stretch by naming its two ends: where it starts on the left and where it stops on the right, separated by a comma.

  • Always write the left end first, then the right end. Smaller number first.
  • A bracket [ or ] means that end is included. It matches ≤, ≥, and a closed circle.
  • A parenthesis ( or ) means that end is not included. It matches <, >, and an open circle.
  • If the stretch goes on forever to the right, the right end is ∞ (infinity). If it goes on forever to the left, the left end is -∞.
  • Infinity always gets a parenthesis. It is not a number you can reach, so it cannot be included.

Write an interval

  2. left end: -∞ left end : negative infinity The shading never stops on the left.
  3. right end: 7, included≤ includes 7, so use a bracket.

Picture the graph first. Most interval mistakes come from skipping the picture. Ask "which way is it shaded?" before writing anything. A "less than" answer starts at -∞. A "greater than" answer ends at ∞.

5. Compound inequalities

What you will be able to do

  • Read a compound inequality as "between."
  • Apply each step to all three parts.
  • Reverse both symbols when dividing by a negative, then reorder.
  • Tell an "and" statement from an "or" statement.

Two conditions at once

A compound inequality such as -2 < x ≤ 6 negative 2 is less than x is less than or equal to 6 packs two statements into one line: x is greater than -2 negative 2 and x is less than or equal to 6. Both must be true. Together they describe every number between -2 negative 2 and 6. Read it from the middle outward: "x is between -2 negative 2 and 6."

For this to make sense, the smaller number has to be on the left and both symbols have to point the same way (both < or ≤).

Practice 23. Which number is a solution of -2 < x ≤ 6 negative 2 is less than x is less than or equal to 6 ?
Show answer

0. 0 is greater than -2 negative 2 and less than 6, so both conditions hold.

Three parts instead of two sides

A compound inequality has a left part, a middle part, and a right part. To get x alone in the middle, use the same moves as always, but do each move to all three parts. The balance idea still applies. There are simply three pans now.

Solve a three-part inequality

Solve -3 < 2x + 5 ≤ 9 negative 3 is less than 2 x plus 5 is less than or equal to 9 .

  1. -8 < 2x ≤ 4 negative 8 is less than 2 x is less than or equal to 4 Subtract 5 from all three parts: -3 - 5 negative 3 minus 5 , 2x + 5 - 5 2 x plus 5 minus 5 , 9 - 5 9 minus 5 .
  2. -4 < x ≤ 2 negative 4 is less than x is less than or equal to 2 Divide all three parts by 2. Positive, so no flip.

Answer: -4 < x ≤ 2 negative 4 is less than x is less than or equal to 2 , which is the interval (-4 negative 4 , 2].

Dividing all three parts by a negative number reverses both symbols. Afterward the larger number is on the left, so rewrite the whole thing from smallest to largest.

"Or" statements

The other kind of compound statement uses or, as in "x < -3 x is less than negative 3 or x > 3 x is greater than 3 ." A number is a solution if it satisfies either part. On a number line that is two separate stretches pointing away from each other, written with a union. An "or" statement cannot be squeezed into one three-part line.

6. Absolute value ideas and clearing fractions

What you will be able to do

  • Describe |x| < a the absolute value of x, end absolute value, is less than a and |x| > a the absolute value of x, end absolute value, is greater than a as distances.
  • Rewrite each without bars.
  • Isolate the absolute value and simplify inside it.
  • Clear a fraction from an inequality.

Absolute value is a distance

|x| the absolute value of x, end absolute value, is the distance between x and zero on the number line. It is never negative. So an absolute value inequality is a statement about distance, and you can picture the answer before doing any algebra.

"Less than" means close to zero

|x| < 3 the absolute value of x, end absolute value, is less than 3 says "the distance from x to zero is less than 3." Which numbers are within 3 steps of zero? Everything between -3 negative 3 and 3. That is one connected stretch, an "and" statement:

|x| < 3 means -3 < x < 3 the absolute value of x, end absolute value, is less than 3 means negative 3 is less than x is less than 3

"Greater than" means far from zero

|x| > 3 the absolute value of x, end absolute value, is greater than 3 says "the distance from x to zero is more than 3." Those numbers are out past 3 on the right, or out past -3 negative 3 on the left. That is two separate stretches, an "or" statement:

|x| > 3 means x < -3 or x > 3 the absolute value of x, end absolute value, is greater than 3 means x is less than negative 3 or x is greater than 3
Why this works. A memory aid some students like: less thand, greator. "Less than" gives an and (between) statement. "Greater than" gives an or (outside) statement. The picture of distance is what makes it true.

Get the bars alone first

The distance picture only applies when the absolute value is alone on one side. If something is added outside the bars or multiplied in front, undo that first. Treat |...| the absolute value of . . ., end absolute value, as one block.

Isolate the absolute value

  2. |x| < 4 the absolute value of x, end absolute value, is less than 4 Divide both sides by 3.

If the expression inside the bars has parentheses or like terms, simplify it before doing anything else. For example, 3(x - 2) + 8 3 open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis plus 8 becomes 3x - 6 + 8 3 x minus 6 plus 8 , which is 3x + 2 3 x plus 2 .

If one whole side is a fraction, multiply both sides by its denominator to clear it. A denominator like 3 or 5 is positive, so the symbol does not flip. Remember to multiply the other side too.

7. Checking with Desmos

What you will be able to do

  • Use the crossing point as the boundary.
  • Read "greater than" as "above" on a graph.

Above and below

To check an inequality with a graph, type the left side as one graph and the right side as another, the same way you would for an equation. The crossing point is where the two sides are equal. It marks the boundary of the solution.

On a graph, a larger value is higher up. So "left side > right side" is true for the x-values where the left-side graph is above the right-side graph, and "left side < right side" is true where it is below. Look to the left and right of the crossing point and see which side of it has the left graph on top.

The answer is a set of x-values, read along the horizontal axis. The boundary is included if the symbol has "or equal to" and not included if it is strict.
