1. Start here

What you will be able to do

  • Count rows and columns and locate an entry.
  • Read every coefficient, including 1, -1 negative 1 , and 0.
  • Rewrite an equation so its terms line up.
  • Turn an equation into a row and a row back into an equation.
  • Multiply a row by a number and add two rows.
  • Enter an augmented matrix in Desmos and read the result.

Section 6.1 solves systems of equations with a matrix. A matrix is a grid of numbers. The lecture writes a system as an augmented matrix, shows two by-hand methods for your reference, and then has Desmos do the work with one button called rref. In this course the solving is done in Desmos. That means your job is the part Desmos cannot do: getting the right numbers into the right places, and reading the answer back out. This page builds exactly that: rows and columns, coefficients (including the ones you cannot see), lining equations up, turning an equation into a row and a row back into an equation, a short look at the arithmetic behind a row operation, and the Desmos steps.

Before you begin. New vocabulary can make a section look harder than it is. If the word "matrix" makes you tense, that usually comes from math anxiety or time away from math, not from a lack of ability. A matrix here is only a tidy way to write down numbers you already see in the equations. Nothing on this page is graded and nobody sees your answers. Take breaks whenever you want. Your progress saves on this device.
How to enter answers. Type a matrix size as rows, the letter x, then columns, such as 2x3. Type a row of a matrix as numbers separated by commas, in order, such as 3, 5, 1. Type a solution as an ordered pair or triple with parentheses. Spaces do not matter.

2. Rows, columns, and where a number sits

What you will be able to do

  • Tell rows from columns.
  • State a size with rows first.
  • Find an entry from its row and column.

What a matrix is

A matrix is a rectangle of numbers inside brackets. Each number is called an entry. Rows go across, left to right. Columns go up and down, like the columns that hold up a building.

P = 6−108−35Q = 27−4109

Size: rows first

The size of a matrix is its number of rows, then its number of columns. Matrix P has 3 rows and 2 columns, so it is a "3 by 2" matrix, written 3 × 2. Matrix Q has 2 rows and 3 columns, so it is 2 × 3. The order matters. A 3 × 2 matrix and a 2 × 3 matrix are different shapes.

Position: row first again

Every entry has an address: its row number, then its column number. In matrix P, the entry in row 2, column 1 is 0. Go down to the second row, then across to the first column. In matrix Q, the entry in row 1, column 3 is -4 negative 4 .

The notes write an address as a small pair of digits after a lowercase letter. The symbol a with a small 21 means "the entry in row 2, column 1". Read the 21 as "two, one", not as twenty-one.

Why rows always come first. A memory aid for rows before columns: you read a line of text across a row before you move down the page. Size and position both follow that habit. A matrix with 3 rows and 4 columns holds 3 * 4 = 12 3 times 4 equals 12 entries, the same way a carton with 3 rows of 4 eggs holds 12 eggs. That count is a quick check that you have typed everything.

Size and position

3. Coefficients, including the ones you cannot see

What you will be able to do

  • Read a coefficient with its sign.
  • Supply the invisible 1 or -1 negative 1 .
  • Supply 0 for a missing variable.

What a coefficient is

A coefficient is the number in front of a variable. In 5x - 2y = 9 5 x minus 2 y equals 9 , the coefficient of x is 5 and the coefficient of y is -2 negative 2 . The number alone on the other side, 9, is the constant. A matrix for a system is built from nothing but these numbers, so every one has to be read correctly.

The sign belongs to the coefficient

A minus sign in front of a term is part of its coefficient. In 5x - 2y 5 x minus 2 y , the y term is -2y negative 2 y , so the coefficient is -2 negative 2 , not 2.

An invisible 1

When no number is written, the coefficient is 1. The term y means 1y. The term -z negative z means -1z negative 1 z , so its coefficient is -1 negative 1 .

An invisible 0

When a variable is missing from an equation, its coefficient is 0. The equation 2x - z = 7 2 x minus z equals 7 has no y, so it is really 2x + 0y - z = 7 2 x plus 0 y minus z equals 7 . In a matrix that 0 has to be typed. Leaving it out would shift every later number into the wrong column.

2x - z = 7 2 x minus z equals 7 2x + 0y - 1z = 7 2 x plus 0 y minus 1 z equals 7 coefficients: 2, 0, -1 constant: 7 coefficients : 2, 0, negative 1 constant : 7
Why the hidden numbers are really there. One y is the same as 1 times y, so writing the 1 changes nothing. No y at all is the same as 0 times y, because 0 times anything is 0 and adds nothing. Writing the hidden 1s and 0s is not changing the equation. It is showing what was already there so each variable has a number to put in its column.

Read every coefficient

List the coefficients of x, y, and z and the constant in x - 5y = -3 x minus 5 y equals negative 3 .

  1. 1x - 5y + 0z = -3 1 x minus 5 y plus 0 z equals negative 3 Write the hidden 1 and the missing z.
  2. x: 1No number written means 1.
  3. y: -5 y : minus 5 The sign is part of it.
  4. z: 0There is no z term.
  5. constant: -3 constant : negative 3 The number on the right.

1, -5 negative 5 , 0 and the constant -3 negative 3 .

4. Lining equations up before reading them

What you will be able to do

  • Move variable terms to the left.
  • Put terms in alphabetical order with their signs.
  • Put the constant on the right.

Every variable needs its own column

In a matrix the first column holds every x coefficient, the second holds every y coefficient, and so on. The last column holds the constants. That only works if each equation is written the same way: variables on the left in alphabetical order, the constant alone on the right. The notes call this general form.

Fixing an equation that is out of form

A variable term on the wrong side. Move it by adding or subtracting it on both sides. Its sign changes when it crosses.

3x = 2y + 5 3 x equals 2 y plus 5 3x - 2y = 5 3 x minus 2 y equals 5

Variables out of order. Addition can be done in any order, so swap the terms. Each term keeps its own sign.

4y + x = 7 4 y plus x equals 7 x + 4y = 7 x plus 4 y equals 7

The constant on the left. Move it to the right the same way.

2x + 3y - 6 = 0 2 x plus 3 y minus 6 equals 0 2x + 3y = 6 2 x plus 3 y equals 6
Why order matters so much here. Think of a spreadsheet with a column for each person's hours. If one week's numbers were typed in a different order, the totals would be nonsense even though every number was correct. A matrix has no labels on its columns. Position is the only thing that says which variable a number belongs to. Lining the equations up first is what makes the positions trustworthy.

Two fixes in one equation

Rewrite 2z = 5 - x 2 z equals 5 minus x so the variables are on the left in order and the constant is on the right.

  1. x + 2z = 5 x plus 2 z equals 5 Add x to both sides.
  2. x before zAlphabetical order. They already are.
  3. x + 0y + 2z = 5 x plus 0 y plus 2 z equals 5 If the system also uses y, it has coefficient 0.

x + 2z = 5 x plus 2 z equals 5

5. From an equation to a row, and back again

What you will be able to do

  • Write the row for an equation.
  • Predict the size of an augmented matrix.
  • Read a row as an equation and a finished matrix as a solution.

One equation, one row

Once an equation is lined up, its row in the matrix is just its numbers read left to right: the coefficients in order, then the constant.

2x - y + 4z = 3 becomes the row 2, -1, 4, 3 2 x minus y plus 4 z equals 3 becomes the row 2, negative 1, 4, 3 x + 3z = -2 becomes the row 1, 0, 3, -2 x plus 3 z equals negative 2 becomes the row 1, 0, 3, negative 2

Stack the rows and you have the augmented matrix. The notes draw a vertical bar before the last column. The bar stands where the equals signs were.

How big it will be

Each equation is a row. Each variable is a column, plus one more column for the constants. So 2 equations in 2 variables make a 2 × 3 matrix, and 3 equations in 3 variables make a 3 × 4 matrix.

Reading a row back

Reverse the process. Put the variables back on the numbers and an equals sign at the bar.

237means 2x + 3y = 7 2 x plus 3 y equals 7 .
104means 1x + 0y = 4 1 x plus 0 y equals 4 , which is x = 4 x equals 4 .

That last kind of row is the goal. After Desmos finishes, each row has a single 1 and the rest 0s, so each row tells you one variable outright.

10301−2means x = 3 x equals 3 and y = -2 y equals negative 2 , the point (3, -2) open parenthesis 3, negative 2 close parenthesis .
Why the letters can be left out. The letters in a system never change during the work. Only the numbers do. So the letters can be dropped as long as each number stays in its column, the same way you leave out the words "tens" and "ones" when you write 47. Putting the letters back at the end loses nothing, because the column still says which variable each number belongs to.

Read a finished matrix

State the solution this finished matrix gives. 1002010−50017

  1. 1x + 0y + 0z = 2 1 x plus 0 y plus 0 z equals 2 First row. So x = 2 x equals 2 .
  2. 0x + 1y + 0z = -5 0 x plus 1 y plus 0 z equals negative 5 Second row. So y = -5 y equals negative 5 .
  3. 0x + 0y + 1z = 7 0 x plus 0 y plus 1 z equals 7 Third row. So z = 7 z equals 7 .

The solution is (2, -5, 7) open parenthesis 2, negative 5, 7 close parenthesis .

6. The arithmetic behind a row operation

What you will be able to do

  • Multiply a row by a number.
  • Add two rows.
  • Describe the finished pattern rref produces.

For reference, as the lecture says

The lecture shows how a matrix is solved by hand and says clearly that this course will use Desmos instead. So this section is short. It is here so the by-hand pages of the notes are readable, and so "rref" is not a mystery.

A row is an equation without its letters

In Section 5.1 you multiplied a whole equation by a number and added two equations. A row operation is the same move on a row.

  • Multiply a row by a number. Multiply every entry, including the last one.
  • Add two rows. Add the entries that sit in the same column.
  • Switch two rows. The equations can be listed in any order.
row 1, 2, 5 times -3 gives -3, -6, -15 row 1, 2, 5 times negative 3 gives negative 3, negative 6, negative 15 -3, -6, -15 plus 3, 1, 4 gives 0, -5, -11 negative 3, negative 6, negative 15 plus 3, 1, 4 gives 0, negative 5, negative 11

The point of that pair of moves was the 0 in the first position. It removed x from the second equation, exactly like the addition method.

What rref aims for

Reduced row-echelon form, rref for short, is the finished pattern: a 1 in each row stepping down the diagonal, and 0 everywhere else to the left of the bar. In that pattern each row names one variable, which is why it can be read off directly. Desmos does all the row operations needed to get there in one step.

Why row operations are safe. Multiplying an equation by a nonzero number, adding two equations, and reordering the list never change which values solve the system. Section 5.1 used the first two for exactly that reason. Since every row operation is one of those moves, the finished matrix describes a system with the same solution as the one you started with. It is the same answer, written in the simplest possible system.

One row operation

Multiply the row 1, -2 negative 2 , 4 by 2 and add it to the row -2 negative 2 , 5, 1.

  1. 2, -4, 8 2, negative 4, 8 Every entry of the first row times 2.
  2. 2 + (-2), -4 + 5, 8 + 1 2 plus open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis, negative 4 plus 5, 8 plus 1 Add column by column.
  3. 0, 1, 9The new row.

The new row is 0, 1, 9.

7. Entering the matrix in Desmos and reading rref

What you will be able to do

  • Set the right number of rows and columns.
  • Type every entry, including zeros.
  • Read the solution from the last column.

A different Desmos tool

Matrices are not typed into the graphing calculator. From desmos.com choose the Matrix tool. The steps are short.

  1. Click New Matrix. Desmos names it A.
  2. Set the number of rows and columns. Count them before you start: equations for rows, variables plus one for columns.
  3. Type the entries left to right, top to bottom. The Tab key moves to the next box.
  4. Click anywhere off the matrix, or the enter key on the screen.
  5. Click rref, then click A.

Three things that surprise people

  • There is no bar. Desmos does not draw the vertical line. Just type the constants in the last column.
  • Zeros must be typed. An empty box is not a 0.
  • A 0 in the answer is an answer. If the last column shows 0 for a variable, that variable equals 0. Nothing went wrong.

Reading the result

Desmos shows the finished matrix. The last column holds the values, top to bottom, in the same order as your variable columns. Write them as an ordered pair, triple, or longer list.

R = 100−101000016

Matrix R says x = -1 x equals negative 1 , y = 0 y equals 0 , and z = 6 z equals 6 . The solution is (-1, 0, 6) open parenthesis negative 1, 0, 6 close parenthesis .

Why the setup is your part of the job. Desmos can only work with what is typed. If one coefficient lands in the wrong box, the tool still gives a clean-looking answer, but to a different system. That is why the careful part of these problems is before the button: lining up, supplying the 1s and 0s, and counting rows and columns. A quick safeguard is to put your answer back into one of the original equations.
