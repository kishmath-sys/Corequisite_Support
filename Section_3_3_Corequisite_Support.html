2. Long division with numbers

What you will be able to do

  • Name the dividend, divisor, quotient, and remainder.
  • Run the divide, multiply, subtract, bring down cycle.
  • Write and check a remainder.

Four names

Polynomial long division follows the same steps as long division with whole numbers. If the number version is solid, the polynomial version is the same routine with letters attached. Start with the vocabulary, which the lecture uses constantly.

  • Dividend: the number being divided.
  • Divisor: the number you divide by.
  • Quotient: the whole-number result.
  • Remainder: what is left over.
17 divided by 5 is 3 with 2 left overdividend 17, divisor 5, quotient 3, remainder 2

The four-step cycle

Long division repeats one cycle: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down. Here is 157 divided by 4.

  1. Divide: 4 goes into 15 three times. Write 3 on top.
  2. Multiply: 3 times 4 is 12. Write 12 under the 15.
  3. Subtract: 15 - 12 = 3 15 minus 12 equals 3 .
  4. Bring down the next digit, 7, to make 37.
  5. Divide: 4 goes into 37 nine times. Write 9 on top.
  6. Multiply: 9 times 4 is 36.
  7. Subtract: 37 - 36 = 1 37 minus 36 equals 1 . There are no more digits, so 1 is the remainder.

The quotient is 39 and the remainder is 1.

Writing the remainder as a fraction

The remainder still needs to be divided by the divisor. So it is written as a fraction: remainder over divisor, added to the quotient.

17/5 = 3 + 2/5 17 over 5 equals 3 plus 2 over 5 157/4 = 39 + 1/4 157 over 4 equals 39 plus 1 over 4

Checking a division

Multiply the divisor by the quotient and add the remainder. You should get the dividend back.

5 * 3 + 2 = 17 5 times 3 plus 2 equals 17 4 * 39 + 1 = 157 4 times 39 plus 1 equals 157
What division is doing. Division asks how many groups of the divisor fit into the dividend. Seventeen items make 3 full groups of 5, which uses 15, with 2 left over. The check just rebuilds the pile: 3 groups of 5 plus the 2 extras. The same check works for polynomials, and it is the reason a remainder of 0 is special. A remainder of 0 means the divisor fits evenly, with nothing left.

A three-digit division

Divide 259 by 7.

  1. 7 into 25 goes 3 times. 3 * 7 = 21. 25 - 21 = 4. 7 into 25 goes 3 times . 3 times 7 equals 21 . 25 minus 21 equals 4 . Divide, multiply, subtract.
  2. Bring down the 9 to make 49.Bring down.
  3. 7 into 49 goes 7 times. 7 * 7 = 49. 49 - 49 = 0. 7 into 49 goes 7 times . 7 times 7 equals 49 . 49 minus 49 equals 0 . Divide, multiply, subtract.

Answer: quotient 37, remainder 0. Because the remainder is 0, 7 divides 259 evenly.

3. Dividing and multiplying terms

What you will be able to do

  • Divide one term by another.
  • Multiply a term by a binomial.
  • Subtract with every sign changed.

The three moves inside one cycle

In polynomial long division, each cycle uses three small algebra moves: divide one term by another, multiply a term by the divisor, and subtract. Each is easy alone. Practicing them separately makes the full procedure much calmer.

Move 1: divide a term by a term

Divide the coefficients, and subtract the exponents on x. Only the first term of each polynomial is used in this move.

12x^2 / 3x = 4x (12/3 = 4, and x^2/x = x) 12 x squared over 3 x equals 4 x open parenthesis 12 over 3 equals 4, and x squared over x equals x close parenthesis 6x^3 / x = 6x^2 6 x cubed over x equals 6 x squared -8x / 4x = -2 (x/x = 1) negative 8 x over 4 x equals negative 2 open parenthesis x over x equals 1 close parenthesis
Why the exponent drops. x^2 x squared means x * x x times x . Dividing by x removes one of them and leaves x. In general each division by x lowers the power by 1. When the powers match, as in -8x/4x, the x's cancel completely and only a number is left.

Move 2: multiply a term by the divisor

Distribute. Multiply coefficients and add exponents.

4x(3x - 2) = 12x^2 - 8x 4 x open parenthesis 3 x minus 2 close parenthesis equals 12 x squared minus 8 x 6x^2(x - 2) = 6x^3 - 12x^2 6 x squared open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis equals 6 x cubed minus 12 x squared

Move 3: subtract

This is where most errors happen. Subtracting a polynomial changes the sign of every term in it. The first terms always cancel, which is the whole point of the cycle.

(12x^2 + 5x) - (12x^2 - 8x) open parenthesis 12 x squared plus 5 x close parenthesis minus open parenthesis 12 x squared minus 8 x close parenthesis = 12x^2 + 5x - 12x^2 + 8x equals 12 x squared plus 5 x minus 12 x squared plus 8 x = 13x equals 13 x

Look at the x terms: 5x minus -8x negative 8 x is 5x + 8x = 13x 5 x plus 8 x equals 13 x . Writing 5x - 8x = -3x 5 x minus 8 x equals negative 3 x is the classic slip. Many people find it safer to change the signs of the bottom row and then add.

One full cycle

The dividend starts with 12x^2 + 5x 12 x squared plus 5 x and the divisor is 3x - 2 3 x minus 2 . Do one cycle.

  1. 12x^2 / 3x = 4x 12 x squared over 3 x equals 4 x Divide the first terms. 4x goes on top.
  2. 4x(3x - 2) = 12x^2 - 8x 4 x open parenthesis 3 x minus 2 close parenthesis equals 12 x squared minus 8 x Multiply 4x by the whole divisor.
  3. (12x^2 + 5x) - (12x^2 - 8x) = 13x open parenthesis 12 x squared plus 5 x close parenthesis minus open parenthesis 12 x squared minus 8 x close parenthesis equals 13 x Subtract. The first terms cancel and 5x + 8x = 13x 5 x plus 8 x equals 13 x .

After one cycle, 4x is on top and 13x is left to continue with.

Finish this one: A cycle with a cube

The dividend starts with 6x^3 - 2x^2 6 x cubed minus 2 x squared and the divisor is x - 2 x minus 2 . Do one cycle.

The first steps are done for you. You finish it.

  1. 6x^3 / x = 6x^2 6 x cubed over x equals 6 x squared Divide the first terms.
  2. 6x^2(x - 2) = 6x^3 - 12x^2 6 x squared open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis equals 6 x cubed minus 12 x squared Multiply by the whole divisor.

4. Coefficients, order, and placeholder zeros

What you will be able to do

  • Write a polynomial in descending order.
  • List its coefficients with zeros for missing powers.
  • Find the number for the box and rebuild the quotient.

Synthetic division works with numbers only

Synthetic division drops the letters and uses only the coefficients. For that to work, the coefficients must be listed in exactly the right order, with nothing skipped. Setting up that list correctly is most of the battle.

Descending order

Write the polynomial with the largest power first, then the next largest, down to the constant. This is called descending order. Each term keeps its sign when it moves.

4 - x^2 + 3x^3 becomes 3x^3 - x^2 + 4 4 minus x squared plus 3 x cubed becomes 3 x cubed minus x squared plus 4

Listing coefficients

Read off the number in front of each term, with its sign. A term with no number shown has coefficient 1 or -1 negative 1 .

2x^3 - 5x^2 + x - 8 gives 2, -5, 1, -8 2 x cubed minus 5 x squared plus x minus 8 gives 2, negative 5, 1, negative 8

Missing powers need a zero

If a power is skipped, its coefficient is 0, and the 0 must be written in the list. Count down through every power to be sure none is missed.

x^4 + 3x^2 - 6 x to the power 4 plus 3 x squared minus 6 powers present: 4, 2, 0 powers missing: 3 and 11, 0, 3, 0, -6 1, 0, 3, 0, negative 6
Why the zero matters. In the number 507, the 0 holds the tens place. Without it you would have 57, a different number. A list of coefficients works the same way: position tells you which power each number belongs to. A polynomial of degree 4 always needs five coefficients, for the powers 4, 3, 2, 1, and 0. A quick check is to count: the list should have one more entry than the degree.

The number that goes in the box

Synthetic division only works when the divisor looks like x - c x minus c . The number used in the process is c, which is the opposite of the constant in the divisor. It is also the value that makes the divisor equal zero.

x - 5 uses 5 x minus 5 uses 5 x + 3 uses -3, because x + 3 is x - (-3) x plus 3 uses negative 3, because x plus 3 is x minus open parenthesis negative 3 close parenthesis

Turning the bottom row back into a polynomial

At the end of synthetic division you get a row of numbers. The last one is the remainder. The others are the coefficients of the quotient, which has degree one less than the dividend. So if a cubic was divided, the quotient starts with x^2. x to the power 2 .

row: 2, 1, -4 and remainder 7, from dividing a cubic row : 2, 1, negative 4 and remainder 7, from dividing a cubic quotient: 2x^2 + x - 4, remainder 7 quotient : 2 x squared plus x minus 4, remainder 7

5. Multiply, then add

What you will be able to do

  • Carry out one multiply-then-add step.
  • Chain several steps with correct signs.
  • Handle a negative number in the box.

The only arithmetic synthetic division uses

Once the numbers are set up, synthetic division repeats one small pattern: multiply the latest result by the number in the box, then add that product to the next coefficient. That is all. The challenge is keeping the signs right across four or five repetitions.

One step

Suppose the box holds 3, the latest result is 2, and the next coefficient is -5 negative 5 .

multiply: 2 * 3 = 6 multiply : 2 times 3 equals 6 add: -5 + 6 = 1 add : negative 5 plus 6 equals 1

The new result is 1, and it is used in the next step.

A chain

Here is a full chain with 3 in the box and coefficients 2, -5 negative 5 , 1, -6 negative 6 . The first coefficient is simply carried down to start.

Chain with 3 in the box
CoefficientMultiply previous result by 3AddResult
2(start)2
-5 negative 5 2 * 3 = 6 2 times 3 equals 6 -5 + 6 negative 5 plus 6 1
11 * 3 = 3 1 times 3 equals 3 1 + 3 1 plus 3 4
-6 negative 6 4 * 3 = 12 4 times 3 equals 12 -6 + 12 negative 6 plus 12 6

The results are 2, 1, 4, and 6. In synthetic division the last result is the remainder.

When the box holds a negative

The pattern is the same, but every product needs a sign check. A positive times a negative is negative. A negative times a negative is positive.

A chain with a negative in the box

The box holds -2 negative 2 . The coefficients are 1, 4, -3 negative 3 . Find the results.

  1. 1Carry down the first coefficient.
  2. 1 * (-2) = -2, then 4 + (-2) = 2 1 times open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals negative 2, then 4 plus open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals 2 Multiply, then add.
  3. 2 * (-2) = -4, then -3 + (-4) = -7 2 times open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals negative 4, then negative 3 plus open parenthesis negative 4 close parenthesis equals negative 7 Multiply, then add. Two negatives added go farther negative.

Results: 1, 2, -7 negative 7

Why add and not subtract. This pattern is long division with the letters hidden. In long division you multiply and then subtract. Synthetic division puts the opposite of the divisor's constant in the box, so the subtraction turns into addition. Adding is less error-prone than subtracting, which is the whole reason the shortcut exists.

A negative input

A slip to avoid. The power is done before the multiplication by the coefficient. 2(-2)^3 2 open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis cubed is 2 times -8 negative 8 , which is -16 negative 16 . It is not (2 times -2 negative 2 ) cubed, which would be -64 negative 64 . Parentheses around the input make it clear what the exponent applies to.
