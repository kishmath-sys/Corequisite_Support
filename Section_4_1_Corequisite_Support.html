2. Zero and negative exponents, and fractions raised to a power

What you will be able to do

  • Use b^0 = 1 b to the power 0 equals 1 .
  • Rewrite a negative exponent as one over.
  • Raise a fraction to a positive or negative power.

What a power is

In 2^3 2 cubed , the 2 is the base and the 3 is the exponent. A positive whole exponent counts how many copies of the base are multiplied. 2^3 = 2 * 2 * 2 = 8 2 cubed equals 2 times 2 times 2 equals 8 . It is not 2 * 3 2 times 3 .

Follow the pattern down

Look at what happens to powers of 2 as the exponent drops by 1 each time.

Powers of 2
Power2^3 2 cubed 2^2 2 squared 2^1 2 to the power 1 2^0 2 to the power 0 2^-1 2 to the power negative 1 2^-2 2 to the power negative 2 2^-3 2 to the power negative 3
Value84211/21/41/8

Each step to the right divides by 2. Going from 2^1 = 2 2 to the power 1 equals 2 one more step gives 2^0 = 1 2 to the power 0 equals 1 . One more step gives 2^-1 2 to the power negative 1 = 1/2. The pattern never breaks, and it gives the two rules below.

  • Zero exponent. Any base (except 0) to the power 0 is 1. So 5^0 = 1 5 to the power 0 equals 1 and (1/3)^0 = 1 open parenthesis 1 over 3 close parenthesis to the power 0 equals 1 .
  • Negative exponent. A negative exponent means "one over." So 3^-2 = 1/3^2 3 to the power negative 2 equals 1 over 3 squared = 1/9. The answer is a small positive number. A negative exponent does not make the answer negative.
Why zero and negative exponents work this way. Raising the exponent by 1 multiplies by the base. So lowering the exponent by 1 has to divide by the base. Start at 3^1 = 3 3 to the power 1 equals 3 . Divide by 3 to get 3^0 = 1 3 to the power 0 equals 1 . Divide by 3 again to get 3^-1 3 to the power negative 1 = 1/3. Divide again to get 3^-2 3 to the power negative 2 = 1/9. The rules are not extra facts to memorize. They are the same pattern continued past 1.

A fraction as the base

To raise a fraction to a power, raise the top and the bottom to that power.

(1/3)^2 = 1^2/3^2 = 1/9 open parenthesis 1 over 3 close parenthesis squared equals 1 squared over 3 squared equals 1 over 9 (2/5)^2 = 4/25 open parenthesis 2 over 5 close parenthesis squared equals 4 over 25

With a negative exponent, flip the fraction first. That takes care of the "one over." Then use the positive exponent.

(1/3)^-1 = 3 open parenthesis 1 over 3 close parenthesis to the power negative 1 equals 3 (1/5)^-2 = 5^2 = 25 open parenthesis 1 over 5 close parenthesis to the power negative 2 equals 5 squared equals 25

3. Tables of values and the shape of the graph

What you will be able to do

  • Fill a table for b^x. b to the power x .
  • Tell increasing from decreasing by the base.
  • Name the y-intercept and the horizontal asymptote.

Filling a table

A table of values lists inputs in one column and outputs in the other. For f(x) = 3^x f open parenthesis x close parenthesis equals 3 to the power x , each output is 3 raised to the input.

Table for f(x) = 3^x f open parenthesis x close parenthesis equals 3 to the power x
x-2 negative 2 -1 negative 1 012
f(x)1/91/3139

Each row gives one point. The row x = 2 x equals 2 , f(x) = 9 f open parenthesis x close parenthesis equals 9 is the point (2, 9). Plot the points and join them with a smooth curve.

Two shapes

Every graph of y = b^x y equals b to the power x has one of two shapes, and the base decides which.

The words the lecture uses

  • Increasing: the graph rises as you read left to right. This happens when the base is greater than 1, as in Graph A.
  • Decreasing: the graph falls as you read left to right. This happens when the base is between 0 and 1, as in Graph B.
  • y-intercept: the point where the graph crosses the y-axis. That is where x = 0 x equals 0 . Since b^0 = 1 b to the power 0 equals 1 , the y-intercept is always (0, 1).
  • Horizontal asymptote: a horizontal line the graph gets closer and closer to without reaching. Here it is the x-axis.
  • Domain: the inputs you may use. Any number works as an exponent, so the domain is all real numbers.
  • Range: the outputs that come out. They are always positive.
Why the graph never touches the x-axis. A positive base raised to any power stays positive. Negative exponents make small fractions such as 1/8 and 1/1000, but never 0 and never a negative number. That is why the graph gets close to the x-axis and never touches or crosses it. It also explains the shapes. Multiplying over and over by a number bigger than 1 grows. Multiplying over and over by a number between 0 and 1 shrinks.

One table entry

4. Rounding decimals

What you will be able to do

  • Name decimal places.
  • Round up or leave alone using the next digit.
  • Round money to the nearest cent.

Place names

Digits to the right of the decimal point have names. In 2.7183, the 7 is in the tenths place, the 1 is in the hundredths place, the 8 is in the thousandths place, and the 3 is in the ten-thousandths place. "Three decimal places" means keep three digits after the point.

The rule

  1. Find the last digit you are keeping.
  2. Look at the one digit right after it.
  3. If that next digit is 5 or more, raise the kept digit by 1. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop everything after the kept digit.
2.7183 to three places: the next digit is 3, so 2.7180.84672 to three places: the next digit is 7, so 0.8471.4142 to two places: the next digit is 4, so 1.41

Money

"Nearest cent" means two decimal places, because a cent is a hundredth of a dollar.

When a 9 rounds up

If the kept digit is 9 and it must go up, it becomes 0 and the digit to its left goes up. 4.3197 to three places is 4.320. The final 0 shows the rounding was done. As a number, 4.320 and 4.32 are equal, and this page accepts either.

Why we look at the next digit. Rounding picks the nearest value with the allowed number of digits. 0.84672 sits between 0.846 and 0.847. Halfway would be 0.8465. Since 0.84672 is past halfway, 0.847 is closer. Looking at the next digit is just a quick test for which side of halfway you are on. Simply chopping off the extra digits would give 0.846, which is farther away.

Round a Desmos result

5. Percents, and what P, r, n, and t stand for

What you will be able to do

  • Change a percent to a decimal.
  • Match compounding words to n.
  • List P, r, n, and t from a sentence.

Percent to decimal

Percent means "out of 100." To use a percent in a formula, divide it by 100. Dividing by 100 moves the decimal point two places to the left.

4% = 4/100 = 0.04 4 % equals 4 over 100 equals 0.04 12% = 0.12 12 % equals 0.12 2.5% = 0.025 2.5 % equals 0.025

A single-digit percent needs a zero to hold the tenths place. 4% is 0.04, not 0.4. The number 0.4 is 40%.

The four letters

The compound interest formula is A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) A equals P open parenthesis 1 plus r over n close parenthesis to the power nt, . Before any arithmetic, the lecture makes a list of what each letter is.

  • P is the principal: the money you start with.
  • r is the yearly interest rate, written as a decimal.
  • n is the number of times interest is added each year.
  • t is the time in years.
  • A is the amount at the end. It is usually what you are asked to find.

Words that tell you n

To compound means to add the interest to the account so that it starts earning interest too. A word in the problem tells you how many times per year that happens.

The word "continuously"

If the problem says compounded continuously, there is no n. That one word tells you to use the other formula, A = Pe^(rt). The letter e in it is not a variable. It is a fixed number, about 2.718, that Desmos already knows.

Why the decimal point moves two places. Take 4%. It means 4 out of every 100, which is the fraction 4/100. Divide: 4/100 = 0.04 4 over 100 equals 0.04 . A quick check with money: 4% of $100 should be $4, and 0.04 * 100 = 4 0.04 times 100 equals 4 . If you used 0.4 you would get $40, which is 40% of the money. A check like this catches a misplaced decimal point every time.

  4. t = 7 t equals 7 Years.

P = 5000 P equals 5000 , r = 0.04 r equals 0.04 , n = 4 n equals 4 , t = 7 t equals 7 . A is what would be found.

6. Order of operations and typing into Desmos

What you will be able to do

  • Work a formula in the right order.
  • Put a whole exponent in parentheses.
  • Use the caret and the number e in Desmos.

The order

A formula with several operations is worked in a fixed order: parentheses first, then exponents, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. In P(1 + r/n)^(nt) P open parenthesis 1 plus r over n close parenthesis to the power nt, that means:

  1. Inside the parentheses, divide r by n, then add 1.
  2. Multiply n by t to get the exponent.
  3. Raise the parentheses to that exponent.
  4. Multiply by P last.

Desmos follows this order for you. Your job is to type the formula so Desmos sees the same grouping you see on paper.

The exponent needs its own parentheses

In Desmos the caret key ^ (Shift and 6) raises to a power. On paper, a raised exponent such as 4 * 7 4 times 7 is clearly all in the exponent. Typed on one line, it is not clear unless you add parentheses.

2^(3*2) = 2^6 = 64 2 to the power 3 times 2, equals 2 to the power 6 equals 64 2^3*2 = 8 * 2 = 16 2 cubed times 2 equals 8 times 2 equals 16

Those are different numbers. Without parentheses, only the 3 is the exponent and the *2 multiplies afterward. So type the whole exponent in parentheses. The lecture types (4)(7), two sets of parentheses side by side, which also means multiply.

A fraction base needs parentheses too

(1/3)^2 open parenthesis 1 over 3 close parenthesis squared is 1/9. Without the parentheses, 1/3^2 1 over 3 squared means 1 divided by 3^2 3 squared , which happens to match here, but 2/3^2 2 over 3 squared is 2/9 while (2/3)^2 open parenthesis 2 over 3 close parenthesis squared is 4/9. Put the whole base in parentheses.

The number e

Type the letter e and Desmos treats it as the number 2.71828... You can type e^2 e squared or e^(0.5) e to the power 0.5, just as you would with any other base.

Why the order matters. Compute 1000(1.05)^2 1000 open parenthesis 1.05 close parenthesis squared both ways. The right order does the exponent first: 1.05^2 = 1.1025 1.05 squared equals 1.1025 , then 1000 * 1.1025 = 1102.50 1000 times 1.1025 equals 1102.50 . If you multiply first you get 1050^2 1050 squared , which is more than a million dollars. Order of operations is an agreement that makes a formula mean one thing only. Parentheses are how you tell Desmos which thing you mean.

The inside of the parentheses

Compute 1 + 0 1 plus 0 .08/4.

  1. 0.08/4 = 0.02 0.08 over 4 equals 0.02 Division comes before addition.
  2. 1 + 0.02 = 1.02 1 plus 0.02 equals 1.02 Then add 1.

Answer: 1.02
