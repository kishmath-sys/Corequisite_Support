2. Percents and decimals

What you will be able to do

  • Change any percent to a decimal.
  • Find a percent of a number.
  • Say what percent is paid after a discount or a tax.

What percent means

Percent means "per hundred." 37% means 37 out of every 100. As a fraction that is 37/100, and as a decimal it is 0.37. A percent, a fraction, and a decimal are three ways to write the same amount.

Algebra works with the decimal form. To change a percent to a decimal, divide by 100, which moves the decimal point two places to the left.

37% = 37/100 = 0.37 37 % equals 37 over 100 equals 0.37 80% = 0.80 = 0.8 80 % equals 0.80 equals 0.8 125% = 1.25 125 % equals 1.25
Single-digit percents need a placeholder zero. 6% is 6.0%, and moving the point two places gives 0.06. Writing 0.6 would mean 60%, ten times too much. This is one of the most common slips in the whole section.

Percent of a number

In math, the word of after a percent means multiply. To find 15% of 60, change 15% to 0.15 and multiply by 60.

Find a percent of a number

Find 15% of 60.

  1. 15% = 0.15 15 % equals 0.15 Change the percent to a decimal.
  2. 0.15 * 60 0.15 times 60 "Of" means multiply.
  3. 9So 15% of 60 is 9.

Answer: 9

A sense check. 10% of 60 is 6, one tenth of it. 5% is half of that, 3. Together 15% is 9. A quick estimate like this is a good way to catch a misplaced decimal point.

Discounts and added percents

The original amount is always 100% of itself. If a store takes 20% off, you pay what is left: 100% - 20% = 80% of the original price. If a 6% tax is added, you pay the original plus 6% more: 100% + 6% = 106% of the original.

What you pay after a discount

A price is reduced by 20%. What percent of the original price do you pay?

  1. 100% - 20% 100 % minus 20 % The whole price is 100%. The discount removes 20% of it.
  2. 80%You pay 80% of the original, which is 0.80 times the original.

Answer: 80%

3. Translating words into expressions

What you will be able to do

  • Say what a variable stands for.
  • Translate add, subtract, multiply, and divide phrases.
  • Write a rate-plus-fee expression.
  • Write a percent of an unknown and combine it with the unknown.

A variable stands for a number you do not know yet

A word problem describes a number without telling you what it is. Algebra gives that unknown number a name, usually x, so you can write about it. The first step is always to say in words what x stands for, such as "x = the original price in dollars." That sentence gives every later line a meaning.

"Less than" reverses the order. "5 less than a number" starts with the number and takes 5 away: x - 5 x minus 5 . It is not 5 - x 5 minus x . Test it with a real number: 5 less than 20 is 15, which is 20 - 5 20 minus 5 .

A rate times an amount, plus a fixed fee

Many real situations have a cost that grows (so much per hour, per mile, per month) plus a one-time amount. "Per" tells you which number multiplies the variable. The one-time fee is added on by itself.

Rate and fee

A gym charges $12 per month plus a one-time $25 sign-up fee. Write the total cost for x months.

  1. x = the number of months x equals the number of months Say what the variable stands for.
  2. 12x$12 for each month: 12 times the number of months.
  3. 12x + 25The sign-up fee is paid once, so it is added without an x.

Answer: 12x + 25 12 x plus 25

A percent of an unknown number

"30% of a number" means 0.30 times x, written 0.30x. If a number is reduced by 30% of itself, start with the whole number x and subtract that part: x - 0.30x x minus 0.30 x .

x - 0.30x = 0.70x x minus 0.30 x equals 0.70 x x + 0.06x = 1.06x x plus 0.06 x equals 1.06 x

4. Perimeter and other formulas

What you will be able to do

  • Tell perimeter from area.
  • Write one side of a rectangle in terms of the other.
  • Put both sides into the perimeter formula.

What perimeter is

The perimeter of a shape is the distance around its edge. Imagine walking the fence line of a yard and counting your steps. A rectangle has two long sides, each called the length (L), and two short sides, each called the width (W). Walking all four sides gives L + W + L + W L plus W plus L plus W , which is 2L + 2W 2 L plus 2 W .

Do not mix this up with area, which is the amount of surface inside, L times W. Perimeter is fencing. Area is carpet.

P = 2L + 2W P equals 2 L plus 2 W

When one side is described using the other

A word problem will not hand you the length and the width. It tells you how they are related, such as "the length is 4 feet more than the width." Since you only want one unknown, let x be one side and write the other side using x.

  • "The length is 4 more than the width": width = x, length = x + 4 x plus 4
  • "The length is 5 times the width": width = x, length = 5x
  • "The length is 3 less than twice the width": width = x, length = 2x - 3 2 x minus 3

Set up and solve a perimeter equation

A rectangular garden is 4 feet longer than it is wide. Its perimeter is 40 feet. Find the width and the length.

  1. width = x, length = x + 4 width equals x, length equals x plus 4 Name the shorter side x. The length is 4 more.
  2. 2(x + 4) + 2x = 40 2 open parenthesis x plus 4 close parenthesis plus 2 x equals 40 Put the two sides into P = 2L + 2W P equals 2 L plus 2 W .
  3. 2x + 8 + 2x = 40 2 x plus 8 plus 2 x equals 40 Distribute the 2.
  4. 4x + 8 = 40 4 x plus 8 equals 40 Combine like terms.
  5. 4x = 32, so x = 8 4 x equals 32, so x equals 8 Subtract 8, then divide by 4.
  6. width 8 feet, length 12 feetThe length is x + 4 = 12 x plus 4 equals 12 . Check: 2(12) + 2(8) = 40 2 open parenthesis 12 close parenthesis plus 2 open parenthesis 8 close parenthesis equals 40 .

Answer: 8 feet wide and 12 feet long

One answer is often not the whole answer. Solving gives x, and x is only what you named it. If the question asks for both dimensions, go back and compute the other one.

5. Solving equations with decimals

What you will be able to do

  • Solve one-step and two-step equations with decimals.
  • Explain why dividing by a number less than 1 gives a larger number.
  • Combine x with a decimal multiple of x.

Decimals follow the same rules

An equation with decimals is solved exactly like one with whole numbers: undo additions and subtractions first, then divide by the coefficient of x. Nothing about the method changes. Only the arithmetic looks different, and a calculator can handle that part.

Dividing by a decimal

The step that feels strange is dividing by a number smaller than 1. Dividing 30 by 0.25 asks "how many quarters fit into 30?" Four quarters fit into each 1, so 120 quarters fit into 30. Dividing by a number less than 1 makes the result larger. That is a good check on your answer.

One step with a decimal coefficient

Solve 0.25x = 30 0.25 x equals 30 .

  1. x = 30 / 0.25 x equals 30 over 0.25 x is multiplied by 0.25, so divide both sides by 0.25.
  2. x = 120 x equals 120 On a calculator, 30 divided by 0.25 is 120. It is larger than 30 because 0.25 is less than 1.
  3. check: 0.25(120) = 30 check : 0.25 open parenthesis 120 close parenthesis equals 30 A quarter of 120 is 30. True.

Answer: x = 120 x equals 120

Two steps

Subtract, then divide

Solve 2.5x + 4.75 = 19.75 2.5 x plus 4.75 equals 19.75 .

  1. 2.5x = 15 2.5 x equals 15 Subtract 4.75 from both sides: 19.75 - 4.75 = 15 19.75 minus 4.75 equals 15 .
  2. x = 6 x equals 6 Divide both sides by 2.5.
  3. check: 2.5(6) + 4.75 = 15 + 4.75 = 19.75 check : 2.5 open parenthesis 6 close parenthesis plus 4.75 equals 15 plus 4.75 equals 19.75 True.

Answer: x = 6 x equals 6

Combining x with a decimal times x

An expression like x - 0.20x x minus 0.20 x has two like terms. The plain x is 1x, so x - 0.20x = 0.80x x minus 0.20 x equals 0.80 x . Combine first, then divide.

Combine, then divide

Solve x - 0.20x = 64 x minus 0.20 x equals 64 .

  1. 0.80x = 64 0.80 x equals 64 1x - 0.20x = 0.80x 1 x minus 0.20 x equals 0.80 x .
  2. x = 64 / 0.80 x equals 64 over 0.80 Divide both sides by 0.80.
  3. x = 80 x equals 80 Check: 80 - 0.20(80) = 80 - 16 = 64 80 minus 0.20 open parenthesis 80 close parenthesis equals 80 minus 16 equals 64 . True.

Answer: x = 80 x equals 80

6. Paychecks, rates, and answering in context

What you will be able to do

  • Turn a pay schedule into a number of paychecks.
  • Write a total as equal parts plus a one-time amount.
  • State an answer with its meaning and check that it makes sense.

Pay period vocabulary

Pay problems depend on how many paychecks there are in a year. The words are easy to mix up, so here they are side by side.

How often and how many
PhrasePaychecks per year
weekly52
every two weeks (biweekly)26
twice a month (semimonthly)24
monthly12

"Every two weeks" and "twice a month" are different. A year has 52 weeks, which makes 26 two-week periods. It has 12 months, which makes 24 half-months.

A total made of equal parts plus an extra

If each paycheck is x dollars and there are 12 of them, the paychecks total 12x. If the yearly pay also includes a one-time bonus, the bonus is added once. This is the same "rate times amount, plus a fixed fee" pattern as before.

Pay with a bonus

A job pays $45,900 a year, which includes a $900 bonus. Paychecks come monthly. How much is each paycheck?

  1. x = the amount of each paycheck x equals the amount of each paycheck Say what the variable stands for.
  2. 12x + 900 = 45900 12 x plus 900 equals 45900 Twelve paychecks plus the bonus make the yearly total.
  3. 12x = 45000 12 x equals 45000 Subtract 900 from both sides.
  4. x = 3750 x equals 3750 Divide both sides by 12.

Each paycheck is $3,750.

Answer the question that was asked

Solving the equation gives a number. The problem asked a question about a real situation. Finish every word problem with three checks:

  1. Meaning. Go back to what x stands for. Is the answer dollars, miles, hours, feet?
  2. Sense. Is the size believable? An original price has to be more than the sale price. A width cannot be negative.
  3. Completeness. Did the question ask for more than x, such as both dimensions?
