2. Moving a point on the grid

What you will be able to do

  • Change the right coordinate for each direction.
  • Move a graph by moving its key points.
  • Reflect a point over the x-axis.

Which coordinate changes?

A point has two coordinates, (x, y). The first says how far left or right the point is. The second says how far up or down. Moving the point changes one or both of those numbers. Every graph transformation in Section 2.5 comes down to moving points, so this is the place to start.

  • Up adds to y. Down subtracts from y. The x-coordinate does not change.
  • Right adds to x. Left subtracts from x. The y-coordinate does not change.
(4, 1) moved up 3 is (4, 4)(4, 1) moved left 6 is (-2, 1) open parenthesis 4, 1 close parenthesis moved left 6 is open parenthesis negative 2, 1 close parenthesis (4, 1) moved right 2 and down 5 is (6, -4) open parenthesis 4, 1 close parenthesis moved right 2 and down 5 is open parenthesis 6, negative 4 close parenthesis
Why those coordinates. The x-axis runs left and right, so sideways motion is a change in x. The y-axis runs up and down, so vertical motion is a change in y. Positive directions are right and up. That is the same convention used to plot the point in the first place.

Moving a whole graph means moving its key points

A graph is made of points. To slide a graph, you slide each of its important points by the same amount and then connect them the same way as before. The shape does not change. Only the position does.

Slide every key point

  2. (0, 3) goes to (2, 6)The same change for every point.
  3. (3, 0) goes to (5, 3)Connect the new points in the same order.

The new graph has key points (0, 4), (2, 6), and (5, 3).

Flipping over the x-axis

To reflect a point over the x-axis, picture a mirror lying along the x-axis. A point above the axis appears the same distance below it. The x-coordinate stays the same and the y-coordinate changes sign.

(2, 5) reflects to (2, -5) open parenthesis 2, 5 close parenthesis reflects to open parenthesis 2, negative 5 close parenthesis (-3, -4) reflects to (-3, 4) open parenthesis negative 3, negative 4 close parenthesis reflects to open parenthesis negative 3, 4 close parenthesis (6, 0) reflects to (6, 0)

The last line matters. A point that sits on the x-axis has height 0, and the opposite of 0 is 0. So points on the x-axis do not move when a graph is flipped.

3. The basic functions and their key points

What you will be able to do

  • Evaluate each basic function at easy inputs.
  • Choose inputs that give whole-number outputs.
  • Know which functions accept negative inputs.

Five rules to know on sight

The lecture names a small set of basic functions and expects you to know what each one does to a number. Then it moves their graphs around. If you can make a quick table for each one, you can always rebuild its graph. Here are the five that take the most care.

Squaring: x^2 x squared

Multiply the number by itself. A negative input gives a positive output, so the outputs for 2 and -2 negative 2 match. Key points: (-2, 4) open parenthesis negative 2, 4 close parenthesis , (-1, 1) open parenthesis negative 1, 1 close parenthesis , (0, 0), (1, 1), (2, 4). The graph is a U shape.

Absolute value: |x| the absolute value of x, end absolute value,

The absolute value of a number is its distance from zero. Distance is never negative, so the bars turn a negative number positive and leave a positive number alone. |-6| = 6 the absolute value of negative 6, end absolute value, equals 6 and |6| = 6 the absolute value of 6, end absolute value, equals 6 and |0| = 0 the absolute value of 0, end absolute value, equals 0 . Key points: (-2, 2) open parenthesis negative 2, 2 close parenthesis , (-1, 1) open parenthesis negative 1, 1 close parenthesis , (0, 0), (1, 1), (2, 2). The graph is a V shape.

Square root: sqrt(x) the square root of x

The square root asks "what non-negative number times itself gives x?" sqrt(9) = 3 the square root of 9 equals 3 . Choose inputs that are perfect squares: 0, 1, 4, 9. Key points: (0, 0), (1, 1), (4, 2), (9, 3). A negative input has no real square root, because no real number times itself is negative. So the graph starts at (0, 0) and only goes right.

Cubing: x^3 x cubed

Multiply three copies of the number. This time a negative input stays negative, because three negative factors make a negative. Key points: (-2, -8) open parenthesis negative 2, negative 8 close parenthesis , (-1, -1) open parenthesis negative 1, negative 1 close parenthesis , (0, 0), (1, 1), (2, 8). The graph is an S shape that rises through the origin.

Cube root

The cube root asks "what number, used as a factor three times, gives x?" The cube root of 8 is 2, because 2 * 2 * 2 = 8 2 times 2 times 2 equals 8 . Unlike a square root, a cube root of a negative number is fine: the cube root of -8 negative 8 is -2 negative 2 , because (-2)(-2)(-2) = -8 open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals negative 8 . Choose inputs that are perfect cubes: -8 negative 8 , -1 negative 1 , 0, 1, 8. Key points: (-8, -2) open parenthesis negative 8, negative 2 close parenthesis , (-1, -1) open parenthesis negative 1, negative 1 close parenthesis , (0, 0), (1, 1), (8, 2). In Desmos, type cbrt to get the cube root symbol.

Why the shapes differ. Squaring and absolute value both erase a negative sign, so their graphs look the same on the left and right sides. Cubing and cube root keep the sign, so their graphs go down on the left and up on the right. Square root cannot accept negatives at all, so it has only a right side. Knowing which of the three behaviors a function has tells you its shape before you plot anything.

4. Inside or outside: reading the order of a formula

What you will be able to do

  • Sort the parts of a formula into inside and outside.
  • Evaluate in the right order.
  • Find the input that makes the inside zero.

Every formula has an order

A formula such as sqrt(x + 5) + 2 the square root of x plus 5, end root, plus 2 tells a small story about what happens to the input. First 5 is added. Then the square root is taken. Then 2 is added. The lecture sorts the pieces of a formula into two groups, and the whole section depends on telling them apart.

  • Inside means it happens to x before the main function acts. It sits with the x: under the root, inside the absolute value bars, or inside the parentheses that get the power.
  • Outside means it happens after the main function acts. It sits apart from the x, usually at the end.
sqrt(x + 5) + 2 the 5 is inside, the 2 is outside the square root of x plus 5, end root, plus 2 the 5 is inside, the 2 is outside (x - 3)^2 + 1 the -3 is inside, the 1 is outside open parenthesis x minus 3 close parenthesis squared plus 1 the negative 3 is inside, the 1 is outside |x + 2| - 4 the 2 is inside, the -4 is outside the absolute value of x plus 2, end absolute value, minus 4 the 2 is inside, the negative 4 is outside

With function notation it is the same. In f(x - 2) + 4 f open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis plus 4 , the -2 negative 2 is inside the parentheses of f, so it is inside. The 4 comes after f has done its work, so it is outside.

Why the placement matters

Moving a number from outside to inside changes the answer. Compare sqrt(x) + 9 the square root of x plus 9 and sqrt(x + 9) the square root of x plus 9, end root, with the input 16. The first gives 4 + 9 = 13 4 plus 9 equals 13 . The second gives sqrt(25) = 5 the square root of 25 equals 5 . Same numbers, different order, different result.

What inside and outside do. An outside number is added to the output, and the output is a height. So an outside number changes heights. An inside number is combined with the input, and the input is a left-right position. So an inside number changes left-right positions. That one observation is the reason the lecture's rules come out the way they do.

What input makes the inside zero?

Each basic function has its special point at input 0: the bottom of the U, the corner of the V, the starting point of the square root. When a number is inside with x, the special point happens where the inside equals 0. Finding that input is a one-step equation.

(x - 3)^2: the inside is x - 3. Set x - 3 = 0. So x = 3. open parenthesis x minus 3 close parenthesis squared : the inside is x minus 3 . Set x minus 3 equals 0 . So x equals 3 . sqrt(x + 5): the inside is x + 5. Set x + 5 = 0. So x = -5. the square root of x plus 5, end root, : the inside is x plus 5 . Set x plus 5 equals 0 . So x equals negative 5 .

Notice the sign. The inside says x + 5 x plus 5 but the special input is -5 negative 5 . This is why the lecture says an inside number moves the graph "the opposite way you would think." It is not a trick. It is what solving the equation gives.

5. A negative sign out front

What you will be able to do

  • Apply a front negative after the main function.
  • Tell -x^2 negative x squared from (-x)^2. open parenthesis negative x close parenthesis to the power 2 .
  • See that a front negative changes the sign of every output.

The negative is the last step

Several functions in this section begin with a negative sign, such as -x^2 negative x squared or -|x| negative the absolute value of x, end absolute value, or -sqrt(x) negative the square root of x . The negative sign out front is an outside operation. It is applied after the main function has finished. So you work out the function first and change the sign of the result last.

-x^2 with x = 3: -(3)^2 = -(9) = -9 negative x squared with x equals 3 : minus open parenthesis 3 close parenthesis squared equals negative open parenthesis 9 close parenthesis equals negative 9 -|x| with x = -4: -|-4| = -(4) = -4 negative the absolute value of x, end absolute value, with x equals negative 4 : minus the absolute value of negative 4, end absolute value, equals negative open parenthesis 4 close parenthesis equals negative 4 -sqrt(x) with x = 25: -sqrt(25) = -(5) = -5 negative the square root of x with x equals 25 : minus the square root of 25 equals negative open parenthesis 5 close parenthesis equals negative 5

The classic mix-up: -x^2 negative x squared and (-x)^2 open parenthesis negative x close parenthesis squared

These look alike and are different. In -x^2 negative x squared the power is done first and the negative is applied to the result. The answer is negative or zero, always. In (-x)^2 open parenthesis negative x close parenthesis squared the parentheses make the negative part of what is squared. The answer is positive or zero, always.

x = 3: -x^2 = -9 but (-x)^2 = (-3)^2 = 9 x equals 3 : minus x squared equals negative 9 but open parenthesis negative x close parenthesis squared equals open parenthesis negative 3 close parenthesis squared equals 9

A negative input needs extra care because two negatives are in play. For -x^2 negative x squared with x = -3 x equals negative 3 : first (-3)^2 = 9 open parenthesis negative 3 close parenthesis squared equals 9 , then the front negative makes it -9 negative 9 . The two negative signs do different jobs. One belongs to the input and one belongs to the formula.

Why the negative waits. Order of operations puts powers ahead of multiplication, and a negative sign out front is a multiplication by -1 negative 1 . So -x^2 negative x squared means (-1) open parenthesis negative 1 close parenthesis times (x^2) open parenthesis x squared close parenthesis . The same holds for the bars and the root: they act like parentheses and are finished before the -1 negative 1 is applied.

What this does to every output

A negative out front changes the sign of every output. An output of 4 becomes -4 negative 4 . An output of -1 negative 1 becomes 1. An output of 0 stays 0. Compare the two columns in the table.
