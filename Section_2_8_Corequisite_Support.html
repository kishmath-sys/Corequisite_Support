2. Squares and square roots

What you will be able to do

  • Square a radius without doubling it.
  • Recover a radius from r^2. r to the power 2 .
  • Handle a radius that is a square root.

Why the equation of a circle has squares in it

The equation of a circle uses the radius squared, written r^2. r to the power 2 . The radius is the distance from the center of the circle to its edge. So this section goes back and forth between a number and its square constantly. Given a radius, you square it. Given the squared value, you take a square root to recover the radius.

From r to r^2 r squared

Squaring means multiplying the number by itself.

r = 4 gives r^2 = 16 r equals 4 gives r squared equals 16 r = 7 gives r^2 = 49 r equals 7 gives r squared equals 49 r = 10 gives r^2 = 100 r equals 10 gives r squared equals 100

The slip to watch for is doubling. 7^2 7 squared is 7 * 7 = 49 7 times 7 equals 49 . It is not 7 * 2 = 14 7 times 2 equals 14 .

From r^2 r squared to r

Take the square root. Because a radius is a distance, it is never negative, so you only need the positive root. There is no plus-or-minus here.

r^2 = 81 gives r = 9 r squared equals 81 gives r equals 9 r^2 = 1 gives r = 1 r squared equals 1 gives r equals 1

When the number is not a perfect square

If r^2 r squared is a number like 7, there is no whole number that squares to it. The radius is written exactly as sqrt(7) the square root of 7 . Do not round it. Going the other way is even simpler: squaring a square root returns the number inside.

r^2 = 7 gives r = sqrt(7) r squared equals 7 gives r equals the square root of 7 r = sqrt(5) gives r^2 = 5 r equals the square root of 5 gives r squared equals 5
Why squaring a root is easy. The square root of 5 is defined as "the number whose square is 5." So squaring it gives 5 by definition. A calculator would show sqrt(5) the square root of 5 as about 2.236, and 2.236 * 2.236 2.236 times 2.236 is about 5. The exact form is cleaner and it is what MyMathLab expects.

Both directions

A circle has r^2 = 144 r squared equals 144 . What is r? Another circle has r = sqrt(11) r equals the square root of 11 . What is r^2 r squared ?

  1. r = sqrt(144) = 12 r equals the square root of 144 equals 12 12 * 12 = 144 12 times 12 equals 144 .
  2. r^2 = (sqrt(11))^2 = 11 r squared equals open parenthesis the square root of 11 close parenthesis squared equals 11 Squaring undoes the square root.

Answers: r = 12 r equals 12 , and r^2 = 11 r squared equals 11 .

3. Filling in and reading (x - h) open parenthesis x minus h close parenthesis and (y - k) open parenthesis y minus k close parenthesis

What you will be able to do

  • Fill a coordinate into (x - h) open parenthesis x minus h close parenthesis with the right sign.
  • Read a coordinate out with the opposite sign.
  • Recognize x^2 x squared as a center coordinate of 0.

The pattern has minus signs built in

The lecture's circle equation has the parts (x - h) open parenthesis x minus h close parenthesis and (y - k) open parenthesis y minus k close parenthesis , where (h, k) is the center. The letters h and k are placeholders for two numbers. Two minus signs are already written into the pattern. That is where the sign trouble comes from.

Filling in

Replace h with the first coordinate of the center and k with the second. Use parentheses, then clean up. A positive coordinate leaves the minus. A negative coordinate makes a double negative, which becomes a plus. A coordinate of 0 leaves nothing to subtract.

h = 2: (x - (2)) = (x - 2) h equals 2 : open parenthesis x minus open parenthesis 2 close parenthesis close parenthesis equals open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis h = -7: (x - (-7)) = (x + 7) h equals negative 7 : open parenthesis x minus open parenthesis negative 7 close parenthesis close parenthesis equals open parenthesis x plus 7 close parenthesis h = 0: (x - (0)) = x h equals 0 : open parenthesis x minus open parenthesis 0 close parenthesis close parenthesis equals x

Reading it back out

Going the other way, you see the cleaned-up form and need the coordinate. The coordinate has the opposite sign from what is written.

(x - 2) comes from h = 2 open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis comes from h equals 2 (x + 7) comes from h = -7 open parenthesis x plus 7 close parenthesis comes from h equals negative 7 x^2 alone comes from h = 0, because x^2 is (x - 0)^2 x squared alone comes from h equals 0, because x squared is open parenthesis x minus 0 close parenthesis squared
A way to never guess the sign. A reliable way to read the coordinate is to ask "what value makes the parentheses equal zero?" For (x + 7) open parenthesis x plus 7 close parenthesis , solve x + 7 = 0 x plus 7 equals 0 to get x = -7 x equals negative 7 . That works because the center is the one spot where both squared parts measure zero distance. It is the same question you used in Section 2.5 for the inside of a function.

Fill in a center

Fill the center (2, -5) open parenthesis 2, negative 5 close parenthesis into (x - h) open parenthesis x minus h close parenthesis and (y - k) open parenthesis y minus k close parenthesis .

  1. (x - (2)) and (y - (-5)) open parenthesis x minus open parenthesis 2 close parenthesis close parenthesis and open parenthesis y minus open parenthesis negative 5 close parenthesis close parenthesis Put each coordinate in parentheses.
  2. (x - 2) and (y + 5) open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis and open parenthesis y plus 5 close parenthesis The double negative becomes a plus.

Answer: (x - 2) open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis and (y + 5) open parenthesis y plus 5 close parenthesis

Read a center

A circle equation contains (x + 9)^2 open parenthesis x plus 9 close parenthesis squared and (y - 1)^2. open parenthesis y minus 1 close parenthesis to the power 2 . What is the center?

  1. x + 9 = 0 gives x = -9 x plus 9 equals 0 gives x equals negative 9 What makes the first parentheses zero?
  2. y - 1 = 0 gives y = 1 y minus 1 equals 0 gives y equals 1 What makes the second parentheses zero?

Answer: the center is (-9, 1) open parenthesis negative 9, 1 close parenthesis .

4. Expanding a squared binomial

What you will be able to do

  • Expand (x + a)^2 open parenthesis x plus a close parenthesis squared and (x - a)^2 open parenthesis x minus a close parenthesis squared with the middle term.
  • Combine the constants.
  • Move everything to one side.

What general form needs

To change a circle's equation into general form, the lecture multiplies out the two squared parentheses and collects everything on one side. The skill is squaring a binomial correctly, twice, and then tidying up.

Squaring means writing it twice

(x - 7)^2 open parenthesis x minus 7 close parenthesis squared means (x - 7)(x - 7) open parenthesis x minus 7 close parenthesis open parenthesis x minus 7 close parenthesis . Multiply every term in the first by every term in the second. That is four products.

(x - 7)(x - 7) open parenthesis x minus 7 close parenthesis open parenthesis x minus 7 close parenthesis = x^2 - 7x - 7x + 49 equals x squared minus 7 x minus 7 x plus 49 = x^2 - 14x + 49 equals x squared minus 14 x plus 49

The two middle products always match, so the middle term is double the number in the parentheses. The last term is that number squared, and it is always positive because a negative times a negative is positive.

Why the middle term cannot be skipped. The shortcut "square each piece" gives x^2 + 49 x squared plus 49 and loses the middle term. Check with x = 10 x equals 10 : (10 - 7)^2 = 3^2 = 9 open parenthesis 10 minus 7 close parenthesis squared equals 3 squared equals 9 . The shortcut gives 100 + 49 = 149 100 plus 49 equals 149 . The full expansion gives 100 - 140 + 49 = 9 100 minus 140 plus 49 equals 9 . Only the full expansion matches.

A pattern you can trust

(x + a)^2 = x^2 + 2ax + a^2 open parenthesis x plus a close parenthesis squared equals x squared plus 2 ax plus a squared (x - a)^2 = x^2 - 2ax + a^2 open parenthesis x minus a close parenthesis squared equals x squared minus 2 ax plus a squared

Double the number for the middle term and keep its sign. Square the number for the last term.

Collecting and setting equal to zero

After both squares are expanded, combine the plain numbers. Then subtract whatever is on the right side from both sides, so the right side is 0. General form lists the terms in a set order: x^2 x squared , y^2 y squared , the x term, the y term, then the number.

Collect and set to zero

Write x^2 - 14x + 49 + y^2 + 2y + 1 = 16 x squared minus 14 x plus 49 plus y squared plus 2 y plus 1 equals 16 with 0 on the right side.

  1. x^2 + y^2 - 14x + 2y + 50 = 16 x squared plus y squared minus 14 x plus 2 y plus 50 equals 16 Reorder, and combine 49 + 1 = 50 49 plus 1 equals 50 .
  2. x^2 + y^2 - 14x + 2y + 34 = 0 x squared plus y squared minus 14 x plus 2 y plus 34 equals 0 Subtract 16 from both sides. 50 - 16 = 34 50 minus 16 equals 34 .

Answer: x^2 + y^2 - 14x + 2y + 34 = 0 x squared plus y squared minus 14 x plus 2 y plus 34 equals 0

5. Completing the square

What you will be able to do

  • Find the number that completes a square.
  • Factor the perfect square that results.
  • Add the same amount to both sides.

Running the last section backward

Expanding turns (x + 5)^2 open parenthesis x plus 5 close parenthesis squared into x^2 + 10x + 25 x squared plus 10 x plus 25 . Completing the square goes the other way. You start with only x^2 + 10x x squared plus 10 x and work out which number has to be added so that the result factors into a perfect square. The lecture uses this to turn general form back into standard form.

The recipe

  1. Look at the number in front of x. Take half of it.
  2. Square that half. This is the number to add.
  3. The factored form is (x + half)^2. The half keeps its sign.
x^2 + 10x: half of 10 is 5, and 5^2 = 25 x squared plus 10 x : half of 10 is 5, and 5 squared equals 25 x^2 + 10x + 25 = (x + 5)^2 x squared plus 10 x plus 25 equals open parenthesis x plus 5 close parenthesis squared

With a negative middle number the half is negative, but its square is still positive.

x^2 - 12x: half of -12 is -6, and (-6)^2 = 36 x squared minus 12 x : half of negative 12 is negative 6, and open parenthesis negative 6 close parenthesis squared equals 36 x^2 - 12x + 36 = (x - 6)^2 x squared minus 12 x plus 36 equals open parenthesis x minus 6 close parenthesis squared
Why half, then square. Look at the pattern from the last section: (x + a)^2 = x^2 open parenthesis x plus a close parenthesis squared equals x squared + 2ax + a^2. a to the power 2 . The middle number is 2a, which is double a. So a is half of the middle number. The last number is a^2 a squared , which is that half squared. The recipe just reads the pattern in reverse. You can picture it too: x^2 + 10x x squared plus 10 x is a square of side x with two strips of width 5 attached. The missing corner that would complete a bigger square is 5 by 5, which is 25.

Keep the equation balanced

In an equation you cannot add a number to one side only. Whatever you add on the left to complete the square, you must also add on the right.

One variable, start to finish

Complete the square: x^2 + 10x = 11 x squared plus 10 x equals 11 .

  1. half of 10 is 5, and 5^2 = 25 half of 10 is 5, and 5 squared equals 25 Find the number to add.
  2. x^2 + 10x + 25 = 11 + 25 x squared plus 10 x plus 25 equals 11 plus 25 Add 25 to both sides.
  3. (x + 5)^2 = 36 open parenthesis x plus 5 close parenthesis squared equals 36 Factor the left side. Add on the right.

Answer: (x + 5)^2 = 36 open parenthesis x plus 5 close parenthesis squared equals 36

A circle equation has an x group and a y group. The lecture completes the square on each group and adds both new numbers to the right side. The steps for each group are exactly the ones above.

Finish this one: A negative middle number

The first steps are done for you. You finish it.

  1. half of -12 is -6, and (-6)^2 = 36 half of negative 12 is negative 6, and open parenthesis negative 6 close parenthesis squared equals 36 Find the number to add.
  2. y^2 - 12y + 36 = 13 + 36 y squared minus 12 y plus 36 equals 13 plus 36 Add 36 to both sides.

6. Counting out from a center

What you will be able to do

  • Find the four edge points of a circle.
  • Write the x-values and y-values a circle covers.
  • Check that both intervals are two radii long.

How a circle is drawn

A circle is every point that is the same distance from one center point. That distance is the radius. To sketch a circle by hand, the lecture plots the center and then finds four easy points: straight right, straight left, straight up, and straight down from the center, each one radius away. Then it draws a round curve through them.

Four points from one center

Moving right or left changes only x. Moving up or down changes only y. So with center (h, k) and radius r:

  • right: (h + r, k) open parenthesis h plus r, k close parenthesis
  • left: (h - r, k) open parenthesis h minus r, k close parenthesis
  • up: (h, k + r) open parenthesis h, k plus r close parenthesis
  • down: (h, k - r) open parenthesis h, k minus r close parenthesis

Find the four points

A circle has center (1, -2) open parenthesis 1, negative 2 close parenthesis and radius 3. Find the points straight right, left, up, and down.

  1. right: (1 + 3, -2) = (4, -2) right : open parenthesis 1 plus 3, negative 2 close parenthesis equals open parenthesis 4, negative 2 close parenthesis Add the radius to x.
  2. left: (1 - 3, -2) = (-2, -2) left : open parenthesis 1 minus 3, negative 2 close parenthesis equals open parenthesis negative 2, negative 2 close parenthesis Subtract the radius from x.
  3. up: (1, -2 + 3) = (1, 1) up : open parenthesis 1, negative 2 plus 3 close parenthesis equals open parenthesis 1, 1 close parenthesis Add the radius to y.
  4. down: (1, -2 - 3) = (1, -5) down : open parenthesis 1, negative 2 minus 3 close parenthesis equals open parenthesis 1, negative 5 close parenthesis Subtract the radius from y.

These are the four marked edge points on Graph A.

How far the circle reaches

The lecture also asks for the domain and range of a circle. As in Section 2.1, that means how far it reaches left to right and bottom to top. The left and right points give the x-values. The bottom and top points give the y-values. The edge points are part of the circle, so the intervals use brackets.

Graph A reaches from x = -2 to x = 4: [-2, 4] Graph A reaches from x equals negative 2 to x equals 4 : [ minus 2, 4 ] Graph A reaches from y = -5 to y = 1: [-5, 1] Graph A reaches from y equals negative 5 to y equals 1 : [ minus 5, 1 ]
A quick check. The width of a circle is two radii: one to the left of center and one to the right. On Graph A that is 3 + 3 = 6 3 plus 3 equals 6 , and the interval [-2 negative 2 , 4] is 6 units long. The height is the same. If your two intervals do not have the same length, something was added or subtracted wrong.
