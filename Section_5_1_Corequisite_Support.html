2. Ordered pairs and checking a point

What you will be able to do

  • Read x and y from an ordered pair.
  • Substitute with parentheses and handle negative signs.
  • Decide if a point solves a system.

What an ordered pair says

An ordered pair such as (4, -1) open parenthesis 4, negative 1 close parenthesis is two numbers in a fixed order. The first number is always x. The second is always y. So (4, -1) open parenthesis 4, negative 1 close parenthesis says x = 4 x equals 4 and y = -1 y equals negative 1 . The order matters: (4, -1) open parenthesis 4, negative 1 close parenthesis and (-1, 4) open parenthesis negative 1, 4 close parenthesis are different points.

Checking a point in an equation

To check a point, take each letter out of the equation and put its number in. Use parentheses around every number you put in. Parentheses keep a negative sign attached to its number and remind you that 5y means 5 times y.

4x + 5y = 2 with (3, -2) 4 x plus 5 y equals 2 with open parenthesis 3, negative 2 close parenthesis 4(3) + 5(-2) = 2 4 open parenthesis 3 close parenthesis plus 5 open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals 2 12 - 10 = 2 12 minus 10 equals 2 2 = 2 2 equals 2

The last line is a true statement. Both sides are the same number. That means the point works in the equation. If the last line were something like 7 = 2 7 equals 2 , it would be a false statement and the point would not work.

A system asks for both

A system is two equations taken together. A point is a solution of the system only if it gives a true statement in the first equation and in the second. True in one and false in the other is not a solution.

Why plugging in settles it. An equation in x and y is a claim about two numbers. The equation x + y = 10 x plus y equals 10 is true for 6 and 4 and false for 6 and 5. Putting the numbers in is the only way to find out, and it always gives a clear yes or no. A system simply makes two claims about the same two numbers, so both have to hold.

Check a point with a negative

Is (-1, 4) open parenthesis negative 1, 4 close parenthesis a solution of 2x - 3y = -14 2 x minus 3 y equals negative 14 ?

  1. 2(-1) - 3(4) = -14 2 open parenthesis negative 1 close parenthesis minus 3 open parenthesis 4 close parenthesis equals negative 14 Replace x with -1 negative 1 and y with 4, each in parentheses.
  2. -2 - 12 = -14 negative 2 minus 12 equals negative 14 Multiply first.
  3. -14 = -14 negative 14 equals negative 14 Combine. Both sides match.

True statement. The point works in this equation.

3. Solving an equation for one variable

What you will be able to do

  • Get one variable alone when the answer still contains the other variable.
  • Flip all signs when the variable is negative.
  • Pick the variable that is easiest to isolate.

What "solve for y" means here

With one variable, solving ends with a number. With two variables, you cannot get a number from one equation. Instead, "solve for y" means: get y alone on one side. The other side will still have x in it. That is expected. The answer is an expression such as y = 5x - 3 y equals 5 x minus 3 .

The moves

  1. Move the other variable term away by adding or subtracting it on both sides.
  2. If the variable you want has a negative sign in front, multiply every term on both sides by -1 negative 1 . This flips every sign.
  3. If it has a number in front, divide every term on both sides by that number.
x + 4y = 9 x plus 4 y equals 9 x = 9 - 4y x equals 9 minus 4 y

Here 4y was subtracted from both sides. You may also write the answer as x = -4y + 9 x equals negative 4 y plus 9 . Both are the same expression.

5x - y = 3 5 x minus y equals 3 -y = -5x + 3 negative y equals negative 5 x plus 3 y = 5x - 3 y equals 5 x minus 3

Subtracting 5x left -y negative y . Multiplying everything by -1 negative 1 turned -y negative y into y, -5x negative 5 x into 5x, and 3 into -3 negative 3 .

Which variable to pick

Pick the variable with no number in front of it. Its coefficient is 1, so there is nothing to divide by and no fractions appear. In the pair 3x + 4y = 10 3 x plus 4 y equals 10 and x - 2y = 6 x minus 2 y equals 6 , the easiest target is the x in the second equation.

Why the solved form is the same equation. Try numbers in x + 4y = 9 x plus 4 y equals 9 . If y = 1 y equals 1 , then x must be 5. If y = 2 y equals 2 , then x must be 1. The solved form x = 9 - 4y x equals 9 minus 4 y gives the same results: 9 - 4(1) = 5 9 minus 4 open parenthesis 1 close parenthesis equals 5 and 9 - 4(2) = 1 9 minus 4 open parenthesis 2 close parenthesis equals 1 . Solving for x did not change which pairs work. It only rearranged the same equation so x is easy to read off.

4. Putting an expression in place of a variable

What you will be able to do

  • Replace a variable with an expression in parentheses.
  • Distribute, combine, and solve.
  • Go back for the second value and write an ordered pair.

The idea

Once you know that x equals an expression such as 2y + 1 2 y plus 1 , you can remove x from another equation and write that expression in its place. This is substitution. It is the same move as plugging in a number. The only difference is that what goes in has two terms.

Parentheses are required

Always put the expression in parentheses. In 4x, the 4 multiplies all of x. So it has to multiply all of 2y + 1 2 y plus 1 .

4x + y = 31 with x = 2y + 1 4 x plus y equals 31 with x equals 2 y plus 1 4(2y + 1) + y = 31 4 open parenthesis 2 y plus 1 close parenthesis plus y equals 31 8y + 4 + y = 31 8 y plus 4 plus y equals 31 9y + 4 = 31 9 y plus 4 equals 31 9y = 27 9 y equals 27 y = 3 y equals 3

After the swap the equation has only one variable. Distribute, combine like terms, and solve as usual.

Going back for the other variable

One number is not the whole answer. Put it back into the expression you started with to get the other one. Here x = 2y + 1 = 2(3) + 1 = 7 x equals 2 y plus 1 equals 2 open parenthesis 3 close parenthesis plus 1 equals 7 . Then write the pair with x first: (7, 3).

Why the swap is allowed. If x = 2y + 1 x equals 2 y plus 1 , then x and 2y + 1 2 y plus 1 are two names for the same number. Swapping one name for the other cannot change whether an equation is true. The parentheses matter because 4x means four copies of the whole thing. Without them, 4 * 2y + 1 4 times 2 y plus 1 would only multiply the first term and the equation would no longer say the same thing.

Substitute and simplify

  2. 5x - 6x + 3 5 x minus 6 x plus 3 Distribute -3 negative 3 . Negative 3 times negative 1 is positive 3.
  3. -x + 3 negative x plus 3 Combine 5x and -6x negative 6 x .

-x + 3 negative x plus 3

5. Multiplying a whole equation and adding two equations

What you will be able to do

  • Multiply every term of an equation by a number.
  • Add two equations column by column.
  • Choose a multiplier that creates opposites.

Opposites add to zero

6y and -6y negative 6 y are opposites. They have the same number with different signs. Add them and you get 0. The addition method is built on this: arrange for one variable to show up as opposites, then add so it disappears.

Multiplying a whole equation

You may multiply an equation by any number except 0, as long as you multiply every term on both sides. The right side is the term people forget.

2x - y = 5 multiply by 3 2 x minus y equals 5 multiply by 3 6x - 3y = 15 6 x minus 3 y equals 15

Multiplying by -1 negative 1 flips every sign and changes nothing else.

Adding two equations

Write one equation under the other with x under x, y under y, and the plain numbers under each other. Then add straight down, one column at a time.

6x - 3y = 15 6 x minus 3 y equals 15 x + 3y = 13 x plus 3 y equals 13 7x = 28 7 x equals 28

The y column was -3y + 3y = 0 negative 3 y plus 3 y equals 0 , so y is gone and one variable is left.

Choosing the multiplier

Look for a variable whose two terms already have opposite signs, or one that needs a single multiplication. To turn x into the opposite of 4x, multiply by -4 negative 4 . If neither term goes into the other, use the smallest number both go into. For 3y and 4y that number is 12, so multiply one equation by 4 and the other by 3.

Why these two moves are allowed. Two true facts: 3 + 4 = 7 3 plus 4 equals 7 and 5 + 1 = 6 5 plus 1 equals 6 . Add the left sides and the right sides: 8 + 5 = 13 8 plus 5 equals 13 . Still true. Adding equal amounts to equal amounts keeps them equal. Multiplying works the same way: if two things are equal, three of each are equal. So neither move changes which point solves the system.

Multiply, then add

Multiply 3x + y = 2 3 x plus y equals 2 by -2 negative 2 . Then add the result to 5x + 2y = 3 5 x plus 2 y equals 3 .

  1. -6x - 2y = -4 negative 6 x minus 2 y equals negative 4 Every term times -2 negative 2 , including the right side.
  2. -6x - 2y = -4 and 5x + 2y = 3 negative 6 x minus 2 y equals negative 4 and 5 x plus 2 y equals 3 Line them up. The y terms are now opposites.
  3. -x = -1 negative x equals negative 1 Add each column: -6x + 5x negative 6 x plus 5 x , then -2y + 2y = 0 negative 2 y plus 2 y equals 0 , then -4 + 3 negative 4 plus 3 .

The y is gone. One variable is left: -x = -1 negative x equals negative 1 .

6. True statements, false statements, and what two lines can do

What you will be able to do

  • Tell an always-true statement from an always-false one.
  • Name the three things two lines can do.
  • Compare slopes and spot a multiplied copy of an equation.

Each equation is a line

Every equation in this section graphs as a straight line. A point is on the line exactly when it makes the equation true. So a solution of a system is a point that sits on both lines. Two lines can do only three things.

  • Cross once. One point is on both lines. One solution.
  • Parallel. Same steepness, different position. No point is on both.
  • The same line. One equation is the other in disguise. Every point on the line is on both.

Telling which from the equations

In the form y = mx + b, the number m in front of x is the slope, the steepness. The number b is where the line crosses the y-axis. Different slopes: the lines cross once. Same slope and different b: parallel. Same slope and same b: the same line.

A line can also hide behind a multiplication. Multiply 2x + y = 3 2 x plus y equals 3 by 2 and you get 4x + 2y = 6 4 x plus 2 y equals 6 . Those two equations describe the same line.

When the variables disappear

Sometimes adding two equations removes both variables. What is left is a statement with numbers only.

0 = 0 always true 0 equals 0 always true 0 = 6 always false 0 equals 6 always false

Compare these with x = 3 x equals 3 , which is true for one value only. A statement with no variable is either true no matter what or false no matter what. The lecture tells you what each of those means for the system.

Desmos shows the picture

Type each equation on its own line in the Desmos graphing calculator, exactly as written, such as 2x + y = 7 2 x plus y equals 7 . Click the point where the lines cross and Desmos shows its coordinates. You may need to zoom out to find the crossing.

Why there are only three cases. Parallel lines have the same slope, so for each step to the right they rise by the same amount. The gap between them never changes, which is why they never meet. Lines with different slopes close the gap at a steady rate, so they must meet, and after that they move apart for good. That is why the count of shared points can only be none, one, or all of them.

One line in disguise

Are 2x + y = 3 2 x plus y equals 3 and 4x + 2y = 6 4 x plus 2 y equals 6 the same line?

  1. 2(2x + y = 3) 2 open parenthesis 2 x plus y equals 3 close parenthesis Try multiplying the first equation by 2.
  2. 4x + 2y = 6 4 x plus 2 y equals 6 Every term doubled.
  3. This matches the second equationSo both equations describe one line.

Yes. They are the same line.

7. Turning a sentence into an equation

What you will be able to do

  • Name the two unknowns.
  • Write a fee-plus-rate equation.
  • Write a count equation and a value equation.

Start by naming the unknowns

A word problem in this section has two unknown amounts. Write down what x stands for and what y stands for before anything else. "Let x be the sign-up fee and y be the monthly rate." Every equation after that is a sentence translated with those two letters.

Pattern 1: a one-time amount plus a repeating amount

A gym charges a sign-up fee once and a monthly rate every month. After 5 months the total is $260.

x + 5y = 260 x plus 5 y equals 260

The fee x is paid once, so it has no number in front. The rate y is paid 5 times, so it is 5y. If time is given in years, change it to months first.

Pattern 2: how many, and how much they hold

A shop has bicycles and tricycles. There are 19 in all, with 45 wheels. Let x be the number of bicycles and y the number of tricycles.

x + y = 19 x plus y equals 19 2x + 3y = 45 2 x plus 3 y equals 45

The first equation counts things. The second counts wheels: each bicycle brings 2 and each tricycle brings 3. The number in front of a variable says how much one of that item contributes.

Two unknowns need two equations. One sentence in the problem gives each.

Why the pattern is right. Test the wheel equation with numbers you can picture. 4 bicycles and 1 tricycle have 2(4) + 3(1) = 11 2 open parenthesis 4 close parenthesis plus 3 open parenthesis 1 close parenthesis equals 11 wheels. Count them: 8 and 3. The expression 2x + 3y 2 x plus 3 y does exactly that count for any x and y. If an equation you wrote gives a silly result for simple numbers, the equation needs another look.

A fee and a rate

A storage company charges a deposit and a weekly rent. After 6 weeks the total is $195. Write an equation.

  1. x = deposit, y = weekly rent x equals deposit, y equals weekly rent Name the unknowns.
  2. x is paid once, y is paid 6 timesDecide how many times each is paid.
  3. x + 6y = 195 x plus 6 y equals 195 Add them to get the total.

x + 6y = 195 x plus 6 y equals 195
