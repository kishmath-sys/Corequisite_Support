Section 3.1 studies the graph of a quadratic function, which is a U-shaped curve called a parabola. This page builds the skills they use: naming a, b, and c with their signs, computing the fraction -b/(2a), evaluating a quadratic at a negative number or a fraction, solving a quadratic set equal to zero, and reading a parabola's features from a graph.

2. Naming a, b, and c

What you will be able to do

  • Match a, b, and c to what they multiply.
  • Keep the sign with the number.
  • Use 1, -1 negative 1 , or 0 when a number is not written.

The general form

A quadratic function written as f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c is in general form. The letters a, b, and c stand for three numbers. A number that multiplies a variable is called a coefficient.

  • a is the coefficient of x^2. x to the power 2 .
  • b is the coefficient of x.
  • c is the number standing alone. It is called the constant.

Every formula in Section 3.1 starts by naming these three numbers. If one of them is named wrong, everything after it is wrong, so this small step deserves care.

The sign belongs to the number

The form is written with plus signs. When the function has a minus, the minus is part of the number.

f(x) = 3x^2 - 12x + 7 a = 3, b = -12, c = 7 f open parenthesis x close parenthesis equals 3 x squared minus 12 x plus 7 a equals 3, b equals negative 12, c equals 7 f(x) = 5x^2 + 2x - 9 a = 5, b = 2, c = -9 f open parenthesis x close parenthesis equals 5 x squared plus 2 x minus 9 a equals 5, b equals 2, c equals negative 9

When a number seems to be missing

  • No number in front of x^2 x squared or x. The coefficient is 1. If there is only a minus sign, it is -1 negative 1 . In f(x) = -x^2 + 6x - 4 f open parenthesis x close parenthesis equals negative x squared plus 6 x minus 4 , a = -1 a equals negative 1 .
  • A term is missing entirely. Its coefficient is 0. In f(x) = x^2 - 25 f open parenthesis x close parenthesis equals x squared minus 25 there is no x term, so b = 0 b equals 0 . In f(x) = 2x^2 + 8x f open parenthesis x close parenthesis equals 2 x squared plus 8 x there is no constant, so c = 0 c equals 0 .
  • The terms are out of order. Match each number to what it multiplies, not to its position. In f(x) = 4 + 3x - x^2 f open parenthesis x close parenthesis equals 4 plus 3 x minus x squared , a = -1 a equals negative 1 , b = 3 b equals 3 , and c = 4 c equals 4 .
Why 1, -1 negative 1 , and 0. x^2 x squared means one x^2 x squared , the same way "a dollar" means one dollar. And -x^2 negative x squared means negative one x^2. x to the power 2 . A missing term contributes nothing, and the only number that multiplies to nothing is 0. Writing the function out in full, such as -1x^2 + 6x - 4 negative 1 x squared plus 6 x minus 4 , can make the three numbers easier to see.

What the sign of a tells you

The lecture uses the sign of a to tell which way the parabola opens. The symbols a > 0 a is greater than 0 mean "a is positive." The symbols a < 0 a is less than 0 mean "a is negative." So the only thing you need from a for that question is its sign.

3. Computing -b/(2a)

What you will be able to do

  • Read -b negative b as the opposite of b.
  • Finish the bottom before dividing.
  • Reduce and leave a fraction when needed.

A formula with three places to slip

The lecture finds the x-coordinate of the vertex with the formula x = -b/(2a). It is short, and it hides three common slips: the negative in front, the multiplication on the bottom, and the reducing at the end. Take them one at a time.

The negative in front means "the opposite of b"

Read -b negative b as "the opposite of b." If b is positive, -b negative b is negative. If b is already negative, -b negative b is positive. Put b in parentheses when you substitute so both signs stay visible.

b = 6: -(6) = -6 b equals 6 : minus open parenthesis 6 close parenthesis equals negative 6 b = -12: -(-12) = 12 b equals negative 12 : minus open parenthesis negative 12 close parenthesis equals 12

The bottom is 2 times a

Work out the bottom completely before dividing. Multiply 2 by a, keeping the sign of a.

a = 3: 2(3) = 6 a equals 3 : 2 open parenthesis 3 close parenthesis equals 6 a = -1: 2(-1) = -2 a equals negative 1 : 2 open parenthesis negative 1 close parenthesis equals negative 2

Then divide and reduce

Now you have one number over another. Apply the sign rules for division and reduce the fraction. If it does not come out to a whole number, leave it as a fraction.

All three steps

  1. -(-12) / (2(3)) negative open parenthesis negative 12 close parenthesis over open parenthesis 2 open parenthesis 3 close parenthesis close parenthesis Substitute with parentheses.
  2. 12 / 6 12 over 6 The opposite of -12 negative 12 is 12. The bottom is 6.

A fraction answer

Compute -b/(2a) when a = 2 a equals 2 and b = 6 b equals 6 .

  1. -(6) / (2(2)) negative open parenthesis 6 close parenthesis over open parenthesis 2 open parenthesis 2 close parenthesis close parenthesis Substitute with parentheses.
  2. -6 / 4 negative 6 over 4 The top is -6 negative 6 . The bottom is 4.
  3. -3/2 negative 3 over 2 Reduce by 2. Leave it as a fraction.

Answer: -3/2 negative 3 over 2

Where the formula comes from. A parabola is symmetric: its left half mirrors its right half. The vertex sits on the mirror line. The formula -b/(2a) finds that line. It comes from the quadratic formula, x = (-b negative b ± sqrt(b^2 b squared - 4ac))/(2a). The two solutions are the same distance to the left and right of -b/(2a), so that value is exactly halfway between them.

Typing it into a calculator

If you type the formula on one line, put the bottom in parentheses: -6/(2*2 2 times 2 ). Without them, a calculator reads -6/2*2 negative 6 over 2 times 2 , divides first, and gives -6 negative 6 . This is the same order-of-operations issue as doing the bottom first by hand.

Decimals

One lecture example has decimal coefficients. The steps do not change. To divide by a decimal, you can move the decimal point the same number of places in both numbers: -0 negative 0 .4/0.04 is the same as -40/4 negative 40 over 4 , which is -10 negative 10 .

4. Evaluating with negatives and fractions

What you will be able to do

  • Evaluate a quadratic at a negative input.
  • Square and multiply fractions.
  • Combine fractions and whole numbers.

The second half of the vertex

Once the lecture has the x-coordinate of the vertex, it substitutes that value back into the function to get the y-coordinate. So you need to evaluate a quadratic at whatever number came out of -b/(2a). That number is often negative and sometimes a fraction.

Negative inputs

Use parentheses around the input every time. Do the power first, then the multiplications, then add and subtract.

To add or subtract, every term needs the same bottom, called a common denominator. Rewrite whole numbers as fractions with that bottom.

Why each step works. Squaring means multiplying by itself, and fractions multiply straight across: (3/2)(3/2) = 9/4. You can only add pieces of the same size, which is why the bottoms have to match first. 5. Solving a quadratic set equal to zero

What you will be able to do

  • Factor, including factoring out -1 negative 1 first.
  • Use the square root property and recognize no real solution.
  • Use the quadratic formula when factoring fails.

Why the lecture sets it to zero

A graph crosses the x-axis where its height is 0. So to find the x-intercepts, the lecture sets the function equal to 0 and solves. Section 1.5 covered three ways to solve a quadratic. This section uses all three, so here is each in brief with the details that matter here.

Method 1: factoring

Find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b. Write the factors and set each one equal to zero.

x^2 - 2x - 15 = 0 x squared minus 2 x minus 15 equals 0 (x - 5)(x + 3) = 0 open parenthesis x minus 5 close parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 3 close parenthesis equals 0 x = 5 or x = -3 x equals 5 or x equals negative 3

If the x^2 x squared term is negative, factor out -1 negative 1 first. That changes every sign inside the parentheses. Then factor as usual. The -1 negative 1 out front never equals zero, so it does not give a solution.

-x^2 + 4x + 5 = 0 negative x squared plus 4 x plus 5 equals 0 -(x^2 - 4x - 5) = 0 negative open parenthesis x squared minus 4 x minus 5 close parenthesis equals 0 -(x - 5)(x + 1) = 0 negative open parenthesis x minus 5 close parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 1 close parenthesis equals 0 x = 5 or x = -1 x equals 5 or x equals negative 1

Method 2: the square root property

When the quadratic looks like (x - h)^2 open parenthesis x minus h close parenthesis squared plus or minus a number, get the square alone and take the square root of both sides. Remember the plus-or-minus.

(x - 3)^2 - 4 = 0 open parenthesis x minus 3 close parenthesis squared minus 4 equals 0 (x - 3)^2 = 4 open parenthesis x minus 3 close parenthesis squared equals 4 x - 3 = ±2 x minus 3 equals plus or minus 2 x = 5 or x = 1 x equals 5 or x equals 1

If the square ends up equal to a negative number, stop. No real number squared is negative, so there are no real solutions. On the graph, that means the parabola never reaches the x-axis.

(x + 1)^2 + 9 = 0 open parenthesis x plus 1 close parenthesis squared plus 9 equals 0 (x + 1)^2 = -9 no real solution open parenthesis x plus 1 close parenthesis squared equals negative 9 no real solution

Method 3: the quadratic formula

When factoring does not work, use x = (-b negative b ± sqrt(b^2 b squared - 4ac))/(2a). It always works.

Quadratic formula

Solve x^2 + 4x + 1 = 0 x squared plus 4 x plus 1 equals 0 .

  1. a = 1, b = 4, c = 1 a equals 1, b equals 4, c equals 1 Name the three numbers.
  2. x = (-4 ± sqrt(16 - 4))/2 x equals open parenthesis negative 4 plus or minus the square root of 16 minus 4, end root, close parenthesis over 2 Substitute. b^2 = 16 b squared equals 16 and 4ac = 4.
  3. x = (-4 ± sqrt(12))/2 x equals open parenthesis negative 4 plus or minus the square root of 12 close parenthesis over 2 Subtract under the root.
  4. x = (-4 ± 2sqrt(3))/2 x equals open parenthesis negative 4 plus or minus 2 the square root of 3 close parenthesis over 2 sqrt(12) = sqrt(4 * 3) = 2sqrt(3) the square root of 12 equals the square root of 4 times 3, end root, equals 2 the square root of 3 .
  5. x = -2 ± sqrt(3) x equals negative 2 plus or minus the square root of 3 Divide both terms on top by 2.

Answer: x = -2 + sqrt(3) x equals negative 2 plus the square root of 3 or x = -2 - sqrt(3) x equals negative 2 minus the square root of 3

Which method when. All three methods answer the same question: which inputs give an output of 0? Factoring is fastest when the numbers are friendly. The square root property is fastest when the function is already written with a squared parenthesis. The quadratic formula is the one that never fails. Picking the method is a matter of looking at the form you were given.

6. Reading a parabola

What you will be able to do

  • Find the turning point on a graph.
  • Write the mirror line as x = a x equals a number.
  • Write the range of an upward or downward parabola.

The turning point

Every parabola has one point where it turns around. If the parabola opens upward, that point is the lowest point on the graph. If it opens downward, it is the highest point. The lecture calls this point the vertex.

The mirror line

A parabola is symmetric. If you fold the page along the vertical line through the turning point, the two halves match. On Graph A that line is dashed. Every point on a vertical line has the same x-value, so a vertical line has an equation of the form x = a x equals a number. The dashed line on Graph A is x = 1 x equals 1 . Notice that it is written as an equation, not as a single number, and it uses x, not y.

Why the mirror line is useful. Symmetry gives you free points. On Graph A, the crossings at x = -1 x equals negative 1 and x = 3 x equals 3 are each 2 units from the line x = 1 x equals 1 . The point (0, -3) open parenthesis 0, negative 3 close parenthesis is 1 unit to the left of the line, so there must be a matching point 1 unit to the right, at (2, -3) open parenthesis 2, negative 3 close parenthesis . This is how the lecture sketches a parabola from only a few computed points.

How far up or down the graph reaches

The range is the set of y-values the graph uses. An upward parabola starts at its lowest point and goes up forever. So its y-values run from the lowest height to infinity. On Graph A that is [-4 negative 4 , ∞). The bracket is there because the lowest point is on the graph.

A downward parabola is the reverse. Its y-values run from negative infinity up to the highest height.

On Graph B the highest height is 5, so the range is (-∞, 5]. For both graphs, the domain (the x-values used) is every real number, (-∞, ∞), because a parabola keeps widening to the left and right.
