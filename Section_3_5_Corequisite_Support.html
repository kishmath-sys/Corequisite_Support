1. Start here

What you will be able to do

  • Find the inputs that make a denominator zero and write the allowed inputs as intervals.
  • Factor the top and bottom of a fraction and cancel a common factor.
  • Write vertical and horizontal lines as equations and describe what a graph does near them.
  • Compare two degrees and form a ratio of leading coefficients.

Section 3.5 is about rational functions, which are fractions with a polynomial on top and a polynomial on the bottom. The lecture teaches how to find the domain, the vertical asymptotes, the holes, and the horizontal asymptote. Each of those is a short procedure. This page builds what the procedures rest on: finding where a denominator is zero and writing the result as an interval, factoring and cancelling inside a fraction, writing a vertical or horizontal line as an equation and seeing what "approaches" means, and comparing the degrees and leading coefficients of two polynomials.

Before you begin. The word "asymptote" is probably new, and a new word can make a section feel harder than it is. The algebra underneath is factoring and solving small equations. If those feel rusty, that usually comes from math anxiety or time away from math, not from a lack of ability. Nothing here is graded and nobody sees your answers. Take breaks whenever you want. 2. Where a denominator is zero

What you will be able to do

  • Solve denominator = 0 for linear, factored, and quadratic bottoms.
  • Recognize a denominator that is never zero.
  • Write the allowed inputs with unions.

Top and bottom

The top of a fraction is the numerator. The bottom is the denominator. Division by zero has no answer, so a fraction has no value at any input that makes its denominator zero. Finding those inputs is the first step of nearly every problem in Section 3.5.

Set the bottom equal to zero and solve

Ignore the top. Write "denominator = 0" and solve that equation.

4/(x - 6): x - 6 = 0, so x = 6 4 over open parenthesis x minus 6 close parenthesis : x minus 6 equals 0, so x equals 6

If the denominator is already factored, use the zero-factor property: each factor gives one value.

3x/((x + 2)(x - 5)): x + 2 = 0 or x - 5 = 0, so x = -2 or x = 5 3 x over open parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 2 close parenthesis open parenthesis x minus 5 close parenthesis close parenthesis : x plus 2 equals 0 or x minus 5 equals 0, so x equals negative 2 or x equals 5

If the denominator is a quadratic that is not factored, factor it first.

x^2 - 3x - 4 = 0 x squared minus 3 x minus 4 equals 0 (x - 4)(x + 1) = 0 open parenthesis x minus 4 close parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 1 close parenthesis equals 0 x = 4 or x = -1 x equals 4 or x equals negative 1

A plain x on the bottom

A factor of x by itself is zero when x = 0 x equals 0 . In 7/(x(x + 3) x open parenthesis x plus 3 close parenthesis ) the excluded values are 0 and -3 negative 3 .

A bottom that is never zero

Some denominators cannot be zero. x^2 x squared is never negative, so x^2 + 9 x squared plus 9 is always at least 9. Setting x^2 + 9 = 0 x squared plus 9 equals 0 gives x^2 = -9 x squared equals negative 9 , which has no real solution. Nothing is excluded, and every real number is allowed.

Why the top does not matter here. Only the bottom can break a fraction. A zero on top is harmless: 0/5 = 0 0 over 5 equals 0 . So when the lecture finds the domain, it does not look at the numerator at all. That can feel odd when the numerator is the more complicated part, but it is correct.

Writing the allowed inputs

The allowed inputs are all real numbers except the excluded ones. In interval notation, split the number line at each excluded value and join the pieces with the union symbol. Use parentheses at every excluded value, and put them in order from smallest to largest.

everything except 6: (-∞, 6) ∪ (6, ∞) everything except 6 : open parenthesis negative infinity, 6 close parenthesis union open parenthesis 6, infinity close parenthesis everything except -2 and 5: (-∞, -2) ∪ (-2, 5) ∪ (5, ∞) everything except negative 2 and 5 : open parenthesis negative infinity, negative 2 close parenthesis union open parenthesis negative 2, 5 close parenthesis union open parenthesis 5, infinity close parenthesis nothing excluded: (-∞, ∞) nothing excluded : open parenthesis negative infinity, infinity close parenthesis

From fraction to intervals

Find the inputs allowed in 2x/(x^2 - 3x - 4 x squared minus 3 x minus 4 ) and write them in interval notation.

3. Factoring and cancelling in a fraction

What you will be able to do

  • Factor the top and bottom.
  • Cancel only whole factors, leaving 1 when the top is gone.
  • Separate the zero of a cancelled factor from the zeros that remain.

Why the lecture factors both parts

To find vertical asymptotes and holes, the lecture factors the top and the bottom and looks for a factor that appears in both. A shared factor cancels. Which factors cancel and which remain decides everything, so the cancelling has to be done correctly.

Factor first

Use the same tools as before. The two that come up most are the difference of squares and the trinomial.

x^2 - 16 = (x - 4)(x + 4) x squared minus 16 equals open parenthesis x minus 4 close parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 4 close parenthesis x^2 - 7x + 10 = (x - 2)(x - 5) x squared minus 7 x plus 10 equals open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis open parenthesis x minus 5 close parenthesis

Cancel whole factors only

A factor on top cancels with an identical factor on the bottom, because anything divided by itself is 1.

(x^2 - 25)/(x - 5) = (x - 5)(x + 5)/(x - 5) = x + 5 open parenthesis x squared minus 25 close parenthesis over open parenthesis x minus 5 close parenthesis equals open parenthesis x minus 5 close parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 5 close parenthesis over open parenthesis x minus 5 close parenthesis equals x plus 5

When everything on top cancels, a 1 is left there. The top does not become 0.

(x - 4)/(x^2 - 16) = (x - 4)/((x - 4)(x + 4)) = 1/(x + 4) open parenthesis x minus 4 close parenthesis over open parenthesis x squared minus 16 close parenthesis equals open parenthesis x minus 4 close parenthesis over open parenthesis open parenthesis x minus 4 close parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 4 close parenthesis close parenthesis equals 1 over open parenthesis x plus 4 close parenthesis
Why only factors. Cancelling is dividing top and bottom by the same thing. That is only allowed for something that multiplies the whole top and the whole bottom, which means a factor. In (x + 5 x plus 5 )/x you cannot cancel the x's, because on top the x is added to 5, not multiplied. Test with x = 5 x equals 5 : (5 + 5 5 plus 5 )/5 = 2, but crossing out the x's would leave 5. A term that is added or subtracted can never be cancelled.

What cancelled and what stayed

After cancelling, keep track of two things. The factor that cancelled has a zero, found by setting it equal to 0. The factors that remain on the bottom have zeros too. The lecture attaches a different meaning to each kind, so it helps to list them separately.

Factor, cancel, and sort

Simplify (x - 2 x minus 2 )/(x^2 - 7x + 10 x squared minus 7 x plus 10 ). Then give the zero of the cancelled factor and the zero of the remaining bottom.

  1. (x - 2)/((x - 2)(x - 5)) open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis over open parenthesis open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis open parenthesis x minus 5 close parenthesis close parenthesis Factor the bottom: multiply to 10, add to -7 negative 7 .
  2. 1/(x - 5) 1 over open parenthesis x minus 5 close parenthesis Cancel (x - 2) open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis . A 1 is left on top.
  3. cancelled factor: x - 2 = 0, so x = 2 cancelled factor : x minus 2 equals 0, so x equals 2 The zero of what cancelled.
  4. remaining bottom: x - 5 = 0, so x = 5 remaining bottom : x minus 5 equals 0, so x equals 5 The zero of what stayed.

Simplified form: 1/(x - 5 x minus 5 ). Cancelled at x = 2 x equals 2 . Still zero on the bottom at x = 5 x equals 5 .

When nothing cancels

Often the top and bottom share no factor. Then nothing cancels, the fraction stays as it is, and every zero of the bottom is of the "remaining" kind.

4. Lines as equations, and what "approaches" means

What you will be able to do

  • Write vertical lines as x = a x equals a number and horizontal lines as y = a y equals a number.
  • See why outputs grow near a zero of the denominator.
  • Know what "approaches" means.

Vertical and horizontal lines

An asymptote is a line, and the lecture expects it to be written as an equation. So you need the equations of the two simplest kinds of line.

  • A vertical line runs up and down. Every point on it has the same x-value, so its equation is x = a x equals a number. The line x = 2 x equals 2 passes through 2 on the x-axis.
  • A horizontal line runs left and right. Every point on it has the same y-value, so its equation is y = a y equals a number. The line y = 1 y equals 1 passes through 1 on the y-axis.

Writing only "2" is not an equation of a line. And the letter matters: x = 2 x equals 2 and y = 2 y equals 2 are different lines.

What happens near a zero of the denominator

Take 1/(x - 2 x minus 2 ). At x = 2 x equals 2 the bottom is zero and there is no value. Look at inputs close to 2.

As x gets closer to 2, the bottom gets closer to 0 and the output grows without limit. On the graph, the curve shoots upward beside the line x = 2 x equals 2 and never reaches it. The lecture describes this as "increases without bound."

Why the output blows up. Dividing by a small number gives a large result. 1 divided by one tenth is 10, because ten tenths fit into 1. 1 divided by one hundredth is 100. The smaller the bottom, the larger the answer. Approaching 2 from the left side gives small negative bottoms, such as -0.1 negative 0.1 , and so large negative outputs, such as -10 negative 10 . That is why the left branch of Graph A plunges downward.

5. Comparing degrees and leading coefficients

What you will be able to do

  • Read the degree and leading coefficient of each polynomial.
  • Compare two degrees using the symbols.
  • Form and reduce a ratio of leading coefficients.

Two numbers from the top, two from the bottom

To find a horizontal asymptote, the lecture compares the degree of the numerator with the degree of the denominator, and in one case divides their leading coefficients. So you need to read a degree and a leading coefficient from each polynomial.

  • The degree is the largest power of x.
  • The leading coefficient is the number in front of that largest power.
8x^2 + 1 degree 2, leading coefficient 8 8 x squared plus 1 degree 2, leading coefficient 8 9x degree 1, leading coefficient 97 degree 0

If the terms are out of order, find the largest power first. In 5 - 3x^2 5 minus 3 x squared the degree is 2 and the leading coefficient is -3 negative 3 .

The comparison symbols

The notes call the degree of the top n and the degree of the bottom m, and write three situations with symbols.

  • n < m n is less than m means the top degree is less than the bottom degree.
  • n = m n equals m means the two degrees are equal.
  • n > m n is greater than m means the top degree is greater than the bottom degree.

The symbol always opens toward the larger number. Deciding which situation you are in is a matter of comparing two whole numbers.

A ratio of leading coefficients

When the degrees are equal, the lecture forms a fraction: top leading coefficient over bottom leading coefficient. Reduce it. It may come out to a whole number or a fraction, and it may be negative.

8 over 4 is 8/4 = 2 8 over 4 is 8 over 4 equals 2 3 over 6 is 3/6 = 1/2 3 over 6 is 3 over 6 equals 1 over 2 -5 over 2 is -5/2 negative 5 over 2 is negative 5 over 2

Read and compare

For (3x + 5 3 x plus 5 )/(6x - 1 6 x minus 1 ), find both degrees, say which situation it is, and form the ratio of leading coefficients.

  1. top: degree 1, leading coefficient 33x is 3x^1. 3 x to the power 1 .
  2. bottom: degree 1, leading coefficient 66x is 6x^1. 6 x to the power 1 .
  3. n = m n equals m The degrees are equal.
  4. 3/6 = 1/2 3 over 6 equals 1 over 2 Top leading coefficient over bottom, reduced.

Degrees equal, ratio 1/2.

Why only the leading terms matter. When x is very large, the term with the highest power is so much bigger than the others that the rest barely matter. For x = 1000 x equals 1000 , 8x^2 8 x squared is 8,000,000 while the + 1 beside it is nothing. So for huge x, (8x^2 + 1 8 x squared plus 1 )/(4x^2 - 3 4 x squared minus 3 ) behaves like 8x^2/(4x^2 4 x squared ), and the x^2 x squared cancels to leave 8/4 = 2 8 over 4 equals 2 . If the bottom has the higher power, the bottom wins and the fraction shrinks toward 0, like 1/x in the last section. If the top has the higher power, the top wins and the fraction keeps growing.
