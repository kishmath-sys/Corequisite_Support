2. What makes a formula fail

What you will be able to do

  • Name the two operations that can fail.
  • Test whether an input works.
  • Remember that zero is allowed under a root but not on the bottom.

Most formulas accept any number

Take a formula like x^2 + 3x - 1 x squared plus 3 x minus 1 . Pick any number at all, substitute it, and you get an answer. Nothing can go wrong, because adding, subtracting, and multiplying always work. The lecture calls a formula like this a polynomial.

In this course, exactly two operations can fail. A formula is safe unless it contains one of them.

Problem 1: dividing by zero

A fraction is a division. Division by zero has no answer. So a formula with x on the bottom of a fraction fails for any input that makes the bottom equal to zero. The bottom of a fraction is called the denominator.

6/(x - 2) with x = 5: 6/(3) = 2 6 over open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis with x equals 5 : 6 over open parenthesis 3 close parenthesis equals 2 6/(x - 2) with x = 2: 6/(0), undefined 6 over open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis with x equals 2 : 6 over open parenthesis 0 close parenthesis, undefined

Only the bottom matters. A zero on top is fine: 0/3 = 0 0 over 3 equals 0 .

Problem 2: the square root of a negative

No real number times itself is negative. So a formula with x under a square root fails for any input that makes the inside of the root negative. The expression under the root is called the radicand.

sqrt(x - 5) with x = 9: sqrt(4) = 2 the square root of x minus 5, end root, with x equals 9 : the square root of 4 equals 2 sqrt(x - 5) with x = 5: sqrt(0) = 0 the square root of x minus 5, end root, with x equals 5 : the square root of 0 equals 0 sqrt(x - 5) with x = 1: sqrt(-4), not a real number the square root of x minus 5, end root, with x equals 1 : the square root of negative 4, end root,, not a real number

Look at the middle line. Zero under a square root is allowed, because 0 * 0 = 0 0 times 0 equals 0 . Zero is a problem on the bottom of a fraction. It is not a problem under a root. Mixing up those two is very common.

Why these two fail. Division asks "what times the bottom gives the top?" With 6/0 the question is "what times 0 gives 6?" Nothing does. A square root asks "what times itself gives this?" A positive times a positive is positive. A negative times a negative is positive. So nothing times itself gives a negative. In both cases the question has no answer among the real numbers, and so the formula has no output for that input.

Testing an input

To see whether an input works, substitute it and look only at the risky spot: the bottom of the fraction or the inside of the root.

3. Finding the inputs that make an expression zero

What you will be able to do

  • Solve a linear expression set equal to zero.
  • Factor x^2 x squared + bx + c.
  • Use the zero-factor property and get the signs right.

Why you need this

To find where a fraction fails, the lecture sets the bottom equal to zero and solves. So you need to solve equations of the form "expression = 0." There are two kinds in this section.

Kind 1: a linear expression

If x appears only to the first power, undo what was done to it. Move the number, then divide.

3x + 12 = 0 3 x plus 12 equals 0 3x = -12 3 x equals negative 12 x = -4 x equals negative 4

The answer may be a fraction. That is fine. Leave it as a fraction.

2x - 5 = 0 2 x minus 5 equals 0 2x = 5 2 x equals 5 x = 5/2 x equals 5 over 2

Kind 2: a quadratic expression

If the expression has an x^2 x squared , factor it and use the zero-factor property from Section 1.5: if two things multiply to zero, one of them must be zero.

To factor x^2 x squared + bx + c, look for two numbers that multiply to c and add to b. Those two numbers go into the parentheses.

Factor and solve

Solve x^2 + 9x + 20 = 0 x squared plus 9 x plus 20 equals 0 .

  1. Two numbers that multiply to 20 and add to 9Try pairs for 20: 1 and 20, 2 and 10, 4 and 5. The pair 4 and 5 adds to 9.
  2. (x + 4)(x + 5) = 0 open parenthesis x plus 4 close parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 5 close parenthesis equals 0 Write the factors.
  3. x + 4 = 0 or x + 5 = 0 x plus 4 equals 0 or x plus 5 equals 0 Zero-factor property.
  4. x = -4 or x = -5 x equals negative 4 or x equals negative 5 Solve each small equation.

Answer: x = -4 x equals negative 4 and x = -5 x equals negative 5 . Notice the signs are opposite to the ones in the factors.

Why factoring solves the equation. Zero is the only number with this property. If a product is 12, the factors could be 3 and 4, or 2 and 6, or many others, so you learn nothing. If a product is 0, one factor has to be 0. That is why the equation must have 0 on one side before you factor. You can check a factoring by multiplying it back out: (x + 4)(x + 5) = x^2 + 5x + 4x + 20 = x^2 + 9x + 20 open parenthesis x plus 4 close parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 5 close parenthesis equals x squared plus 5 x plus 4 x plus 20 equals x squared plus 9 x plus 20 .

The pair 5 and -2 negative 2 works: 5 * (-2) = -10 5 times open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals negative 10 and 5 + (-2) = 3 5 plus open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals 3 . So the factors are (x + 5)(x - 2) open parenthesis x plus 5 close parenthesis open parenthesis x minus 2 close parenthesis , and the solutions are x = -5 x equals negative 5 and x = 2 x equals 2 .

4. Inequalities and intervals with gaps

What you will be able to do

  • Solve a linear inequality and turn the symbol when needed.
  • Write an inequality as an interval.
  • Write "all real numbers except" with unions.

Solving a linear inequality

For a square root, the lecture needs the inside to be zero or more. That is an inequality: inside ≥ 0. You solve it the same way you solve an equation, with one extra rule.

2x + 10 ≥ 0 2 x plus 10 is greater than or equal to 0 2x ≥ -10 2 x is greater than or equal to negative 10 x ≥ -5 x is greater than or equal to negative 5

The extra rule: when you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, the inequality symbol turns around.

6 - 3x ≥ 0 6 minus 3 x is greater than or equal to 0 -3x ≥ -6 (subtract 6) negative 3 x is greater than or equal to negative 6 open parenthesis subtract 6 close parenthesis x ≤ 2 (divide by -3 and turn the symbol) x is less than or equal to 2 open parenthesis divide by negative 3 and turn the symbol close parenthesis
Why the symbol turns. Start with a true statement: 2 < 5 2 is less than 5 . Multiply both sides by -1 negative 1 and you get -2 negative 2 and -5 negative 5 . On the number line -2 negative 2 is to the right of -5 negative 5 , so now -2 > -5 negative 2 is greater than negative 5 . Multiplying by a negative flips the number line around zero, so "smaller" and "larger" trade places. You can always check your answer with a test number. For x ≤ 2 x is less than or equal to 2 , try x = 0 x equals 0 in the original: 6 - 3(0) = 6 6 minus 3 open parenthesis 0 close parenthesis equals 6 , and 6 ≥ 0 6 is greater than or equal to 0 is true.

Interval notation, reviewed

Section 1.7 introduced interval notation. A bracket means the end value is included. A parenthesis means it is not. Infinity always gets a parenthesis, because it is not a number you can reach.

Leaving a number out

For a fraction, the lecture needs every real number except the ones that make the bottom zero. To write "everything except 4," describe the number line in two pieces: everything to the left of 4, and everything to the right of 4. Join them with the union symbol ∪, which you can type as a capital U.

all real numbers except 4: (-∞, 4) ∪ (4, ∞) all real numbers except 4 : open parenthesis negative infinity, 4 close parenthesis union open parenthesis 4, infinity close parenthesis

The 4 gets parentheses on both sides because it is left out. With two numbers left out, there are three pieces. Always put the smaller number first.

all real numbers except -5 and 1: (-∞, -5) ∪ (-5, 1) ∪ (1, ∞) all real numbers except negative 5 and 1 : open parenthesis negative infinity, negative 5 close parenthesis union open parenthesis negative 5, 1 close parenthesis union open parenthesis 1, infinity close parenthesis

5. Adding, subtracting, and multiplying polynomials

What you will be able to do

  • Add and subtract polynomials with parentheses.
  • Multiply binomials and longer polynomials.
  • Cancel a common factor in a fraction.

Putting two formulas together

The middle of Section 2.6 takes two functions and adds them, subtracts them, multiplies them, or divides them. Once the formulas are written side by side, the rest is polynomial arithmetic. Always put each formula in its own parentheses first. That one habit prevents most of the sign errors.

Adding

Drop the parentheses and combine like terms. Like terms have the same letter with the same power.

(3x + 4) + (5x - 9) = 3x + 4 + 5x - 9 = 8x - 5 open parenthesis 3 x plus 4 close parenthesis plus open parenthesis 5 x minus 9 close parenthesis equals 3 x plus 4 plus 5 x minus 9 equals 8 x minus 5

Subtracting

The minus sign applies to every term in the second parentheses. Each of those terms changes sign. Then combine like terms. The notes call forgetting this "the most common mistake."

(3x + 4) - (5x - 9) = 3x + 4 - 5x + 9 = -2x + 13 open parenthesis 3 x plus 4 close parenthesis minus open parenthesis 5 x minus 9 close parenthesis equals 3 x plus 4 minus 5 x plus 9 equals negative 2 x plus 13

Multiplying two binomials

A binomial has two terms. To multiply two binomials, every term in the first multiplies every term in the second. That is four products. Many people remember them as FOIL: First, Outer, Inner, Last.

(3x + 1)(2x - 5) open parenthesis 3 x plus 1 close parenthesis open parenthesis 2 x minus 5 close parenthesis = 3x * 2x + 3x * (-5) + 1 * 2x + 1 * (-5) equals 3 x times 2 x plus 3 x times open parenthesis negative 5 close parenthesis plus 1 times 2 x plus 1 times open parenthesis negative 5 close parenthesis = 6x^2 - 15x + 2x - 5 equals 6 x squared minus 15 x plus 2 x minus 5 = 6x^2 - 13x - 5 equals 6 x squared minus 13 x minus 5
Why four products. Multiplying is repeated distributing. First treat (2x - 5) open parenthesis 2 x minus 5 close parenthesis as a single block and distribute it: 3x(2x - 5) + 1(2x - 5) 3 x open parenthesis 2 x minus 5 close parenthesis plus 1 open parenthesis 2 x minus 5 close parenthesis . Then distribute again inside each part. Four products come out, the same four as FOIL. You can check with a number. Let x = 1 x equals 1 : (3 + 1)(2 - 5) = 4 * (-3) = -12 open parenthesis 3 plus 1 close parenthesis open parenthesis 2 minus 5 close parenthesis equals 4 times open parenthesis negative 3 close parenthesis equals negative 12 . And 6 - 13 - 5 = -12 6 minus 13 minus 5 equals negative 12 .

6. Substituting one expression into another

What you will be able to do

  • Use empty parentheses to substitute an expression.
  • Work from the inside out with numbers.
  • Square an expression that contains x^2. x to the power 2 .

The empty-parentheses method

The last part of Section 2.6 puts one formula inside another. The notes do it in a careful way that is worth copying. Take the outer formula and erase every x, leaving empty parentheses. Then write the same thing in every empty space.

3x + 4 becomes 3( ) + 4fill with 5x - 2: 3(5x - 2) + 4 fill with 5 x minus 2 : 3 open parenthesis 5 x minus 2 close parenthesis plus 4

Then simplify. Distribute, and combine like terms.

3(5x - 2) + 4 = 15x - 6 + 4 = 15x - 2 3 open parenthesis 5 x minus 2 close parenthesis plus 4 equals 15 x minus 6 plus 4 equals 15 x minus 2
Why the parentheses matter. A formula is a set of instructions for an input: "multiply by 3, then add 4." Those instructions do not care what the input is. It can be a number, a letter, or a whole expression. The parentheses make sure the instructions act on the entire input. Without them, 3 * 5x - 2 + 4 3 times 5 x minus 2 plus 4 would multiply only the 5x, which is a different calculation.

With numbers: work from the inside out

Sometimes the inner thing is a formula with a number already in it. Work out the inner value first. Then feed that result into the outer formula. Suppose one rule is "add 5" and the other is "double." To double the result of adding 5 to 3: first 3 + 5 = 8 3 plus 5 equals 8 , then 2 * 8 = 16 2 times 8 equals 16 .

Order matters

Reverse the two rules and the answer changes. Double 3 first to get 6, then add 5 to get 11. The lecture makes a point of this: putting the first function into the second is generally not the same as putting the second into the first.

When the outer formula has a square

If the outer formula squares its input, the whole inner expression is squared. Write it twice and multiply all four products.
