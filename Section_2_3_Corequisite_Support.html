2. Subtracting signed numbers

What you will be able to do

  • Rewrite a subtraction as adding the opposite.
  • Subtract a negative correctly.
  • Keep parentheses around a negative value when substituting.

Why this comes first

The slope formula subtracts two y-values and subtracts two x-values. The points in this section often have negative coordinates, so nearly every problem asks you to subtract with negatives. If this one skill is solid, the slope formula is easy.

Subtracting is adding the opposite

Every subtraction can be rewritten as an addition. Keep the first number, change the minus to a plus, and change the second number to its opposite. The opposite of 5 is -5 negative 5 . The opposite of -5 negative 5 is 5.

3 - 9 = 3 + (-9) = -6 3 minus 9 equals 3 plus open parenthesis negative 9 close parenthesis equals negative 6 -2 - 5 = -2 + (-5) = -7 negative 2 minus 5 equals negative 2 plus open parenthesis negative 5 close parenthesis equals negative 7 4 - (-3) = 4 + 3 = 7 4 minus open parenthesis negative 3 close parenthesis equals 4 plus 3 equals 7 -1 - (-6) = -1 + 6 = 5 negative 1 minus open parenthesis negative 6 close parenthesis equals negative 1 plus 6 equals 5

The third and fourth lines show the case that causes the most trouble: subtracting a negative. Two signs next to each other, "minus a negative," turn into a plus.

Why minus a negative is a plus. Think of a number line. Subtracting 3 means stepping 3 to the left. Subtracting -3 negative 3 must do the reverse of that, so you step 3 to the right. Or think about money. A negative number is a debt. Taking away a debt of 3 dollars leaves you 3 dollars better off. So 4 - (-3) 4 minus open parenthesis negative 3 close parenthesis is 7.

Adding numbers with different signs

Once the problem is an addition, compare the sizes. If the signs match, add the sizes and keep the sign. If the signs differ, subtract the smaller size from the larger and keep the sign of the larger. In -1 + 6 negative 1 plus 6 the 6 is larger and positive, so the answer is positive 5. In 3 + (-9) 3 plus open parenthesis negative 9 close parenthesis the 9 is larger and negative, so the answer is -6 negative 6 .

Use parentheses when you substitute

When a formula says y2 - y1 y 2 minus y 1 and the value of y1 is -2 negative 2 , write the -2 negative 2 in parentheses: 6 - (-2) 6 minus open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis . This keeps both signs visible. Dropping the parentheses and writing 6 - 2 6 minus 2 is the most common slope error there is.

3. Fractions: reducing, signs, and zero

What you will be able to do

  • Reduce a fraction and leave it improper.
  • Move a negative sign correctly.
  • Tell 0 on top from 0 on the bottom.

A slope is a fraction

Slope is written as a fraction: one number over another. So after the subtracting is done, you need to tidy up a fraction. Three things come up: reducing, where the negative sign goes, and what to do with a zero.

Reducing

To reduce a fraction, divide the top and the bottom by the same number. Keep going until no number other than 1 divides both. MyMathLab expects reduced fractions.

6/8 = 3/4 (divide top and bottom by 2) 6 over 8 equals 3 over 4 open parenthesis divide top and bottom by 2 close parenthesis 10/4 = 5/2 (divide top and bottom by 2) 10 over 4 equals 5 over 2 open parenthesis divide top and bottom by 2 close parenthesis 12/3 = 4 (the bottom divides the top evenly) 12 over 3 equals 4 open parenthesis the bottom divides the top evenly close parenthesis

Leave an answer like 5/2 as it is. It is called an improper fraction and it is the preferred form for a slope. Do not change it to a mixed number or a decimal.

Where the negative sign goes

A fraction is a division, and division follows the usual sign rules. One negative makes the fraction negative. Two negatives make it positive.

(-3)/4 = 3/(-4) = -3/4 open parenthesis negative 3 close parenthesis over 4 equals 3 over open parenthesis negative 4 close parenthesis equals negative 3 over 4 (-3)/(-4) = 3/4 open parenthesis negative 3 close parenthesis over open parenthesis negative 4 close parenthesis equals 3 over 4

So a negative fraction has one negative sign, and it can sit on the top, on the bottom, or out front. All three mean the same number. It cannot be on the top and the bottom at once, because then the fraction would be positive. Write your final answer with the sign out front or on top.

Zero on top, zero on the bottom

These two look alike and mean opposite things.

  • Zero on top: 0/6 = 0 0 over 6 equals 0 . Zero divided by anything (except zero) is zero.
  • Zero on the bottom: 6/0 is undefined. It has no value at all.
Why they are different. Division asks a multiplication question. 12/3 asks "what times 3 is 12?" The answer is 4. Now 0/6 asks "what times 6 is 0?" The answer is 0, which is a perfectly good number. But 6/0 asks "what times 0 is 6?" Nothing works, because anything times 0 is 0. No answer exists, so we say undefined. On a calculator, 6/0 gives an error for the same reason.

Tidy a fraction

Simplify (-8 negative 8 )/(-6 negative 6 ).

  1. 8/6 8 over 6 Two negatives make a positive.
  2. 4/3 4 over 3 Divide top and bottom by 2.

Answer: 4/3

4. Rise and run on a grid

What you will be able to do

  • Count rise and run with signs.
  • Get rise and run by subtracting coordinates in the same order.
  • Move from a point using a rise and a run.

Two directions of movement

To get from one point on a grid to another, you can move in two stages: first straight up or down, then straight left or right.

  • The up-or-down move is the rise. Up is positive. Down is negative.
  • The left-or-right move is the run. Right is positive. Left is negative.

The lecture defines slope as rise over run. The skill underneath is counting those two moves correctly, with the right signs.

Counting, and why subtraction gives the same thing

On Graph A, go from (-4, -1) open parenthesis negative 4, negative 1 close parenthesis to (2, 3). Count up from height -1 negative 1 to height 3: that is 4 units up. Count right from -4 negative 4 to 2: that is 6 units right. So the rise is 4 and the run is 6.

You can get the same numbers without counting. Subtract the y-values: 3 - (-1) = 4 3 minus open parenthesis negative 1 close parenthesis equals 4 . Subtract the x-values in the same order: 2 - (-4) = 6 2 minus open parenthesis negative 4 close parenthesis equals 6 . This is all the slope formula does. It counts the rise and the run using subtraction.

Why any two points work. The steepness of a straight line is the same everywhere on it. So it does not matter which two points you pick, and it does not matter which one you call first. Going the other way on Graph A, from (2, 3) to (-4, -1) open parenthesis negative 4, negative 1 close parenthesis , the rise is -4 negative 4 and the run is -6 negative 6 . The fraction (-4 negative 4 )/(-6 negative 6 ) reduces to 2/3, the same as 4/6. What does matter is using the same order on top and on the bottom.

A falling line

On Graph B, find the rise and the run from (-2, 4) open parenthesis negative 2, 4 close parenthesis to (4, 1). Then write rise over run.

  1. rise = 1 - 4 = -3 rise equals 1 minus 4 equals negative 3 From height 4 down to height 1. Down is negative.
  2. run = 4 - (-2) = 6 run equals 4 minus open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals 6 From x = -2 x equals negative 2 right to x = 4 x equals 4 .
  3. (-3)/6 = -1/2 open parenthesis negative 3 close parenthesis over 6 equals negative 1 over 2 Rise over run, reduced.

Answer: rise -3 negative 3 , run 6, and the fraction is -1/2. A line that falls from left to right always gives a negative fraction.

Moving from a point by a rise and a run

The lecture also graphs lines by starting at one point and using a rise and run to find the next. Start at the point, change y by the rise, and change x by the run. From (0, 1) with rise 2 and run 3: y goes from 1 to 3 and x goes from 0 to 3. You land on (3, 3).

Flat and straight-up lines

A flat (horizontal) line has no rise, so rise over run has 0 on top. A straight up-and-down (vertical) line has no run, so rise over run has 0 on the bottom. The fractions section told you what each of those means.

5. Double negatives and distributing

What you will be able to do

  • Rewrite x - (-5) x minus open parenthesis negative 5 close parenthesis as x + 5 x plus 5 .
  • Distribute whole numbers, negatives, and fractions.
  • Add or subtract on both sides to leave y alone.

The pattern y - y1 = m(x - x1) y minus y 1 equals m open parenthesis x minus x 1 close parenthesis

The lecture introduces a form for the equation of a line that looks like y - (a number) = (slope)(x - (a number)). You do not need to know the form yet. What you need is the algebra used to fill it in and tidy it up. There are three moves.

Move 1: clear a double negative

The form has minus signs built in. When the number going in is negative, you get a minus next to a negative. As with arithmetic, that pair becomes a plus.

x - (-5) = x + 5 x minus open parenthesis negative 5 close parenthesis equals x plus 5 y - (-2) = y + 2 y minus open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals y plus 2

If the number going in is positive, nothing changes: x - 3 x minus 3 stays x - 3 x minus 3 . And subtracting zero does nothing: y - 0 y minus 0 is just y.

Move 2: distribute

A number in front of parentheses multiplies every term inside.

3(x - 4) = 3x - 12 3 open parenthesis x minus 4 close parenthesis equals 3 x minus 12 -4(x + 2) = -4x - 8 negative 4 open parenthesis x plus 2 close parenthesis equals negative 4 x minus 8 -2(x - 5) = -2x + 10 negative 2 open parenthesis x minus 5 close parenthesis equals negative 2 x plus 10

Watch the last line. A negative times a negative is positive, so -2 negative 2 times -5 negative 5 is +10.

Distributing a fraction

When the number in front is a fraction, the first product just attaches the fraction to x. For the second product, multiply the fraction by the number. A quick way: divide the number by the bottom, then multiply by the top.

(2/3)(6) = 6 divided by 3, times 2 = 4 open parenthesis 2 over 3 close parenthesis open parenthesis 6 close parenthesis equals 6 divided by 3, times 2 equals 4 (1/2)(x - 6) = (1/2)x - 3 open parenthesis 1 over 2 close parenthesis open parenthesis x minus 6 close parenthesis equals open parenthesis 1 over 2 close parenthesis x minus 3 (-3/4)(x - 8) = (-3/4)x + 6 open parenthesis negative 3 over 4 close parenthesis open parenthesis x minus 8 close parenthesis equals open parenthesis negative 3 over 4 close parenthesis x plus 6
Why the shortcut works. (2/3)(6) means two thirds of 6. One third of 6 is 2, so two thirds is 4. Dividing by the bottom finds one part. Multiplying by the top takes the right number of parts. If the bottom does not divide the number evenly, multiply across the top and leave a fraction: (2/3)(5) = 10/3.

Move 3: get y alone

After distributing, y usually has a number being added or subtracted beside it. Undo it on both sides.

All three moves

Rewrite y - (-2) = (1/2)(x - 6) y minus open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals open parenthesis 1 over 2 close parenthesis open parenthesis x minus 6 close parenthesis so that y is alone.

  1. y + 2 = (1/2)(x - 6) y plus 2 equals open parenthesis 1 over 2 close parenthesis open parenthesis x minus 6 close parenthesis Clear the double negative on the left.
  2. y + 2 = (1/2)x - 3 y plus 2 equals open parenthesis 1 over 2 close parenthesis x minus 3 Distribute. Half of 6 is 3.
  3. y = (1/2)x - 5 y equals open parenthesis 1 over 2 close parenthesis x minus 5 Subtract 2 from both sides. -3 - 2 = -5 negative 3 minus 2 equals negative 5 .

Answer: y = (1/2)x - 5 y equals open parenthesis 1 over 2 close parenthesis x minus 5

6. Solving for y when y has a number in front

What you will be able to do

  • Move terms across the equals sign.
  • Divide every term by the number in front of y.
  • Find where a line crosses each axis by substituting 0.

One more step than before

The lecture rewrites equations such as 6x - 3y - 12 = 0 6 x minus 3 y minus 12 equals 0 so that y is alone. The new feature is the number attached to y. Getting y alone takes two stages: first move every other term to the other side, then divide by the number in front of y.

Stage 1: move the other terms

Add or subtract to move each term that does not contain y. A term changes sign when it crosses the equals sign, because you are adding its opposite to both sides.

6x - 3y - 12 = 0 6 x minus 3 y minus 12 equals 0 -3y = -6x + 12 (subtract 6x and add 12 on both sides) negative 3 y equals negative 6 x plus 12 open parenthesis subtract 6 x and add 12 on both sides close parenthesis

Stage 2: divide every term

Now divide both sides by the number in front of y, sign included. On the right side, every term is divided.

-3y/(-3) = (-6x)/(-3) + 12/(-3) negative 3 y over open parenthesis negative 3 close parenthesis equals open parenthesis negative 6 x close parenthesis over open parenthesis negative 3 close parenthesis plus 12 over open parenthesis negative 3 close parenthesis y = 2x - 4 y equals 2 x minus 4
Why every term is divided. An equation is a balance. Dividing the whole left side by -3 negative 3 keeps the balance only if the whole right side is divided by -3 negative 3 . The right side is a sum of two terms, and dividing a sum means dividing each part. Check with a point: if x = 3 x equals 3 , the original equation says 18 - 3y - 12 = 0 18 minus 3 y minus 12 equals 0 , so y = 2 y equals 2 . The new equation says y = 2(3) - 4 = 2 y equals 2 open parenthesis 3 close parenthesis minus 4 equals 2 . They agree.

Dividing by a negative flips the sign of every term. That is the step where signs go wrong most often, so slow down there.

When there is no x

An equation like 5y + 10 = 0 5 y plus 10 equals 0 has no x at all. Solve it the same way: 5y = -10 5 y equals negative 10 , so y = -2 y equals negative 2 . The result says the height is always -2 negative 2 , which is a flat line.

Finding where a line crosses each axis

Every point on the y-axis has x = 0 x equals 0 , and every point on the x-axis has y = 0 y equals 0 . So to find where a line crosses the y-axis, replace x with 0 and solve for y. To find where it crosses the x-axis, replace y with 0 and solve for x. A term with 0 in it disappears, which makes these quick.

3x + 4y = 12 with x = 0: 4y = 12, so y = 3 3 x plus 4 y equals 12 with x equals 0 : 4 y equals 12, so y equals 3 3x + 4y = 12 with y = 0: 3x = 12, so x = 4 3 x plus 4 y equals 12 with y equals 0 : 3 x equals 12, so x equals 4
