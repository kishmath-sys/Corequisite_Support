2. Square roots and simplifying radicals

What you will be able to do

  • Name the perfect squares up to 144.
  • Explain why a perfect square factor can come out of a radical.
  • Simplify a square root.

Squares and square roots

To square a number, multiply it by itself: 7 squared is 7 times 7 = 49 7 equals 49 . The square root goes backward. It asks, "what number, multiplied by itself, gives this?" The square root of 49 is 7. On a keyboard we write that as sqrt(49) = 7 the square root of 49 equals 7 . On paper it is drawn with the radical sign, and the number under the sign is called the radicand.

Numbers that come from squaring a whole number are called perfect squares. Their square roots are whole numbers. It is worth knowing the first twelve by sight.

When the number is not a perfect square

Most numbers are not perfect squares. The square root of 108 is not a whole number. It is a decimal that goes on forever, about 10.39. In this course you do not round it. You write it in simplified radical form, which is exact.

The tool is the product rule for square roots: the square root of a product is the product of the square roots.

sqrt(a * b) = sqrt(a) * sqrt(b) the square root of a times b, end root, equals the square root of a times the square root of b
Why this works. Test it with numbers you know. sqrt(4 * 9) the square root of 4 times 9, end root, is sqrt(36) the square root of 36 , which is 6. And sqrt(4) the square root of 4 times sqrt(9) the square root of 9 is 2 times 3, which is also 6. The rule lets you pull a perfect square out of a radical and replace it with its whole-number root.

The method

  1. Find the largest perfect square that divides the number evenly.
  2. Write the number as that perfect square times what is left.
  3. Take the square root of the perfect square and write it in front. The leftover stays under the radical.

Simplify a radical

Simplify sqrt(108) the square root of 108 .

  1. 108 = 36 * 3 108 equals 36 times 3 Check the perfect squares 4, 9, 16, 25, 36. Three of them divide 108: 4, 9, and 36. Use the largest, 36.
  2. sqrt(36 * 3) = sqrt(36) * sqrt(3) the square root of 36 times 3, end root, equals the square root of 36 times the square root of 3 Split with the product rule.
  3. 6sqrt(3) 6 the square root of 3 The square root of 36 is 6. The 3 stays under the radical.

Answer: 6sqrt(3) 6 the square root of 3

If you pick a smaller perfect square, you are not wrong, just not finished. Using 4 gives 2sqrt(27) 2 the square root of 27 , and 27 still contains the perfect square 9. Simplify again: 2 times 3sqrt(3) = 6sqrt(3) 3 the square root of 3 equals 6 the square root of 3 . Choosing the largest one gets you there in one step.

3. Distributing negatives and combining like terms

What you will be able to do

  • Add two groups by combining like terms.
  • Subtract a group by flipping every sign inside it.
  • Simplify after replacing a symbol with -1 negative 1 .

Like terms, once more

An expression such as 5 - 12x 5 minus 12 x has two terms: the plain number 5 and the x-term -12x negative 12 x . Each term owns the sign in front of it. Like terms have the same variable part. Plain numbers combine with plain numbers, and x-terms combine with x-terms. A plain number and an x-term never combine.

Adding two groups

When two groups in parentheses are added, the parentheses can simply be dropped. Then collect the plain numbers and collect the x-terms.

Add two groups

Write in the form a + bx: (5 - 12x) + (3 - 4x) open parenthesis 5 minus 12 x close parenthesis plus open parenthesis 3 minus 4 x close parenthesis

  1. 5 - 12x + 3 - 4x 5 minus 12 x plus 3 minus 4 x The groups are added, so drop the parentheses.
  2. 5 + 3 = 8 5 plus 3 equals 8 Combine the plain numbers.
  3. -12x - 4x = -16x negative 12 x minus 4 x equals negative 16 x Combine the x-terms. Both are negative, so the sizes add and the result is negative.
  4. 8 - 16x 8 minus 16 x Write the plain number first, then the x-term.

Answer: 8 - 16x 8 minus 16 x

Subtracting a group

Subtracting a whole group is different. The minus sign in front of the parentheses applies to every term inside. It works like multiplying the group by -1 negative 1 , so each sign inside flips. After that, combine as before.

Why this works. If you owe a friend "6 dollars minus a 2 dollar discount," you owe 4. Taking that debt away from 10 dollars leaves 6. Check with the rule: 10 - (6 - 2) = 10 - 6 + 2 = 6 10 minus open parenthesis 6 minus 2 close parenthesis equals 10 minus 6 plus 2 equals 6 . The 2 came back as a plus because subtracting a subtraction undoes it.

Subtract a group

Write in the form a + bx: (7 + 2x) - (3 - 5x) open parenthesis 7 plus 2 x close parenthesis minus open parenthesis 3 minus 5 x close parenthesis

  1. 7 + 2x - 3 + 5x 7 plus 2 x minus 3 plus 5 x The first group is unchanged. In the second group, 3 becomes -3 negative 3 and -5x negative 5 x becomes +5x.
  2. 7 - 3 = 4 7 minus 3 equals 4 Combine the plain numbers.
  3. 2x + 5x = 7x 2 x plus 5 x equals 7 x Combine the x-terms.
  4. 4 + 7xPlain number first.

Answer: 4 + 7x 4 plus 7 x

4. Multiplying with variables

What you will be able to do

  • Tell multiplying x-terms from adding them.
  • Distribute one term over a group.
  • Multiply two groups with FOIL.
  • Recognize conjugates and the square of a group.

What x^2 x squared means

A power counts how many times something is multiplied by itself. x^2 x squared means x times x. So when two x-terms are multiplied, the numbers multiply and the x's multiply:

(3x)(5x) = 3 * 5 * x * x = 15x^2 open parenthesis 3 x close parenthesis open parenthesis 5 x close parenthesis equals 3 times 5 times x times x equals 15 x squared

Compare that with adding: 3x + 5x = 8x 3 x plus 5 x equals 8 x . Adding like terms keeps the x as it is. Multiplying x by x raises the power. Mixing these two up is one of the most common slips in algebra, so slow down whenever you see x next to x.

One term times a group

To multiply one term by a group, distribute: multiply it by each term inside, signs included.

Distribute a term with x

Multiply: -2x(5 - 7x) negative 2 x open parenthesis 5 minus 7 x close parenthesis

  1. (-2x)(5) + (-2x)(-7x) open parenthesis negative 2 x close parenthesis open parenthesis 5 close parenthesis plus open parenthesis negative 2 x close parenthesis open parenthesis negative 7 x close parenthesis Multiply -2x negative 2 x by each term inside.
  2. -10x + 14x^2 negative 10 x plus 14 x squared Negative times positive is negative. Negative times negative is positive, and x times x is x^2. x to the power 2 .

A group times a group

To multiply two groups, every term in the first must multiply every term in the second. With two terms in each group that makes four products. The word FOIL is a way to remember all four: First terms, Outer terms, Inner terms, Last terms. Then combine any like terms.

Why this works. FOIL is the distributive property used twice. (4 - 3x)(2 + 5x) open parenthesis 4 minus 3 x close parenthesis open parenthesis 2 plus 5 x close parenthesis means 4 times the second group, plus -3x negative 3 x times the second group. Each of those is an ordinary distribution, and together they give the four products.

Multiply two groups

Multiply: (4 - 3x)(2 + 5x) open parenthesis 4 minus 3 x close parenthesis open parenthesis 2 plus 5 x close parenthesis

  1. First: 4 * 2 = 8 First : 4 times 2 equals 8 The first term of each group.

  3. Inner: -3x * 2 = -6x Inner : negative 3 x times 2 equals negative 6 x The two inside terms. Keep the negative.
  4. Last: -3x * 5x = -15x^2 Last : negative 3 x times 5 x equals negative 15 x squared The last term of each group.

Two special patterns

Pattern 1: same terms, opposite signs. Two groups like (6 + 5x) open parenthesis 6 plus 5 x close parenthesis and (6 - 5x) open parenthesis 6 minus 5 x close parenthesis are identical except for the sign in the middle. They are called conjugates. When you FOIL them, the outer and inner products are opposites and cancel, leaving only two terms.

Multiply conjugates

Multiply: (6 + 5x)(6 - 5x) open parenthesis 6 plus 5 x close parenthesis open parenthesis 6 minus 5 x close parenthesis

  1. 36 - 30x + 30x - 25x^2 36 minus 30 x plus 30 x minus 25 x squared First, Outer, Inner, Last.
  2. 36 - 25x^2 36 minus 25 x squared -30x + 30x = 0 negative 30 x plus 30 x equals 0 , so the middle disappears.

Answer: 36 - 25x^2. 36 minus 25 x to the power 2 . Shortcut: (first)^2 - (last)^2.

Pattern 2: squaring a group. (3 + 4x)^2 open parenthesis 3 plus 4 x close parenthesis squared means (3 + 4x)(3 + 4x) open parenthesis 3 plus 4 x close parenthesis open parenthesis 3 plus 4 x close parenthesis . Write it as two groups and FOIL. The outer and inner products are equal this time, so the middle term doubles.

Square a group

Multiply: (3 + 4x)^2 open parenthesis 3 plus 4 x close parenthesis squared

  1. (3 + 4x)(3 + 4x)Squaring means multiplying the group by itself.
  2. 9 + 12x + 12x + 16x^2 9 plus 12 x plus 12 x plus 16 x squared First, Outer, Inner, Last.
  3. 9 + 24x + 16x^2 9 plus 24 x plus 16 x squared Combine 12x + 12x = 24x 12 x plus 12 x equals 24 x .

Answer: 9 + 24x + 16x^2 9 plus 24 x plus 16 x squared

The classic slip. (3 + 4x)^2 open parenthesis 3 plus 4 x close parenthesis squared is not 9 + 16x^2. 9 plus 16 x to the power 2 . Squaring each piece separately loses the middle term. Always write the two groups out.

5. Simplifying and splitting fractions

What you will be able to do

  • Reduce a fraction, including a negative one.
  • Explain why multiplying top and bottom by the same thing keeps the value.
  • Split a two-term numerator over one denominator.

Reducing a fraction

A fraction is reduced, or in lowest terms, when no whole number other than 1 divides both the top and the bottom. To reduce, find a number that divides both and divide each by it. Repeat until nothing more works.

4/34: both divide by 2, giving 2/17 4 over 34 : both divide by 2, giving 2 over 17 15/35: both divide by 5, giving 3/7 15 over 35 : both divide by 5, giving 3 over 7
Why this works. Dividing the top and bottom by the same number is dividing the fraction by 1 in disguise (2/2 = 1 2 over 2 equals 1 ), so the value stays the same. Only the way it is written changes.

A negative sign can sit on the top, on the bottom, or in front, and the value is the same. The standard way to write it is in front: -6/34 negative 6 over 34 reduces to -3/17.

Multiplying the top and bottom by the same thing

The reverse also holds. You may multiply the top and the bottom of a fraction by the same number or expression without changing its value, because that is multiplying by 1. For example, 3/5 = (3 * 2 3 times 2 )/(5 * 2 5 times 2 ) = 6/10. The lecture uses this idea when it divides: it multiplies the top and bottom by a carefully chosen expression so the bottom becomes a plain number.

Splitting a fraction with two terms on top

When the top of a fraction has two terms and the bottom has one, the fraction can be split into two fractions with the same bottom. Then each piece is reduced on its own.

(a + b)/c = a/c + b/c open parenthesis a plus b close parenthesis over c equals a over c plus b over c
Why this works. This is adding fractions run backward. You know 2/7 + 3/7 2 over 7 plus 3 over 7 = (2 + 3 2 plus 3 )/7, because the pieces are the same size. Reading that from right to left splits one fraction into two.

Split and reduce

Write (-4 - 10x negative 4 minus 10 x )/34 as two simplified fractions.

  1. -4/34 - 10x/34 negative 4 over 34 minus 10 x over 34 Each term on top gets its own copy of the bottom. Signs stay with their terms.
  2. -2/17 - (5/17)x negative 2 over 17 minus open parenthesis 5 over 17 close parenthesis x Reduce each fraction by dividing top and bottom by 2.

Answer: -2/17 - (5/17)x negative 2 over 17 minus open parenthesis 5 over 17 close parenthesis x

You can split the top, not the bottom. (a + b a plus b )/c splits. But c/(a + b a plus b ) does not split into c/a + c/b. Only a sum on top can be shared out over a single bottom.
