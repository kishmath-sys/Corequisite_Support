1. Start here

What you will be able to do

  • Check three numbers in an equation with three variables.
  • Multiply and add equations that have three variable terms.
  • Work backward from one value to the other two.
  • Follow the plan: same variable, two different pairs.
  • Write equations for three unknowns from sentences.

Section 5.2 moves from two equations with two variables to three equations with three variables: x, y, and z. The lecture checks whether three numbers work in all three equations, solves a system by eliminating one variable twice, and sets up a word problem with three unknowns. Nothing in the method is new. It is the addition method from Section 5.1 used more than once. What makes it hard is the length and the bookkeeping. This page builds the pieces: checking with three numbers, multiplying and adding longer equations, working backward to the remaining values, keeping the plan straight, and writing the equations from sentences.

Before you begin. These are long problems, and long problems can feel like too much before you start. That feeling usually comes from math anxiety or time away from math, not from a lack of ability. In one of the videos the instructor makes a division slip, gets the answer marked wrong, and calmly finds it. That is what doing this kind of problem looks like for everyone. Nothing here is graded and nobody sees your answers. Take breaks whenever you want. Your progress saves on this device.
How to enter answers. Type an equation with its equals sign, such as 3y + 2z = 9 3 y plus 2 z equals 9 . Type three numbers as an ordered triple with parentheses and commas, such as (4, 3, 2). Spaces do not matter.

2. Ordered triples and checking three numbers

What you will be able to do

  • Read x, y, and z from an ordered triple.
  • Substitute three numbers and handle a subtracted negative.
  • Decide if a triple solves a system.

What an ordered triple says

An ordered triple such as (2, -1, 4) open parenthesis 2, negative 1, 4 close parenthesis is three numbers in a fixed order. The order is alphabetical: x, then y, then z. So (2, -1, 4) open parenthesis 2, negative 1, 4 close parenthesis says x = 2 x equals 2 , y = -1 y equals negative 1 , and z = 4 z equals 4 .

Checking in one equation

Take each letter out and put its number in, with parentheses around each one. Then do the arithmetic on the left and compare with the right.

3x + y - 2z = -3 with (2, -1, 4) 3 x plus y minus 2 z equals negative 3 with open parenthesis 2, negative 1, 4 close parenthesis 3(2) + (-1) - 2(4) = -3 3 open parenthesis 2 close parenthesis plus open parenthesis negative 1 close parenthesis minus 2 open parenthesis 4 close parenthesis equals negative 3 6 - 1 - 8 = -3 6 minus 1 minus 8 equals negative 3 -3 = -3 negative 3 equals negative 3

True statement, so the triple works in this equation. A solution of the system has to give a true statement in all three equations. Two out of three is not a solution.

The sign that causes the most trouble

Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding. When y is negative and the equation has "- 2y" in it, the two negatives make a positive.

x - 2y + z with (1, -2, 3) x minus 2 y plus z with open parenthesis 1, negative 2, 3 close parenthesis (1) - 2(-2) + (3) open parenthesis 1 close parenthesis minus 2 open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis plus open parenthesis 3 close parenthesis 1 + 4 + 38

Let Desmos do the arithmetic

Desmos does not solve these systems from a graph. It is still your calculator. Type the left side with the numbers already in it, such as 6 - 2(-3) - 1 6 minus 2 open parenthesis negative 3 close parenthesis minus 1 , and compare the result with the right side. The instructor does exactly this.

Why checking matters more here. An equation with three variables is one claim about three numbers. The claim x + y + z = 10 x plus y plus z equals 10 is true for 5, 3, 2 and false for 5, 3, 3. A system makes three claims about the same three numbers. Checking is the one sure way to know all three hold, and it is also how you can confirm your own answer at the end of a long problem.

Check a triple with a negative

Is (1, -2, 3) open parenthesis 1, negative 2, 3 close parenthesis a solution of 2x - y - z = 1 2 x minus y minus z equals 1 ?

  1. 2(1) - (-2) - (3) = 1 2 open parenthesis 1 close parenthesis minus open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis minus open parenthesis 3 close parenthesis equals 1 Replace x, y, and z, each in parentheses.
  2. 2 + 2 - 3 = 1 2 plus 2 minus 3 equals 1 Subtracting -2 negative 2 adds 2.
  3. 1 = 1 1 equals 1 Both sides match.

True statement. The triple works in this equation.

3. Multiplying and adding equations with three variables

What you will be able to do

  • Multiply all four terms of an equation.
  • Add two equations in columns.
  • Treat a missing variable as a zero.

Same two moves, one more column

Section 5.1 used two moves: multiply every term of an equation by a number, and add two equations column by column. Here the equations have an x column, a y column, a z column, and the plain numbers. The moves do not change.

x + 2y + z = 5 multiply by -3 x plus 2 y plus z equals 5 multiply by negative 3 -3x - 6y - 3z = -15 negative 3 x minus 6 y minus 3 z equals negative 15

Four terms get multiplied. The one on the right side is still the easiest to forget.

Adding with columns lined up

x + 2y - z = 4 x plus 2 y minus z equals 4 -x + y + 3z = 5 negative x plus y plus 3 z equals 5 3y + 2z = 9 3 y plus 2 z equals 9

The x column was x + (-x) = 0 x plus open parenthesis negative x close parenthesis equals 0 , so x is gone. The result still has two variables. That is normal. Adding two three-variable equations cannot finish the problem. It only gets you down from three variables to two.

A missing variable is a zero

An equation such as x - 4z = 3 x minus 4 z equals 3 has no y term. Think of it as x + 0y - 4z = 3 x plus 0 y minus 4 z equals 3 . Leave the y column empty when you line it up, and the other equation's y term comes straight down.

Choosing the multiplier

Same rule as before. To cancel a variable, its two terms have to be opposites. If one equation has x and the other has 3x, multiply the first by -3 negative 3 .

Why the columns work. Adding equal amounts to equal amounts keeps them equal, no matter how many terms each side has. The column layout is only a way to make sure like terms get combined with like terms: x with x, y with y, z with z, numbers with numbers. It is combining like terms, written vertically.

Multiply, then add

Multiply x + 2y + z = 5 x plus 2 y plus z equals 5 by -3 negative 3 . Then add the result to 3x - y + 2z = 1 3 x minus y plus 2 z equals 1 .

  1. -3x - 6y - 3z = -15 negative 3 x minus 6 y minus 3 z equals negative 15 Every term times -3 negative 3 , including the right side.
  2. -3x - 6y - 3z = -15 and 3x - y + 2z = 1 negative 3 x minus 6 y minus 3 z equals negative 15 and 3 x minus y plus 2 z equals 1 Line them up. The x terms are opposites.
  3. -7y - z = -14 negative 7 y minus z equals negative 14 Add each column: -6y + (-y) negative 6 y plus open parenthesis negative y close parenthesis , then -3z + 2z negative 3 z plus 2 z , then -15 + 1 negative 15 plus 1 .

The x is gone. Two variables are left: -7y - z = -14 negative 7 y minus z equals negative 14 .

4. Working backward to the other values

What you will be able to do

  • Use a two-variable equation to get the second value.
  • Use an original equation to get the third.
  • Write the triple in x, y, z order.

One value unlocks the next

The method produces one value first, often z. That is not the answer yet. The answer is three numbers. You get the other two by going backward through equations you already have.

  1. Put the known value into an equation that has two variables. One unknown is left. Solve it.
  2. Put both known values into one of the original equations. One unknown is left. Solve it.
  3. Write the three values as an ordered triple in the order x, y, z.
z = 2 and 3y - 2z = 5 z equals 2 and 3 y minus 2 z equals 5 3y - 2(2) = 5 3 y minus 2 open parenthesis 2 close parenthesis equals 5 3y - 4 = 5 3 y minus 4 equals 5 3y = 9 3 y equals 9 y = 3 y equals 3 y = 3, z = 2 and x + y + z = 9 y equals 3, z equals 2 and x plus y plus z equals 9 x + 3 + 2 = 9 x plus 3 plus 2 equals 9 x + 5 = 9 x plus 5 equals 9 x = 4 x equals 4

The solution is (4, 3, 2). You found z first and x last, but the triple is still written x, y, z.

Why not go straight to an original equation

If you know only z and put it into an original equation, the result still has x and y in it. One equation with two unknowns cannot be solved. So the first stop is always a two-variable equation.

Why this order works. Each time you substitute a known number, the equation loses a variable. A two-variable equation with one value filled in becomes a one-variable equation, and those you can always solve. A three-variable equation needs two values filled in before it gets that simple. Working backward is just choosing, at each stage, an equation with exactly one unknown left.

From two values to the third

You know y = 2 y equals 2 and z = -3 z equals negative 3 . Use x - 2y + z = 1 x minus 2 y plus z equals 1 to find x.

  1. x - 2(2) + (-3) = 1 x minus 2 open parenthesis 2 close parenthesis plus open parenthesis negative 3 close parenthesis equals 1 Replace y and z, each in parentheses.
  2. x - 4 - 3 = 1 x minus 4 minus 3 equals 1 Multiply. Adding -3 negative 3 subtracts 3.
  3. x - 7 = 1 x minus 7 equals 1 Combine the numbers.
  4. x = 8 x equals 8 Add 7 to both sides.

x = 8 x equals 8

Finish this one: From one value to the next

You know z = -2 z equals negative 2 . Use 2y + 3z = 4 2 y plus 3 z equals 4 to find y.

The first steps are done for you. You finish it.

  1. 2y + 3(-2) = 4 2 y plus 3 open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals 4 Replace z with -2 negative 2 in parentheses.
  2. 2y - 6 = 4 2 y minus 6 equals 4 Multiply.

5. Keeping the plan straight

What you will be able to do

  • State the plan in order.
  • Eliminate the same variable from two different pairs.
  • Recognize when a pair of equations is ready to solve.

The plan in one picture

The hardest part of these problems is not any single step. It is remembering where you are. Here is the whole plan.

  1. Number the original equations (1), (2), (3).
  2. Choose one variable to eliminate.
  3. Eliminate it using one pair of equations. Call the result equation A.
  4. Eliminate the same variable using a different pair. Call the result equation B.
  5. A and B have the same two variables. Solve that pair with the tools of Section 5.1.
  6. Work backward for the remaining values.

Same variable, different pair

The two rules in the middle are the ones that go wrong. If you eliminate x the first time and y the second time, then A has y and z while B has x and z. Together they still have three variables, and you are no closer. And if you use the same pair twice, you get the same equation twice.

Every original equation should be used at least once. If you used (1) and (2) for A, then B must use (3) with either (1) or (2).

Labels save you

Write the labels on your paper: (1), (2), (3), A, B. When you are deep in the arithmetic, a label tells you what an equation is and where it came from. The notes do this.

Why the plan has this shape. Each elimination trades two equations for one new equation with fewer variables. Three variables need to come down to two, and you need two equations in those two variables to solve them. That is why there are exactly two eliminations and why they must remove the same variable. It is the two-variable problem with one extra layer wrapped around it.

Plan the two eliminations

Equations: (1) x + y + z = 6 x plus y plus z equals 6 , (2) 2x - y + z = 3 2 x minus y plus z equals 3 , (3) x + 2y - z = 2 x plus 2 y minus z equals 2 . Plan how to get two equations in x and y.

  1. z and -z z and negative z Equation (3) has -z negative z . Equations (1) and (2) have +z. So z is the easy one to eliminate.
  2. (1) + (3): 2x + 3y = 8 open parenthesis 1 close parenthesis plus open parenthesis 3 close parenthesis : 2 x plus 3 y equals 8 Add the first pair. Call this A.
  3. (2) + (3): 3x + y = 5 open parenthesis 2 close parenthesis plus open parenthesis 3 close parenthesis : 3 x plus y equals 5 Add a different pair. Call this B.
  4. A and B have only x and yThat is a two-variable system.

A is 2x + 3y = 8 2 x plus 3 y equals 8 and B is 3x + y = 5 3 x plus y equals 5 . The tools of Section 5.1 take it from here.

6. Sentences with three unknowns

What you will be able to do

  • Write a count equation and a value equation with three unknowns.
  • Translate a comparison sentence.
  • Move every variable to the left side.

Name three unknowns

A word problem here has three unknown amounts. Write what x, y, and z stand for before anything else. Then each sentence in the problem becomes one equation.

Three kinds of sentence

A total count. "50 items were sold" means the three amounts add to 50.

x + y + z = 50 x plus y plus z equals 50

A total value. If the three items cost $2, $1, and $9 each and the sales were $130, multiply each amount by its price and add.

2x + y + 9z = 130 2 x plus y plus 9 z equals 130

A comparison. "The first two together are 4 times the third" says one side equals a multiple of the other. Put the multiplier with the amount that would otherwise be smaller.

x + y = 4z x plus y equals 4 z

Moving everything to the left

To line up in columns, a system needs every variable on the left and a plain number on the right. Subtract 4z from both sides. The right side becomes 0, and 0 is a perfectly good constant.

x + y = 4z x plus y equals 4 z x + y - 4z = 0 x plus y minus 4 z equals 0
Why the multiplier goes with z. Test the comparison with numbers. Suppose z = 10 z equals 10 and the first two together are 4 times that, so 40. Check x + y = 4z x plus y equals 4 z : 40 = 4(10) 40 equals 4 open parenthesis 10 close parenthesis . True. Now try the reversed version, 4(x + y) = z 4 open parenthesis x plus y close parenthesis equals z : 160 = 10 160 equals 10 . False. A quick test with easy numbers tells you which side the multiplier belongs on.

A comparison sentence

A shop sold x small, y medium, and z large drinks. The number of small and medium drinks together was 8 times the number of large drinks. Write the equation with all variables on the left.

  1. x + ySmall and medium together.
  2. 8z8 times the large.
  3. x + y = 8z x plus y equals 8 z The sentence says the two amounts are equal.
  4. x + y - 8z = 0 x plus y minus 8 z equals 0 Subtract 8z from both sides.

The comparison becomes an equation that ends in = 0.
