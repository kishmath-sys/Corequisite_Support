2. Size and matching positions

What you will be able to do

  • State the size of a matrix.
  • Find the entry in a matching position.
  • Decide whether two matrices can be added, subtracted, or are equal.

Size

The size of a matrix is its number of rows, then its number of columns. The notes also call this the order of the matrix. These three matrices are used on this page.

A = 3−106B = 54−2−6C = 123456

A and B each have 2 rows and 2 columns, so they are 2 × 2. C has 2 rows and 3 columns, so it is 2 × 3. A matrix with the same number of rows and columns is called square. A and B are square. C is not.

Matching positions

Two entries are in matching positions when they have the same row number and the same column number in their own matrices. The notes say "corresponding entries". The 6 in A is in row 2, column 2. The entry of B in the matching position is -6 negative 6 .

Why size comes first

Adding and subtracting work on matching positions. So two matrices can be added or subtracted only when they are the same size. Then every entry has a partner. A and B can be added. A and C cannot, because C has a third column with nothing to pair with.

Equal matrices

Two matrices are equal when they are the same size and every pair of matching entries is equal. The entries may be written differently and still be equal. 3/4 and 0.75 are the same number, so a matrix with 3/4 in one spot equals a matrix with 0.75 in that spot if everything else matches.

Why the shapes have to agree. Picture two seating charts for the same room. You can compare them seat by seat only if the rooms have the same rows and the same seats per row. If one chart has an extra column of seats, those seats have nothing to be compared with. Matrix addition, subtraction, and equality are all seat-by-seat comparisons, which is why the shapes must agree first.

Can they be added?

Decide whether B + C B plus C makes sense.

  1. B is 2 × 22 rows, 2 columns.
  2. C is 2 × 32 rows, 3 columns.
  3. The sizes are differentThe third column of C has no partner in B.

B + C B plus C cannot be done.

3. Signed-number arithmetic, one position at a time

What you will be able to do

  • Add numbers with unlike signs.
  • Subtract a negative.
  • Do both position by position and form an opposite.

The arithmetic is the hard part

Adding two matrices means adding each pair of matching entries. Subtracting means subtracting each pair. The matrix idea is simple. Nearly every wrong answer comes from the signs, so this section is about signed numbers.

Adding with negatives

  • Same signs: add the sizes and keep the sign. -4 + (-6) = -10 negative 4 plus open parenthesis negative 6 close parenthesis equals negative 10 .
  • Different signs: subtract the sizes and keep the sign of the larger one. 3 + (-8) = -5 3 plus open parenthesis negative 8 close parenthesis equals negative 5 .

Subtracting

Subtracting a number is the same as adding its opposite. That one rule covers every case.

7 - (-2) = 7 + 2 = 9 7 minus open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals 7 plus 2 equals 9 -1 - 5 = -1 + (-5) = -6 negative 1 minus 5 equals negative 1 plus open parenthesis negative 5 close parenthesis equals negative 6 -3 - (-3) = -3 + 3 = 0 negative 3 minus open parenthesis negative 3 close parenthesis equals negative 3 plus 3 equals 0

Position by position

A = 3−106B = 54−2−6
A + B, row 1: 3 + 5 = 8 and -1 + 4 = 3 A plus B, row 1 : 3 plus 5 equals 8 and negative 1 plus 4 equals 3 A + B, row 2: 0 + (-2) = -2 and 6 + (-6) = 0 A plus B, row 2 : 0 plus open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals negative 2 and 6 plus open parenthesis negative 6 close parenthesis equals 0

The answer is a matrix of the same size, with each result sitting where its two partners sat. Here A + B A plus B has rows 8, 3 and -2 negative 2 , 0.

Opposites

The opposite of a matrix, written -A negative A , has the opposite of every entry. The notes call it the additive inverse. A matrix plus its opposite gives all zeros, the same way 5 + (-5) = 0 5 plus open parenthesis negative 5 close parenthesis equals 0 . A matrix of all zeros is called a zero matrix. Adding it changes nothing.

Why subtracting a negative adds. Think of positive numbers as money you have and negative numbers as money you owe. Subtracting a debt of 2 means a debt is taken away, which leaves you 2 better off. That is why 7 - (-2) 7 minus open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis is 9. The matrix part adds no new rule. It only says: do that same arithmetic separately in each position, and keep the results in place.

Subtract position by position

Find row 2 of A - B A minus B .

  1. Row 2 of A is 0, 6. Row 2 of B is -2, -6 Row 2 of A is 0, 6 . Row 2 of B is negative 2, negative 6 Pick out the partners.
  2. 0 - (-2) = 2 0 minus open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals 2 Subtracting -2 negative 2 adds 2.
  3. 6 - (-6) = 12 6 minus open parenthesis negative 6 close parenthesis equals 12 Subtracting -6 negative 6 adds 6.

Row 2 of A - B A minus B is 2, 12.

4. Multiplying every entry by a number

What you will be able to do

  • Multiply every entry by a number.
  • Handle a negative multiplier.
  • Do a combination in the right order.

A number in front of a matrix

A plain number written in front of a matrix, as in 2A, multiplies every entry. The notes call this multiplying by a constant. It is the distributive idea again: the number reaches every position.

A = 3−106
2A, row 1: 2(3) = 6 and 2(-1) = -2 2 A, row 1 : 2 open parenthesis 3 close parenthesis equals 6 and 2 open parenthesis negative 1 close parenthesis equals negative 2 2A, row 2: 2(0) = 0 and 2(6) = 12 2 A, row 2 : 2 open parenthesis 0 close parenthesis equals 0 and 2 open parenthesis 6 close parenthesis equals 12

Sign rules for multiplying

  • Positive times negative is negative. 7(-2) = -14 7 open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals negative 14 .
  • Negative times negative is positive. -2(-6) = 12 negative 2 open parenthesis negative 6 close parenthesis equals 12 .
  • Anything times 0 is 0.

A negative number in front

With -3B negative 3 B , every entry is multiplied by -3 negative 3 , so every sign flips and every size triples.

B = 54−2−6
-3B, row 1: -3(5) = -15 and -3(4) = -12 negative 3 B, row 1 : minus 3 open parenthesis 5 close parenthesis equals negative 15 and negative 3 open parenthesis 4 close parenthesis equals negative 12 -3B, row 2: -3(-2) = 6 and -3(-6) = 18 negative 3 B, row 2 : minus 3 open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals 6 and negative 3 open parenthesis negative 6 close parenthesis equals 18

Combinations

An expression such as 2A + B 2 A plus B has two operations. Multiplication comes first, as in ordinary order of operations. Find 2A, then add B position by position.

2A + B, row 1: 6 + 5 = 11 and -2 + 4 = 2 2 A plus B, row 1 : 6 plus 5 equals 11 and negative 2 plus 4 equals 2
Why the number reaches every entry. 2A means A + A A plus A . Add A to itself position by position and each entry gets doubled: 3 + 3 = 6 3 plus 3 equals 6 , -1 + (-1) = -2 negative 1 plus open parenthesis negative 1 close parenthesis equals negative 2 . So multiplying every entry by 2 is not a new rule. It is what repeated addition forces. The same reasoning works for any number in front.

Multiply, then subtract

Find row 2 of 2A - B 2 A minus B .

  1. Row 2 of 2A is 0, 12Double row 2 of A.
  2. Row 2 of B is -2, -6 Row 2 of B is negative 2, negative 6 Read it from B.
  3. 0 - (-2) = 2 0 minus open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals 2 First position.
  4. 12 - (-6) = 18 12 minus open parenthesis negative 6 close parenthesis equals 18 Second position.

Row 2 of 2A - B 2 A minus B is 2, 18.

5. Row times column: multiply in pairs, then add

What you will be able to do

  • Multiply a row by a column.
  • Pick the right row and column for an entry.
  • Predict whether a product exists and its size.

The pattern

Multiplying two matrices is not done position by position. Each entry of the answer comes from one row of the first matrix and one column of the second. Multiply first with first, second with second, and add the products.

row 2, 5 times column 3, 42(3) + 5(4)6 + 20 = 26 6 plus 20 equals 26

That one number is one entry of the answer. The notes describe it as multiplying matching entries of a row and a column and finding their sum.

Which row, which column

The entry in row 1, column 2 of AB uses row 1 of A and column 2 of B. The row number tells you which row of the first matrix. The column number tells you which column of the second.

A = 3−106B = 54−2−6
AB, row 1, column 1: 3(5) + (-1)(-2) = 15 + 2 = 17 AB, row 1, column 1 : 3 open parenthesis 5 close parenthesis plus open parenthesis negative 1 close parenthesis open parenthesis negative 2 close parenthesis equals 15 plus 2 equals 17

When multiplication is possible

A row and a column can be paired only if they have the same number of entries. So the number of columns of the first matrix must equal the number of rows of the second. Write the two sizes side by side.

2 × 3 times 3 × 4

The two inside numbers, 3 and 3, match, so the product exists. The two outside numbers give its size: 2 × 4. If the inside numbers differ, the product cannot be found.

Order matters

With numbers, 3 * 5 3 times 5 and 5 * 3 5 times 3 are the same. With matrices, AB and BA usually are not. They use different rows and columns. This is why problems ask for both.

Why multiply in pairs and add. Picture a shopping trip. A row holds how many of each item you buy: 2 notebooks and 5 pens. A column holds the prices: 3 dollars and 4 dollars. The total cost is 2(3) + 5(4) = 26 2 open parenthesis 3 close parenthesis plus 5 open parenthesis 4 close parenthesis equals 26 . Quantity times price, added up. Matrix multiplication does that same total for every combination of a row from one table and a column from another. It also shows why the counts must match: every quantity needs a price.

One entry of a product

Find the entry in row 2, column 2 of AB.

  1. Row 2 of A is 0, 6The row number picks the row of the first matrix.
  2. Column 2 of B is 4, -6 Column 2 of B is 4, negative 6 The column number picks the column of the second.
  3. 0(4) + 6(-6) 0 open parenthesis 4 close parenthesis plus 6 open parenthesis negative 6 close parenthesis First with first, second with second.
  4. 0 - 36 = -36 0 minus 36 equals negative 36 Add the products.

The entry is -36 negative 36 .

6. Identity, zero, and entering it all in Desmos

What you will be able to do

  • Recognize the zero and identity matrices.
  • Say what each one does.
  • Type matrix expressions into Desmos.

Two special numbers, two special matrices

With numbers, adding 0 changes nothing and multiplying by 1 changes nothing. Matrices have a version of each.

0000is the 2 × 2 zero matrix. The notes call it the additive identity.
I = 1001is the 2 × 2 identity matrix.

The zero matrix is all 0s. Add it to a matrix and nothing changes. The identity matrix, named I, has 1s down the diagonal from top left to bottom right and 0s everywhere else. Multiply a matrix by it and nothing changes. An identity matrix is always square. Notice that the identity is not all 1s.

The Desmos steps

  1. From desmos.com open the Matrix tool.
  2. Click New Matrix, set the rows and columns, and type the entries. Tab moves to the next box. This one is named A.
  3. Click New Matrix again for the second matrix. Desmos names it B.
  4. Click off the matrix. Then type the expression the problem asks for, such as A + B A plus B or 2A + 3B 2 A plus 3 B or AB.
  5. Press Enter before starting the next expression so each one gets its own line.

Type a number in front of a letter for a multiple, such as -4A negative 4 A . Type the two letters next to each other for a product. AB and BA are different expressions, so type the one you are asked for. If the sizes do not fit for an operation, Desmos shows a warning instead of an answer.

Why the identity has 0s in it. Check the identity with the row-times-column pattern. Row 3, -1 negative 1 times the first column of I, which is 1, 0, gives 3(1) + (-1)(0) = 3 3 open parenthesis 1 close parenthesis plus open parenthesis negative 1 close parenthesis open parenthesis 0 close parenthesis equals 3 . Times the second column, 0, 1, it gives 3(0) + (-1)(1) = -1 3 open parenthesis 0 close parenthesis plus open parenthesis negative 1 close parenthesis open parenthesis 1 close parenthesis equals negative 1 . The 1 picks out one entry and the 0 throws the other away, so the row comes back unchanged. A matrix of all 1s would add the entries together instead.
