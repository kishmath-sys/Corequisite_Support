2. Three exponent rules

What you will be able to do

  • Add exponents when multiplying.
  • Subtract exponents when dividing.
  • Multiply exponents for a power of a power.

Why exponents come first

A logarithm is an exponent. So every property of logarithms in this section is an exponent rule wearing different clothes. If the exponent rules are solid, the log rules will feel familiar instead of new.

Multiplying: add the exponents

When the same base is multiplied, add the exponents.

a^2 * a^5 = a^7 a squared times a to the power 5 equals a to the power 7 10^2 * 10^3 = 10^5 10 squared times 10 cubed equals 10 to the power 5

Dividing: subtract the exponents

When the same base is divided, subtract the bottom exponent from the top exponent.

a^6/a^2 = a^4 a to the power 6 over a squared equals a to the power 4 2^7/2^3 = 2^4 2 to the power 7 over 2 cubed equals 2 to the power 4

A power of a power: multiply the exponents

When a power is raised to another power, multiply the exponents.

(a^3)^2 = a^6 open parenthesis a cubed close parenthesis squared equals a to the power 6 (5^2)^3 = 5^6 open parenthesis 5 squared close parenthesis cubed equals 5 to the power 6
Why the rules work. Write the copies out. a^2 * a^5 a squared times a to the power 5 is (a * a) open parenthesis a times a close parenthesis times (a * a * a * a * a) open parenthesis a times a times a times a times a close parenthesis . That is seven copies of a, and 2 + 5 = 7 2 plus 5 equals 7 . For division, a^6/a^2 has six copies on top and two on the bottom. Two pairs cancel and four copies are left, and 6 - 2 = 4 6 minus 2 equals 4 . For a power of a power, (a^3)^2 open parenthesis a cubed close parenthesis squared is a^3 * a^3 a cubed times a cubed , which is 3 + 3 = 6 3 plus 3 equals 6 copies, the same as 3 * 2 3 times 2 . None of these rules needs memorizing if you can picture the copies.

3. Roots as fraction exponents

What you will be able to do

  • Turn a root into a fraction exponent.
  • Decide what the root covers.
  • Turn a 1/2 power back into a square root.

Every root is an exponent

The Power Rule for logs works on exponents. A root does not look like an exponent, so the lecture rewrites it as one first. The rule is: the root number goes on the bottom of a fraction.

  • A square root is the 1/2 power: sqrt(w) = w^(1/2) the square root of w equals w to the power 1 over 2, .
  • A cube root is the 1/3 power: cbrt(w) = w^(1/3) w to the power 1 over 3, .
  • A fifth root is the 1/5 power, and so on.

A plain square root sign has no number written on it, but its root number is 2.

A root over more than one letter

If the root covers a product, the fraction exponent covers the whole product. Use parentheses to show that.

sqrt(mn) = (mn)^(1/2) the square root of mn equals open parenthesis mn close parenthesis to the power 1 over 2,

If the root covers only one letter and another letter sits outside it, only the covered letter gets the fraction exponent.

sqrt(m) * n^3 = m^(1/2) * n^3 the square root of m times n cubed equals m to the power 1 over 2, times n cubed

Going back

It works in reverse. A 1/2 power can be written as a square root: k^(1/2) = sqrt(k) k to the power 1 over 2, equals the square root of k . MyMathLab usually accepts either form.

Why a root is a fraction exponent. Use the first exponent rule. w^(1/2) * w^(1/2) = w^(1/2 + 1/2) = w^1 = w w to the power 1 over 2, times w to the power 1 over 2, equals w to the power 1 over 2 plus 1 over 2, equals w to the power 1 equals w . So w^(1/2) w to the power 1 over 2, is a number that multiplies by itself to give w. That is the meaning of the square root of w. In the same way, three copies of w^(1/3) w to the power 1 over 3, multiply to w, so w^(1/3) w to the power 1 over 3, is the cube root.

Rewrite before using a rule

Write sqrt(a) * c^4 the square root of a times c to the power 4 with exponents only.

  1. sqrt(a) = a^(1/2) the square root of a equals a to the power 1 over 2, The square root covers only the a.
  2. a^(1/2) * c^4 a to the power 1 over 2, times c to the power 4 The c^4 c to the power 4 is outside the root and does not change.

Answer: a^(1/2) * c^4 a to the power 1 over 2, times c to the power 4

4. Reading how an expression is built

What you will be able to do

  • Name the numerator, denominator, and factors.
  • Say what an exponent applies to.
  • Tell a coefficient from an exponent.
  • Find the outermost operation.

Why this matters

To expand a logarithm, the lecture looks inside it and asks one question: what is this, a quotient, a product, or a power? The answer decides which rule to use. So you need to read an expression the way you would read a sentence, and find its parts.

Top and bottom

A fraction bar means division. The part above the bar is the numerator, or top. The part below is the denominator, or bottom. On one line, a fraction is typed with parentheses and a slash: (a^2 a squared c)/w^4 has top a^2 a squared c and bottom w^4. w to the power 4 .

Factors

Letters written side by side are multiplied. Each piece being multiplied is a factor. The top a^2 a squared c has two factors: a^2 a squared and c.

What an exponent applies to

An exponent applies only to what it is attached to.

  • In a^2 a squared c the exponent 2 belongs to a only. The c has no exponent written, which means its exponent is 1.
  • In (ac)^2 the parentheses make the exponent belong to both a and c.

Coefficient or exponent

A coefficient is a number written in front, and it multiplies. An exponent is raised, and it counts copies. 3a means a + a + a a plus a plus a . a^3 a cubed means a * a * a a times a times a . In this section the Power Rule turns an exponent inside a log into a coefficient in front of the log, so it helps to keep the two words straight.

The outermost operation

In (a^2 a squared c)/w^4 the fraction bar splits the whole thing into a top and a bottom. Division is the outermost operation, the last one you would do if you put numbers in. Inside the top there is a product. Inside each factor there may be a power. The lecture peels these layers from the outside in: quotient first, then product, then powers.

Why the lecture works from the outside in. Put numbers in and watch the order. With a = 2 a equals 2 , c = 3 c equals 3 , w = 1 w equals 1 , you would compute 2^2 = 4 2 squared equals 4 first, then 4 * 3 = 12 4 times 3 equals 12 , then divide by 1^4. 1 to the power 4 . Powers come first, products next, the division last. Expanding a log undoes that, so it runs the other way: the division is handled first and the powers last. That is the order the instructor uses in every expanding example.

Take an expression apart

Describe the parts of (p^3 p cubed k)/n^2.

  1. quotientA fraction bar splits everything, so division is outermost.
  2. top: p^3 k top : p cubed k Two factors, p^3 p cubed and k.
  3. bottom: n^2 bottom : n squared One factor.
  4. exponents: p has 3, k has 1, n has 2k has no exponent written, so it is 1.

A quotient. The top is a product of p^3 p cubed and k. The bottom is n^2. n to the power 2 .

5. Seeing the log rules with numbers

What you will be able to do

  • Check the Product, Quotient, and Power Rules with numbers.
  • Know that a sum inside a log does not split.

Logs you can evaluate

From Section 4.2, a logarithm equals an exponent. log(100) log of 100 asks "10 to what power gives 100," so it is 2. log(1000) log of 1000 is 3. log_2(4) log base 2 of 4 is 2 and log_2(32) log base 2 of 32 is 5. With numbers like these you can watch each rule work before the lecture uses it on letters.

Product: the logs add

log(100 * 1000) = log(100000) = 5 log of 100 times 1000, end log, equals log of 100000 equals 5 log(100) + log(1000) = 2 + 3 = 5 log of 100 plus log of 1000 equals 2 plus 3 equals 5

Quotient: the logs subtract

log_2(32/4) = log_2(8) = 3 log base 2 of 32 over 4, end log, equals log base 2 of 8 equals 3 log_2(32) - log_2(4) = 5 - 2 = 3 log base 2 of 32 minus log base 2 of 4 equals 5 minus 2 equals 3

Power: the exponent comes down in front

log_2(4^3) = log_2(64) = 6 log base 2 of 4 cubed, end log, equals log base 2 of 64 equals 6 3 * log_2(4) = 3 * 2 = 6 3 times log base 2 of 4 equals 3 times 2 equals 6

The lecture calls this last move "bringing down the power." Going the other way, a number in front of a log can be moved back up as an exponent.

What the rules do not say

The rules are about products, quotients, and powers inside the log. They say nothing about a sum inside. log(100 + 1000) log of 100 plus 1000, end log, is log(1100) log of 1100 , which is a little more than 3. It is not 2 + 3 2 plus 3 . Also, both logs must have the same base before they can be combined.

Why the rules work. log(100) = 2 log of 100 equals 2 and log(1000) = 3 log of 1000 equals 3 because 100 = 10^2 100 equals 10 squared and 1000 = 10^3. 1000 equals 10 to the power 3 . Multiply them: 10^2 * 10^3 = 10^5. 10 squared times 10 cubed equals 10 to the power 5 . The exponents added. A log is the exponent, so the logs added. The Product Rule is the first exponent rule read from the exponent's point of view. The other two rules match the other two exponent rules the same way.

Check the Quotient Rule

Evaluate log(1000/10) log of 1000 over 10, end log, two ways.

  1. log(1000/10) = log(100) = 2 log of 1000 over 10, end log, equals log of 100 equals 2 Divide first, then take the log.
  2. log(1000) - log(10) = 3 - 1 = 2 log of 1000 minus log of 10 equals 3 minus 1 equals 2 Take each log, then subtract.

Both ways give 2.

6. Numbers and signs in front of a term

What you will be able to do

  • Read the sign of each term.
  • Move a factor with a negative exponent to the bottom.
  • Distribute a number or a minus sign.

Terms and their signs

A term is a piece of an expression separated from the others by plus or minus signs. The sign in front belongs to the term. In 3A - 2B + C 3 A minus 2 B plus C there are three terms: 3A, -2B negative 2 B , and C. The first and last are positive. The middle one is negative.

When the lecture condenses logs, each positive term ends up on top of a fraction and each negative term ends up on the bottom. So the first job is to read the signs correctly.

Negative exponents and the bottom of a fraction

A negative exponent means "one over." So a factor with a negative exponent belongs on the bottom of a fraction, where its exponent becomes positive.

a^-2 = 1/a^2 a to the power negative 2 equals 1 over a squared p * m^-2 = p/m^2 p times m to the power negative 2 equals p over m squared p * m^-2 * n^-3 = p/(m^2 n^3) p times m to the power negative 2 times n to the power negative 3 equals p over open parenthesis m squared n cubed close parenthesis

When more than one factor goes to the bottom, they are multiplied together there. Typed on one line, the bottom needs parentheses.

A number in front of parentheses

A number in front of parentheses multiplies every term inside. This is called distributing.

(1/2)(6a + 4c) = 3a + 2c open parenthesis 1 over 2 close parenthesis open parenthesis 6 a plus 4 c close parenthesis equals 3 a plus 2 c -(a + c) = -a - c negative open parenthesis a plus c close parenthesis equals negative a minus c

A minus sign in front of parentheses is a -1 negative 1 . It changes the sign of every term inside.

Why a minus sign means "bottom." Subtracting is the opposite of adding, and dividing is the opposite of multiplying. Logs turn multiplying into adding. So they turn dividing into subtracting. That is why a term with a minus sign in front of it matches a factor on the bottom of a fraction. Check it with numbers: log(1000) - log(10) = 3 - 1 = 2 log of 1000 minus log of 10 equals 3 minus 1 equals 2 , and log(1000/10) = log(100) = 2. log of 1000 over 10, end log, equals log of 100 equals 2 . The subtracted one went to the bottom.

Sort by sign

In 2A - 5B - C + D 2 A minus 5 B minus C plus D , which terms are positive and which are negative?

  1. 2A and D are positiveNo sign in front of the first term means plus.
  2. 5B and C are negativeEach has a minus sign in front.

Positive: 2A and D. Negative: 5B and C.

7. Dividing two logs in Desmos

What you will be able to do

  • Put the input on top and the base on the bottom.
  • Type a quotient of two logs.
  • Check the size of the answer.

What Desmos has

Desmos has a log button for base 10 and an ln button for base e. You can also type the words log and ln. The change-of-base formula in the lecture rewrites a log with any other base as one log divided by another, so that these two buttons are enough.

Which one goes on top

The formula puts the log of the input on top and the log of the base on the bottom. For log_4(50) log base 4 of 50 , the input is 50 and the base is 4.

log_4(50) = log(50)/log(4) log base 4 of 50 equals log of 50 over log of 4 log_4(50) = ln(50)/ln(4) log base 4 of 50 equals the natural log of 50 over the natural log of 4

Either line works and both give the same number. Use log on top and bottom, or ln on top and bottom. Do not mix them.

Typing a fraction of logs

  1. Type log(50) log of 50 . Close the parenthesis.
  2. Press the slash key. Desmos makes a fraction.
  3. Type log(4) log of 4 in the bottom.
  4. Read the result and round as directed.

Close each parenthesis before the slash. log(50)/log(4) log of 50 over log of 4 is two logs divided. log(50/4) log of 50 over 4, end log, is one log of 12.5. Those are different numbers.

Does the answer make sense

log_4(50) log base 4 of 50 asks "4 to what power gives 50." Since 4^2 = 16 4 squared equals 16 and 4^3 = 64 4 cubed equals 64 , the answer should be between 2 and 3. If Desmos shows something like 0.35, the top and bottom are probably switched.

Why dividing works. log(50) log of 50 is the power of 10 that gives 50. log(4) log of 4 is the power of 10 that gives 4. Dividing them asks how many "4-sized" exponents fit into the "50-sized" exponent, which is exactly the power of 4 that gives 50. You can test the formula on a log you already know: log(1000)/log(10) = 3/1 = 3 log of 1000 over log of 10 equals 3 over 1 equals 3 , and log_10(1000) log base 10 of 1000 is 3.

Estimate, then compute

Evaluate log_2(9) log base 2 of 9 to four decimal places.

  1. between 3 and 42^3 = 8 2 cubed equals 8 and 2^4 = 16 2 to the power 4 equals 16 , and 9 is just past 8.
