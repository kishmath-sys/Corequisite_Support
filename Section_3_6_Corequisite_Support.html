Section 3.6 solves inequalities such as "a quadratic is greater than zero." The lecture teaches a four-step method: find the roots, cut the number line into intervals, test one value in each interval, and write the solution in interval notation. 2. Finding the roots by factoring

What you will be able to do

  • Factor x^2 x squared + bx + c and x^2 x squared + bx.
  • Use the ac method when the leading coefficient is not 1.
  • Solve a factor like 3x + 2 = 0 3 x plus 2 equals 0 .

What a root is

A root of a polynomial is an input that makes it equal zero. It is the same thing as a zero or a solution of "polynomial = 0." The lecture's first step is to swap the inequality symbol for an equals sign and solve. The roots are the only places where the polynomial can change between positive and negative, which is why they come first.

Why the ac method works. Multiply (2x - 1)(x + 3) open parenthesis 2 x minus 1 close parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 3 close parenthesis back out: 2x^2 + 6x - x - 3 2 x squared plus 6 x minus x minus 3 . The ac method simply searches for those two middle terms and then reverses the multiplication. You can always check a factoring by multiplying it out.

3. Splitting the number line and picking test values

What you will be able to do

  • Order roots, including negatives and fractions.
  • Count and name the intervals.
  • Choose an easy test value inside each interval.

Roots cut the number line into pieces

Picture the number line with the roots marked on it. They cut the line into pieces called intervals. One root makes two intervals. Two roots make three. In general the number of intervals is one more than the number of roots.

Put the roots in order first

Intervals are written from left to right, so the roots must be in order from smallest to largest. With negatives, the number farther from zero is the smaller one. -5 negative 5 is less than -2 negative 2 . Fractions go where their decimal values put them: 1/2 is 0.5, between 0 and 1. And -2/3 negative 2 over 3 is about -0.67 negative 0.67 , between -1 negative 1 and 0.

Naming the intervals

With roots -2 negative 2 and 5, the three intervals are:

  • everything to the left of -2 negative 2
  • everything between -2 negative 2 and 5
  • everything to the right of 5

The leftmost interval starts at negative infinity and the rightmost one ends at infinity.

Choosing a test value

The lecture tests one number from each interval. Any number strictly inside the interval works. It cannot be a root. Pick whatever is easiest to compute with.

  • To the left of the smallest root: go one or two below it. Left of -2 negative 2 , use -3 negative 3 .
  • To the right of the largest root: go one or two above it. Right of 5, use 6.
  • In between: if 0 is inside the interval, use 0, because it makes the arithmetic trivial. Otherwise use any number between the roots.

When two roots are consecutive whole numbers, such as 3 and 4, no whole number lies between them. Use the number halfway: 3.5.

Why one test value is enough. A polynomial is continuous. Its graph has no jumps. So it cannot go from positive to negative without passing through zero, and the zeros are exactly the roots. Between two neighboring roots the polynomial therefore keeps one sign the whole way. That is why a single test value is enough to speak for an entire interval.

Set up the intervals

The roots are 1/2 and -3 negative 3 . Order them, name the intervals, and choose a test value for each.

  1. -3, then 1/2 negative 3, then 1 over 2 -3 negative 3 is smaller.
  2. left of -3: test -4 left of negative 3 : test negative 4 One below the smallest root.
  3. between -3 and 1/2: test 0 between negative 3 and 1 over 2 : test 0 0 is inside this interval.
  4. right of 1/2: test 1 right of 1 over 2 : test 1 1 is greater than 1/2.

Three intervals with test values -4 negative 4 , 0, and 1.

4. Positive or negative?

What you will be able to do

  • Read an inequality as a question about sign.
  • Find a sign by computing or by multiplying the signs of factors.
  • Decide whether the inequality is true at a test value.

What the inequality is asking

Every inequality in this section compares a polynomial with 0. That is a question about sign.

  • > 0 asks where the polynomial is positive.
  • < 0 asks where the polynomial is negative.
  • ≥ 0 asks where it is positive or zero.
  • ≤ 0 asks where it is negative or zero.

So testing a value does not require an exact answer. You only need to know whether the result is positive or negative.

Method 1: substitute and compute

Put the test value into the polynomial and work it out.

x^2 - 3x - 10 at x = 0: 0 - 0 - 10 = -10, negative x squared minus 3 x minus 10 at x equals 0 : 0 minus 0 minus 10 equals negative 10, negative x^2 - 3x - 10 at x = 6: 36 - 18 - 10 = 8, positive x squared minus 3 x minus 10 at x equals 6 : 36 minus 18 minus 10 equals 8, positive x^2 - 3x - 10 at x = -3: 9 + 9 - 10 = 8, positive x squared minus 3 x minus 10 at x equals negative 3 : 9 plus 9 minus 10 equals 8, positive

In the last line, -3 negative 3 times -3 negative 3 is +9, and subtracting 3(-3) 3 open parenthesis negative 3 close parenthesis adds 9.

Method 2: use the factored form and track signs only

If the polynomial is factored, find the sign of each factor and multiply the signs. You never need the actual product.

(x - 5)(x + 2) at x = 0: (negative)(positive) = negative open parenthesis x minus 5 close parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 2 close parenthesis at x equals 0 : open parenthesis negative close parenthesis open parenthesis positive close parenthesis equals negative (x - 5)(x + 2) at x = 6: (positive)(positive) = positive open parenthesis x minus 5 close parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 2 close parenthesis at x equals 6 : open parenthesis positive close parenthesis open parenthesis positive close parenthesis equals positive (x - 5)(x + 2) at x = -3: (negative)(negative) = positive open parenthesis x minus 5 close parenthesis open parenthesis x plus 2 close parenthesis at x equals negative 3 : open parenthesis negative close parenthesis open parenthesis negative close parenthesis equals positive
Why signs are enough. The sign of a product depends only on how many negative factors it has. An even number of negatives gives a positive. An odd number gives a negative. The sizes of the numbers do not matter for the sign. This makes the factored form faster and less error-prone than squaring and subtracting, especially with decimals.

True or false

Once you know the sign, compare it with what the inequality asks. Suppose the inequality is x^2 - 3x - 10 < 0 x squared minus 3 x minus 10 is less than 0 , which asks for negative. At x = 0 x equals 0 the result is negative, so the statement is true there. At x = 6 x equals 6 the result is positive, so the statement is false there. The lecture keeps the intervals where the test comes out true.

5. Brackets, parentheses, and unions

What you will be able to do

  • Choose brackets or parentheses from the symbol.
  • Always use a parenthesis at infinity.
  • Join separate intervals with a union.

The symbol decides the endpoint

The last step writes the solution in interval notation. Whether a root is included depends on the inequality symbol.

Symbols and endpoints
SymbolRoots included?Endpoint mark
< or >Noparenthesis ( )
≤ or ≥Yesbracket [ ]

The bar under ≤ and ≥ means "or equal to." At a root the polynomial equals zero, so the bar is what lets the roots in.

Infinity always gets a parenthesis

Infinity is not a number you can reach, so it can never be included. Write (-∞ and ∞) with parentheses every time, whatever the symbol.

from -2 to 5, roots included: [-2, 5] from negative 2 to 5, roots included : [ negative 2, 5 ] from -2 to 5, roots not included: (-2, 5) from negative 2 to 5, roots not included : open parenthesis negative 2, 5 close parenthesis 5 and everything to the right: [5, ∞)

Joining two separate pieces

Often the solution is two pieces that do not touch, such as everything left of -2 negative 2 together with everything right of 5. Write each piece and join them with the union symbol ∪, typed as a capital U. Put the left piece first.

roots included: (-∞, -2] ∪ [5, ∞) roots included : open parenthesis negative infinity, negative 2 ] union [ 5, infinity close parenthesis roots not included: (-∞, -2) ∪ (5, ∞) roots not included : open parenthesis negative infinity, negative 2 close parenthesis union open parenthesis 5, infinity close parenthesis
Why the bar matters. Take x^2 - 3x - 10 ≥ 0 x squared minus 3 x minus 10 is greater than or equal to 0 . At x = 5 x equals 5 the polynomial is exactly 0, and 0 ≥ 0 0 is greater than or equal to 0 is true, so 5 belongs in the solution. With x^2 - 3x - 10 > 0 x squared minus 3 x minus 10 is greater than 0 , the statement at x = 5 x equals 5 would be 0 > 0 0 is greater than 0 , which is false, so 5 is left out. The root is right on the boundary, and the symbol decides which side of the boundary it falls on.

Write the solution

The roots are -2 negative 2 and 5. Tests show the polynomial is positive on the left, negative in the middle, and positive on the right. Write the solution of "polynomial ≥ 0."

  1. keep the left and right intervals≥ 0 asks for positive or zero.
  2. use brackets at -2 and 5 use brackets at negative 2 and 5 The symbol has the "or equal to" bar.
  3. (-∞, -2] ∪ [5, ∞) open parenthesis negative infinity, negative 2 ] union [ 5, infinity close parenthesis Join the two pieces with a union.

Answer: (-∞, -2 negative 2 ] ∪ [5, ∞)
