4. Checking Inverses with Composition

These checks match item 3 on pages 3 and 4 and Classroom Examples 2.7.1 and 2.7.2 on page 5.

Check 9 (When Two Functions Are Inverses). When are f and g inverses of each other?
Show answer

When (f ∘ g)(x) = x and (g ∘ f)(x) = x.

Where this is in your notes: item 3 (Checking if Two Functions are Inverses) on page 3 of the notes.

Check 10 (Classroom Example 2.7.1). For f(x) = 3x − 6f of x equals 3 x minus 6 and g(x) = (x + 6)/3g of x equals the quantity x plus 6, over 3, what do the compositions show?
Show answer

f(g(x)) = x and g(f(x)) = x, so they are inverses.

Where this is in your notes: item 3 and its example on pages 3 and 4, Classroom Example 2.7.1 on page 5 of the notes and the video "Classroom Example 2.7.1."

Check 11 (Classroom Example 2.7.2). For f(x) = 4x + 3f of x equals 4 x plus 3 and g(x) = (x − 4)/3g of x equals the quantity x minus 4, over 3, are f and g inverses?
Show answer

No, because f(g(x)) = (4x − 7)/3, which is not x.

Where this is in your notes: item 3 and its example on pages 3 and 4, Classroom Example 2.7.2 on page 5 of the notes and the video "Classroom Example 2.7.2."
