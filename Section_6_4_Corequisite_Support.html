2. Reciprocals: the inverse of a number

What you will be able to do

  • Find the reciprocal of a whole number or fraction.
  • Check a pair by multiplying.
  • Explain why 0 has no reciprocal.

Two numbers that multiply to 1

Two numbers are multiplicative inverses of each other when their product is 1. The everyday word is reciprocal. The reciprocal of 4 is 1/4, because 4 * 1/4 = 1 4 times 1 over 4 equals 1 .

Finding one

Write the number as a fraction and flip it. A whole number is itself over 1.

4 = 4/1 reciprocal: 1/4 4 equals 4 over 1 reciprocal : 1 over 4 2/5 reciprocal: 5/2 2 over 5 reciprocal : 5 over 2 1/7 reciprocal: 7/1 = 7 1 over 7 reciprocal : 7 over 1 equals 7

The sign stays the same. The reciprocal of -2 negative 2 is -1/2 negative 1 over 2 , because a negative times a negative is the positive number 1.

A reciprocal can be a decimal

5 and 0.2 are reciprocals, because 5 * 0.2 = 1 5 times 0.2 equals 1 . The number 0.2 is 1/5 written as a decimal. This matters later, because Desmos often shows inverses with decimals.

Zero has none

Zero times any number is 0. It can never be 1. So 0 has no reciprocal. Keep this in mind: the lecture meets matrices that have no inverse either, and it calls them singular.

Why flipping gives the inverse. Multiplying by 4 makes something four times as large. Multiplying by 1/4 cuts it into four equal parts and keeps one. Do both and you are back where you started, which is what multiplying by 1 does. That is the whole meaning of an inverse: it undoes the multiplication. Flipping the fraction works because the top of one cancels the bottom of the other.

Reciprocal of a fraction

Find the multiplicative inverse of 3/8 and check it.

  1. 8/3 8 over 3 Flip the fraction.
  2. 3/8 * 8/3 = 24/24 3 over 8 times 8 over 3 equals 24 over 24 Multiply tops and bottoms.
  3. = 1 equals 1 The product is 1, so they are inverses.

The inverse of 3/8 is 8/3.

3. What the small -1 negative 1 means

What you will be able to do

  • Evaluate a number to the power -1 negative 1 .
  • Read A^-1 A inverse as A inverse.
  • Tell an opposite from an inverse.

On a number

From Chapter 4, a negative exponent means "one over". So a number raised to the power -1 negative 1 is its reciprocal.

5^-1 = 1/5 5 to the power negative 1 equals 1 over 5 (1/2)^-1 = 2 open parenthesis 1 over 2 close parenthesis to the power negative 1 equals 2

The -1 negative 1 does not make the number negative. 5^-1 5 to the power negative 1 is 1/5, a positive number.

On a matrix

The inverse of a matrix A is written A^-1 A inverse and read "A inverse". The small -1 negative 1 is borrowed from numbers. It still means "the thing that undoes multiplying by A". It is a new matrix, the same size as A.

Three things it is not

  • It is not -A negative A . The opposite of A flips each sign and adds with A to give all zeros.
  • It is not the reciprocal of each entry. Flipping every entry separately does not produce the inverse.
  • It is not a power you compute by hand in this course. Desmos finds it.
Opposite and inverse side by side
IdeaFor the number 5For a matrix A
Opposite-5 negative 5 -A negative A
What it does5 + (-5) = 0 5 plus open parenthesis negative 5 close parenthesis equals 0 A + (-A) A plus open parenthesis negative A close parenthesis is the zero matrix
Inverse5^-1 = 1/5 5 to the power negative 1 equals 1 over 5 A^-1 A inverse
What it does5 * 1/5 = 1 5 times 1 over 5 equals 1 A times A^-1 A inverse is the identity matrix

Only square matrices can have an inverse, and not every square matrix does.

Why -1 negative 1 is the symbol for inverse. The pattern of exponents explains the notation. 5^2 = 25 5 squared equals 25 , 5^1 = 5 5 to the power 1 equals 5 , 5^0 = 1 5 to the power 0 equals 1 . Each step down divides by 5. One more step gives 5^-1 5 to the power negative 1 = 1/5. So the exponent -1 negative 1 lands exactly on the reciprocal. Mathematicians reuse the same mark for a matrix because A^-1 A inverse plays the same role: multiply by A, then by A^-1 A inverse , and you are back to where you began.

Opposite or inverse?

For the number 8, find the opposite and the inverse, and say what each one does.

  1. -8 negative 8 The opposite. 8 + (-8) = 0 8 plus open parenthesis negative 8 close parenthesis equals 0 .
  2. 8^-1 = 1/8 8 to the power negative 1 equals 1 over 8 The inverse. 8 * 1/8 = 1 8 times 1 over 8 equals 1 .

Opposites add to 0. Inverses multiply to 1.

4. Solving a one-step equation by multiplying by an inverse

What you will be able to do

  • Multiply both sides by a reciprocal.
  • Write the solution as inverse times right side.
  • See why a zero coefficient blocks the method.

The usual way

To solve 4x = 20 4 x equals 20 you would normally divide both sides by 4. There is a second way to say the same thing: multiply both sides by the reciprocal of 4.

4x = 20 4 x equals 20 (1/4)(4x) = (1/4)(20) open parenthesis 1 over 4 close parenthesis open parenthesis 4 x close parenthesis equals open parenthesis 1 over 4 close parenthesis open parenthesis 20 close parenthesis x = 5 x equals 5

On the left, 1/4 times 4 is 1, and 1x is x. On the right, a quarter of 20 is 5.

Why bother saying it that way

Because matrices cannot be divided. There is no such operation. But a matrix can be multiplied by an inverse. So the lecture solves its matrix equation the second way, and it helps to have seen that way with plain numbers first.

The pattern

If a number a times x equals b, then x equals the inverse of a times b.

a * x = b a times x equals b x = a^-1 * b x equals a to the power negative 1 times b

The inverse goes in front, on the left of b. With numbers the order would not matter. With matrices it does, so build the habit now.

A fraction in front

(2/3)x = 8 open parenthesis 2 over 3 close parenthesis x equals 8 x = (3/2)(8) x equals open parenthesis 3 over 2 close parenthesis open parenthesis 8 close parenthesis x = 12 x equals 12
Why this is the same as dividing. Multiplying both sides of an equation by the same number keeps it true. Choose that number to be the reciprocal of the coefficient and the coefficient turns into 1, which leaves x alone. Dividing by 4 and multiplying by 1/4 are the same action with two names. The second name is the one that still makes sense for matrices.

Multiply by the inverse

Solve (3/4)x = 6 open parenthesis 3 over 4 close parenthesis x equals 6 by multiplying by an inverse.

  1. The inverse of 3/4 is 4/3 The inverse of 3 over 4 is 4 over 3 Flip the fraction.
  2. x = (4/3)(6) x equals open parenthesis 4 over 3 close parenthesis open parenthesis 6 close parenthesis Inverse in front, times the right side.
  3. x = 24/3 = 8 x equals 24 over 3 equals 8 Multiply, then simplify.

x = 8 x equals 8

5. Is it the identity? Reading Desmos output

What you will be able to do

  • Recognize an identity matrix.
  • Apply the two-product test for inverses.
  • Turn common decimals into fractions.

The matrix that acts like 1

The identity matrix, I, has 1s down the diagonal from top left to bottom right and 0s everywhere else. Here is the 3 × 3 one.

I = 100010001

Two matrices are inverses of each other when their product is the identity. For matrices the test is done in both orders: AB must be I and BA must be I. If either product is anything else, they are not inverses.

Look at every entry

A matrix is the identity only if every entry is right. One stray number off the diagonal, or a diagonal entry that is not 1, and it is not the identity.

Decimals in the output

An inverse often contains fractions. Desmos first shows them as decimals such as 0.333 or -0.6667 negative 0.6667 . The Matrix tool has a button that switches the display to fractions. Use it, because MyMathLab expects exact entries. It also helps to know the common ones on sight.

Why every entry has to match. The identity earns its name by leaving things unchanged, the way 1 does. For that to happen in every position, each row must pick out exactly one entry and ignore the rest, which takes a single 1 and 0s elsewhere. Change any entry and some position of the product changes. So "close to the identity" is not the identity, just as 0.99 is not 1.

Check a product

Desmos shows a product with rows 1, 0, 0 and 0, 1, 0 and 0, 1, 1. Is it the identity?

  1. Diagonal: 1, 1, 1Good so far.
  2. Row 3, column 2 is 1It should be 0.

Not the identity. One entry is wrong.

6. Pulling a system apart into A, X, and B

What you will be able to do

  • Write the coefficient matrix A.
  • Write the constants matrix B.
  • State the sizes of A, X, and B.

Three matrices from one system

Section 6.1 packed a system into one augmented matrix. This section splits it into three.

  • A, the coefficient matrix. Only the coefficients. No constants.
  • X, the variable matrix. The variables in one column.
  • B, the constants matrix. The numbers on the right sides, in one column.
2x - y + 3z = -3 2 x minus y plus 3 z equals negative 3 x + 4z = -3 x plus 4 z equals negative 3 -x + 5y - 2z = 11 negative x plus 5 y minus 2 z equals 11
A = 2−13104−15−2X = xyzB = −3−311

The same care as before

Reading coefficients has not changed. Line the equations up first. A term with no number has coefficient 1 or -1 negative 1 . A missing variable gets 0. In the second equation there is no y, so row 2 of A is 1, 0, 4.

Sizes

With 3 equations in 3 variables, A is 3 × 3. X and B each have 3 rows and 1 column, so they are 3 × 1. A must be square, because only a square matrix can have an inverse.

What A times X equals B says

Multiply row 1 of A by the column X with the row-times-column pattern: 2(x) + (-1)(y) + 3(z) 2 open parenthesis x close parenthesis plus open parenthesis negative 1 close parenthesis open parenthesis y close parenthesis plus 3 open parenthesis z close parenthesis . That is the left side of the first equation. Setting it equal to the first entry of B gives the first equation back. So the matrix equation is the system, written in three letters.

Why split it up at all. Writing the system as A times X equals B puts it in the same shape as the one-step equation a * x = b a times x equals b . Everything known about the left sides is in A. Everything known about the right sides is in B. The unknowns sit alone in X. That is what makes the next step possible: multiply by the inverse of A, and X is left by itself.

Build A and B

Write row 2 of A and the matching entry of B for the equation 3y - z = 8 3 y minus z equals 8 in a system with x, y, and z.

  1. 0x + 3y - 1z = 8 0 x plus 3 y minus 1 z equals 8 Write the missing x and the hidden 1.
  2. Row of A: 0, 3, -1 Row of A : 0, 3, negative 1 Coefficients only.
  3. Entry of B: 8The constant goes in B, not in A.

Row of A is 0, 3, -1 negative 1 and the entry of B is 8.

7. The Desmos steps, in the right order

What you will be able to do

  • Enter A and B with the right sizes.
  • Multiply in the right order.
  • Read the column as an ordered triple.

The steps

  1. Open the Matrix tool at desmos.com.
  2. New Matrix. Set 3 rows and 3 columns and type A.
  3. New Matrix again. Set 3 rows and 1 column and type B.
  4. Click off the matrices.
  5. Click A, then the inverse button, then the times sign, then B. Press Enter.

The inverse button on the Desmos keypad is labeled with an A and a small -1 negative 1 no matter which matrix you are inverting. It is the button, not the name of your matrix.

Order matters

Type A inverse first and B second. Check the sizes with the rule from Section 6.3.

A^-1 times B: 3 × 3 times 3 × 1 A inverse times B : 3 × 3 times 3 × 1

The inside numbers are 3 and 3. They match, and the result is 3 × 1, one column of three numbers. Reversed, B times A inverse would be 3 × 1 times 3 × 3. The inside numbers are 1 and 3, so that product cannot be found.

Reading the answer

The result is a column. Set it next to X. The top number is x, the middle is y, the bottom is z.

X = xyzequals12−1

So x = 1 x equals 1 , y = 2 y equals 2 , and z = -1 z equals negative 1 . The solution is (1, 2, -1) open parenthesis 1, 2, negative 1 close parenthesis . You can check it in the first equation: 2(1) - (2) + 3(-1) = -3 2 open parenthesis 1 close parenthesis minus open parenthesis 2 close parenthesis plus 3 open parenthesis negative 1 close parenthesis equals negative 3 .

Just an inverse

When a problem only asks for the inverse, click A and then the inverse button. If the matrix has no inverse, Desmos shows a warning instead of a matrix. That is the answer: the inverse does not exist.

Why A inverse goes first. Start from A times X equals B. Multiply both sides, on the left, by A inverse. On the left side A inverse times A is the identity, and the identity times X is just X. On the right side you get A inverse times B. Because the multiplying was done on the left of both sides, A inverse ends up on the left of B. That is where the order comes from.

Read a Desmos column

Desmos shows A inverse times B as a column with entries 4, -2 negative 2 , 0 from top to bottom. State the solution.

  1. x = 4 x equals 4 Top entry.
  2. y = -2 y equals negative 2 Middle entry.
  3. z = 0 z equals 0 Bottom entry. Zero is a value like any other.

The solution is (4, -2, 0) open parenthesis 4, negative 2, 0 close parenthesis .
